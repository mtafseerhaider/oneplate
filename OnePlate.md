# OnePlate

**A practical, evidence-based fat-loss plan: 84 kg → 70 kg, built around one well-constructed plate, a daily fast you can live with, and low-impact training that respects a healed tibia/fibula fracture.**

> **Who this is for:** a male software engineer, 84 kg, sedentary desk job (9 AM–6 PM, 1-hour motorcycle commute each way), currently eating one meal a day (OMAD) between roughly 7 PM and 10 PM, with a titanium nail in the left tibia from a fracture about two years ago.
>
> **Written from the combined perspective of** an obesity-medicine physician, registered dietitian, sports physiotherapist, sports nutritionist and intermittent-fasting coach.
>
> **Not a substitute for your own doctor.** This plan is educational. Sections 15 and 16 say exactly when to get medical clearance. If you have diabetes, take any regular medication, or have kidney, liver, heart or gout history, show this plan to your physician before starting.

**Assumptions used for the numbers:** height ≈ 175 cm (adjust if yours differs: 5 cm shorter is about 30 kcal less maintenance), age 38 (born 5 February 1988). The calculations below were first drafted at age 30; at 38 the basal rate is about 40 kcal/day lower, which is inside the rounding of every figure here and changes nothing in the plan. The hand-portion rules do not change.

## 1. Executive Summary

**The verdict on your current OMAD:** it works for fat loss, but it is unnecessarily restrictive for your goals. A 21–22 hour fast gives you no extra fat loss compared with an 18–20 hour fast at the same weekly calorie intake. What it does do is make three things harder: eating enough protein to protect muscle, recovering from the training you want to add, and arriving home at 7 PM calm instead of ravenous.

**What OnePlate recommends instead: a 20:4 "two-course day."**

- **5:45 PM, before you leave the office:** a small protein starter (for example, a bowl of yogurt and two boiled eggs). Takes five minutes, costs little, and changes the whole evening.
- **7:30–8:30 PM at home:** your OnePlate dinner, built by hand: 2 palms protein, 2 fists vegetables, 1 cupped hand of carbs or 1 roti, 1 thumb of fat, plus a bowl of yogurt.
- **Kitchen closes at 9:30 PM.** Your fast runs ~20 hours, from 9:30 PM to 5:45 PM.
- **Upgrade to 18:6** (move the starter to ~3:30 PM) if afternoon productivity, hunger or training recovery suffer. **Drop back to true OMAD** only on occasional days when you simply can't fit the starter in. All three lose fat at the same rate for the same weekly intake; you are choosing the one you can repeat for seven months.

**The numbers in one glance**

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Maintenance now (sedentary) | ≈ 2,150–2,250 kcal/day |
| Maintenance with this plan's activity | ≈ 2,350–2,450 kcal/day |
| Target intake (no counting needed; the plate does it) | ≈ 1,700–1,900 kcal/day |
| Daily deficit | ≈ 500–700 kcal |
| Expected fat loss | 0.45–0.65 kg/week; ≈ 2–2.5 kg/month after month one |
| Timeline 84 → 70 kg | Most likely 24–28 weeks (6–7 months). Best case 18–20 weeks. |
| Minimum daily protein | 120 g (≈ 4 palms), aim 140 g |

**Your leg:** two years after a nailed tibia/fibula fracture, walking, stationary cycling and progressive strength training are appropriate and actively good for the bone. Running, jumping and heavy single-leg loading wait until an orthopaedic surgeon clears them. A moderate deficit protects bone; an aggressive one (more than ~1,000 kcal/day) measurably suppresses bone formation within days, which is one more reason not to push the fast harder.

**If you read nothing else, read Section 17: The Only 5 Rules I Need.**

## 2. Weight-Loss Calculations

### 2.1 Estimated maintenance calories

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Basal metabolic rate (Mifflin-St Jeor, 84 kg, 175 cm, 30 y, male) | 10×84 + 6.25×175 − 5×30 + 5 | ≈ 1,790 kcal |
| Sedentary multiplier (desk job, motorcycle commute, little walking) | × 1.2–1.25 | ≈ 2,150–2,250 kcal |
| With OnePlate activity (8,000 steps + 3–4 training sessions/week) | × 1.3–1.37 | ≈ 2,350–2,450 kcal |

A motorcycle commute burns almost nothing beyond sitting. Treat yourself as fully sedentary today.

### 2.2 Estimated calorie deficit from your lifestyle

Your fast does not create a deficit. Your single meal does. Most people on OMAD eat 1,500–2,200 kcal in that window because the body compensates for 21 hours of nothing. Three honest scenarios:

| Your OMAD meal is actually… | Deficit vs. 2,200 maintenance | Expected weekly loss |
|---|---|---|
| ≈ 2,000 kcal (big plate, two rotis, dessert, chai with milk) | ≈ 200 kcal | ≈ 0.2 kg (feels like a plateau) |
| ≈ 1,700 kcal (OnePlate as designed) | ≈ 500 kcal | ≈ 0.45 kg |
| ≈ 1,400 kcal (small plate, no carbs) | ≈ 800 kcal | ≈ 0.7 kg, but hunger and muscle risk rise |

**Diagnostic:** if you have been on OMAD for more than four weeks and are not losing roughly 0.4 kg or more per week, your meal is close to 2,000 kcal. The fix is the plate, not a longer fast.

### 2.3 Expected weekly fat-loss rate

Target deficit 500–700 kcal/day. One kilogram of body fat stores roughly 7,700 kcal, so:

- **≈ 0.45–0.65 kg per week** of real fat loss.
- Weeks 1–2 will show 1.5–3 kg on the scale. Most of it is water and glycogen, not fat. Enjoy it, but don't expect it to continue at that pace.

### 2.4 Expected monthly fat-loss rate

| Month | Expected scale change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | −3 to −4 kg | Fat loss plus water/glycogen drop |
| Months 2–5 | −2 to −2.5 kg each | Steady fat loss |
| Months 6–7 | −1.5 to −2 kg each | Maintenance falls as you get lighter (≈ 15–20 kcal less per kg lost) |

### 2.5 Realistic timeline from 84 kg to 70 kg

| Scenario | Weekly average | Time to 70 kg | Target date if you start this week |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Best case** (high adherence, 4 training sessions/week, one cheat meal only) | ≈ 0.7 kg | 18–20 weeks | mid-March to early April 2027 |
| **Most likely** (80–85 % adherence, normal life, Eid, weddings, a few bad weeks) | ≈ 0.5 kg | 24–28 weeks | late April to mid-May 2027 |
| **Worst realistic** (cheat meals creep to two or three a week) | ≈ 0.3 kg | 45+ weeks | plan needs a reset, see Section 14 |

Build in **one planned "maintenance week" every 8–10 weeks** (eat at maintenance, keep training). It costs one week of progress and buys you months of adherence.

### 2.6 Benefits and drawbacks of OMAD

**Benefits**

- Simple: one decision a day, no lunch logistics at the office.
- Natural calorie ceiling: it is hard to overeat badly in one sitting.
- Lower insulin exposure across the day; mild improvements in fasting glucose and triglycerides are typical in time-restricted eating trials.
- Fits a long workday with no kitchen.

**Drawbacks (specific to you)**

- **Protein in one sitting.** You need ~120–140 g/day. Eating all of it in one meal is possible but uncomfortable, and in practice people fall short.
- **Recovery.** You want to add cycling and strength work. Training with your last meal 20+ hours ago is fine for walking, but recovery from strength training is better with protein spread across two feedings.
- **Hunger and the 7 PM arrival.** The controlled trial most often cited for OMAD (Stote et al., 2007) found higher hunger, and small rises in LDL cholesterol and blood pressure, in the one-meal group compared with three meals at the same calories.
- **No fat-loss advantage.** The best-run trial of time-restricted eating versus plain calorie restriction (Liu et al., NEJM 2022, 12 months) found no difference in weight, fat or metabolic markers. The fast is a tool for eating less, not a fat-burning mechanism on its own.
- **Social cost.** Family dinners, iftars, weddings and office lunches are all collisions with a 1–2 hour window.

### 2.7 Risk of muscle loss

Without enough protein and resistance training, 20–30 % of the weight lost during a diet can be lean tissue. With adequate protein (≥ 1.6 g/kg) and two to three strength sessions a week, that falls to roughly 10 % or less. The fast itself is not the main risk; a low-protein plate and no lifting are.

Reassuring recent evidence: a 2023 study (Trommelen et al., Cell Reports Medicine) showed that a single 100 g protein feeding continued to drive muscle protein synthesis for 12 hours, so the old "only 30 g per meal is used" claim is overstated. Total daily protein matters most. A two-course day just makes hitting the total realistic.

### 2.8 Bone-health considerations due to fracture history

- The nail is inert and permanent unless a surgeon decides otherwise. It does not weaken with fasting. The bone around it responds to the same things all bone does: mechanical loading, protein, calcium, vitamin D and adequate energy.
- **Low energy availability suppresses bone formation fast.** In controlled studies, five days of a large deficit (energy availability around 15 kcal per kg of lean mass) measurably lowered bone-formation markers. This is why OnePlate uses a moderate deficit and refuses to push below ~1,600 kcal/day.
- Weight loss itself reduces load on the leg by roughly 3–4 times body-weight-lost at each walking step. Losing 14 kg is good for the leg.
- Walking and progressive strength training are the loading stimulus that keeps the tibia strong. Cycling protects the heart and burns calories but does not load bone much; it is not a substitute for walking and lifting.

### 2.9 Long-term sustainability

The single best predictor of maintained weight loss is whether the method still fits your life two years later. A 1–2 hour window does not survive marriage dinners, Ramadan iftar schedules (where your window will flip to 6 PM–4 AM anyway), travel or illness. A 4–6 hour evening window with one hand-built plate does. OnePlate is designed to be the plan you are still following at 70 kg, with the carb portion simply increased.

## 3. Recommended Fasting Method

### 3.1 Scoring your current OMAD against the alternatives

| Criterion | OMAD (21–22 h fast, 2–3 h window) | 20:4 (two-course evening) | 18:6 (office mini-meal + dinner) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fat loss (same weekly intake) | Equal | Equal | Equal |
| Muscle retention | Hardest (protein bolus) | Good (2 feedings) | Best (2–3 feedings) |
| Metabolic health | Good | Good | Good |
| Recovery from exercise | Weakest | Good | Best |
| Long-term adherence | Lowest | High | High |
| Office productivity | Afternoon dip common | Afternoon dip possible, pre-commute fix | Best (fed at 3:30 PM) |
| Hunger management | Worst at 6–7 PM | Good | Best |
| Simplicity / logistics | Simplest | Nearly as simple (one box from home) | Needs food at the office |
| Fits a workday with no kitchen | Yes | Yes | Yes, with a box |

### 3.2 The recommendation: 20:4 "Two-Course Day"

**Why 20:4 and not 18:6 first?** Because it is the smallest change to what you already do. You keep fasting through the whole workday, you keep the single home-cooked plate, and you add one five-minute protein starter at your desk before the commute. Small changes stick.

**The schedule**

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 9:30 PM | Last bite. Fast begins. Log it in Fasting Tracker (set a custom 20-hour goal). |
| 9:30 PM → 5:45 PM | Fast. Water, black coffee, plain tea, sparkling water only. |
| 5:45 PM (at the office) | **Course 1, protein starter** ≈ 250–350 kcal, 20–30 g protein. Pick one: bowl of yogurt + 2 boiled eggs · 1 scoop whey in water + 10 almonds · 1 palm cold grilled chicken + 1 fist salad · bowl of yogurt + 1 palm paneer. |
| 6:00–7:00 PM | Commute, fed and calm. |
| 7:15–7:50 PM | Training on training days (cycle or lift). |
| 7:30–8:30 PM | **Course 2, OnePlate dinner.** 2 palms protein · 2 fists veg · 1 cupped hand carbs or 1 roti · 1 thumb fat · bowl of yogurt. |
| by 9:30 PM | Kitchen closed. |

**Daily totals this produces without counting:** ≈ 1,700–1,900 kcal, 120–150 g protein, which is exactly the deficit calculated in Section 2.

### 3.3 When to use 18:6 instead

Switch the starter from 5:45 PM to about **3:30 PM** (same food, eaten at your desk during the afternoon slump) if any of these happen for more than a week:

- You can't concentrate after 3 PM.
- You are still ravenous at dinner and overeating the carbs.
- Strength sessions feel flat and you are sore for more than 48 hours.
- Sleep is disrupted by hunger or by eating too much too late.

Fat loss will not slow down. You are moving 300 kcal earlier, not adding any.

### 3.4 When true OMAD is fine

Use a plain 21–22 hour OMAD day when it is simply convenient (a day you forgot the starter, a travel day, the day after a cheat meal). It is a tool, not an identity. Do not stack two OMAD days with hard training.

### 3.5 Why the extra 2 hours of fasting is not worth it

- **Autophagy:** human data at 20–24 hours are thin. Most of what is marketed comes from mouse studies or multi-day human fasts. No trial has shown meaningful body-composition or health differences between 18, 20 and 22 hours at equal intake.
- **"Fat-burning mode":** fat oxidation does rise as the fast lengthens, but what matters for the week is the total deficit. You will burn exactly the same amount of fat over seven days on 20:4 as on 22:2 if your plate is the same.
- **Cost:** each extra hour of fasting buys you nothing and costs you hunger, worse protein distribution and poorer recovery.

### 3.6 Ramadan note

During Ramadan your window flips to roughly iftar (sunset) to suhoor (pre-dawn). Keep the OnePlate structure: protein-rich iftar plate, light protein suhoor (eggs, yogurt), walk after iftar, lift or cycle late evening if you can. Fat loss usually stalls during Ramadan for most people because of fried iftar foods and sweet drinks; your goal is simply to hold weight.

## 4. Pakistani OMAD Meal Plan

### 4.1 How to build any OnePlate dinner by hand

| Hand measure | Food | Rough size | Rough value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 palm** | Cooked protein (chicken, beef, fish, paneer) | 100–120 g, palm-size and thickness, no fingers | ≈ 160–220 kcal, 25–30 g protein |
| **1 fist** | Vegetables / salad | 1 cup | ≈ 25–50 kcal |
| **1 cupped hand** | Cooked rice, pulao, boiled potato, chana | ≈ ¾ cup | ≈ 130–180 kcal, 30–35 g carbs |
| **1 roti** | Medium whole-wheat roti (≈ 40 g atta) | 15 cm, no ghee | ≈ 120 kcal, 25 g carbs, 4 g protein |
| **1 thumb** | Oil, ghee, butter, nut butter, cream | ≈ 1 tablespoon | ≈ 120 kcal, 14 g fat |
| **1 bowl** | Daal (medium bowl, ≈ 250 ml) | | ≈ 200 kcal, 12 g protein, 28 g carbs |
| **1 bowl** | Plain yogurt / dahi (≈ 200 g) | | ≈ 130 kcal (full fat), 8 g protein, 250–300 mg calcium |
| **1 egg** | Boiled or fried in a shared thumb of oil | | ≈ 75 kcal, 6 g protein |

**The default plate: 2 palms protein + 2 fists veg + 1 cupped hand carbs (or 1 roti) + 1 thumb fat + 1 bowl yogurt ≈ 800–950 kcal, 60–75 g protein.** With the 5:45 PM starter, your day lands at 1,100–1,300 kcal from food you cook anyway, plus sauces, chutneys, tea and the odd extra, which is how real days reach 1,700–1,900.

**On a true OMAD day** (no starter): add **1 more palm of protein and 1 more bowl of yogurt or daal** to the plate. That adds ≈ 300–350 kcal and ≈ 35 g protein so the day's protein stays above 120 g.

**Three cooking rules that save 300 kcal a day without weighing anything**

1. **Measure oil with a tablespoon, not a pour.** Karahi for four people: 4 tablespoons total, not half a cup. One thumb lands on your plate.
2. **Roti without ghee; rice without extra oil.** One roti or one cupped hand of rice. Never both, except on the cheat meal.
3. **Vegetables and salad go on the plate first.** Half the plate, every night. Kachumber (onion, tomato, cucumber, lemon, salt, black pepper) is free food.

## 5. Evidence-Based Fasting Guide

### 5.1 Three kinds of fast, and the only one that matters for you

| Fast type | Goal | What "breaks" it |
|---|---|---|
| **Strict fast** | Zero energy intake. Religious and research definition. | Any calorie. Water, black coffee and plain tea only. |
| **Fat-loss fast** (your goal) | Keep insulin low and energy intake near zero so the deficit is intact. | Anything with meaningful calories or sugar. A practical convention: under ~50 kcal of non-sugar items over the fast won't change fat loss. |
| **Autophagy-focused fast** | Keep mTOR low and AMPK high so cells recycle components. | Protein (especially leucine) and insulin-raising foods. Fat and non-caloric items do not meaningfully stop it. Human evidence at 20–24 h is weak; the strongest data are from animals and multi-day fasts. |

**Your target is the fat-loss fast.** The autophagy column is included because it is heavily marketed, not because it will change your result.

### 5.2 Drinks

**Water** · 0 kcal · No insulin response · Reduces hunger acutely (stomach stretch) · No effect on autophagy · Does not break any fast · Helps weight loss indirectly: 500 ml before a meal modestly reduces intake in trials. Drink 2.5–3.5 L/day in Pakistani heat, more on motorcycle commute days.

**Mineral water** · 0 kcal · No insulin · Slight extra sodium, magnesium, calcium (useful during fasting) · Does not break any fast · Neutral for weight.

**Sparkling water** · 0 kcal · No insulin · Carbonation increases fullness for 20–30 minutes in small human trials · Does not break any fast · Neutral for weight. (A 2017 rat study suggested carbonation raises ghrelin; this has not been shown meaningfully in humans. Avoid flavoured versions with sugar.)

**Black coffee** · ≈ 2–5 kcal per cup · No meaningful insulin rise (caffeine can transiently lower insulin sensitivity, which is irrelevant in a fasted state) · Suppresses appetite modestly for 1–3 hours · Caffeine and polyphenols activate AMPK and autophagy in cell and mouse studies; not shown to matter in humans · Does not break a strict, fat-loss or autophagy fast · Helps weight loss modestly: ~3–11 % higher energy expenditure for a few hours. Limit to ≈ 3 cups and none after 2 PM for sleep (caffeine half-life 5–6 hours).

**Americano** · Same as black coffee. Espresso diluted with water. No milk, no syrup.

**Green tea** · ≈ 0–2 kcal · No insulin · Mild appetite suppression · EGCG plus caffeine; small thermogenic effect (~4 % energy expenditure in meta-analyses, which is ~80 kcal/day at best) · Does not break any fast · Neutral-to-slightly-helpful for weight. Avoid high-dose green-tea extract pills (liver injury reports).

**Black tea (no milk, no sugar)** · ≈ 2 kcal · No insulin · Mild appetite suppression via caffeine · Does not break any fast · Neutral for weight. Lemon and a pinch of cinnamon are fine.

**Herbal teas (mint, chamomile, ginger, fennel, qehwa without sugar)** · ≈ 0–2 kcal · No insulin · Warm volume helps hunger waves · Do not break any fast · Neutral for weight. Avoid "slimming" or "detox" teas (often senna, a laxative) and don't overdo liquorice root (raises blood pressure).

**Sugar-free electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium; sweetened with stevia or sucralose)** · ≈ 0–10 kcal · Minerals do not raise insulin. Non-nutritive sweeteners in isolation do not produce a meaningful insulin response in most human trials, though some people find sweetness increases cravings · Do not break a fat-loss or autophagy fast; purists exclude them from a strict fast because of the sweetener · Neutral for weight. A quarter-teaspoon of salt in a litre of water does the same job for a 20-hour fast at zero cost.

### 5.3 Commonly debated items

**MCT oil** · ≈ 115–130 kcal per tablespoon · Essentially no insulin response · Raises ketones which may blunt appetite slightly · Does not stimulate mTOR much, so often marketed as "autophagy-safe"; unproven in humans · **Breaks a strict fast and a fat-loss fast**: you are eating 120 kcal of fat, which is 120 kcal of your own fat you now will not burn · Harmful to weight loss if used daily. **Verdict for you: skip it.**

**Bone broth (yakhni)** · ≈ 30–50 kcal per cup, 5–10 g protein · Amino acids produce a small insulin response · Mild appetite reduction, good sodium source · **Breaks strict and autophagy fasts** (leucine stimulates mTOR) · Minor effect on fat loss (50 kcal) · Useful during multi-day fasts for electrolytes; unnecessary for a 20-hour daily fast. Fine as the first thing you eat when breaking the fast.

**Apple cider vinegar (1 tablespoon in water)** · ≈ 3 kcal · Lowers the post-meal glucose and insulin rise when taken **with** a meal (acetic acid slows gastric emptying); in a fasted state there is nothing to blunt · Slight appetite reduction, partly through mild nausea · No proven autophagy effect · Does not break any fast · Weight-loss evidence is small and low quality: a 12-week Iranian trial found about 1 kg extra loss, and a 2024 Lebanese trial reported large losses that are widely questioned. Optional. If you use it, take it with dinner, diluted, and rinse your mouth to protect enamel.

**Lemon water (squeeze of fresh lemon)** · ≈ 1–3 kcal · No insulin · Flavour improves water intake · No autophagy effect · Does not break any fast · Neutral for weight. "Detox" and "alkalising" claims have no basis. Rinse teeth.

**Chia seeds (1 tablespoon)** · ≈ 60 kcal, 5 g fibre, 2 g protein, 4.5 g fat · Minimal insulin · Strong satiety from the gel · Protein and energy intake interrupt autophagy signalling · **Breaks a strict fast; at 60 kcal it technically dents a fat-loss fast** · Neutral for weight if calories are counted, harmful if treated as "free". Put them in the yogurt at dinner instead.

**Ispaghol / psyllium husk (1 teaspoon, ≈ 5 g, in water)** · ≈ 15–20 kcal on the label, but almost all is non-digestible fibre; net absorbed energy is close to zero · No insulin · Increases fullness; reduces LDL cholesterol; prevents constipation, which is common on OMAD · No meaningful autophagy effect · Technically breaks a strict fast; **does not meaningfully break a fat-loss fast** · Helpful for adherence and gut regularity. Take with a full glass of water, not within two hours of medicines. Best timing: with dinner or right before it.

**Cinnamon (a pinch, ≈ 1 g, in tea or coffee)** · ≈ 2–3 kcal · Small improvements in fasting glucose in meta-analyses, mostly in people with diabetes; no insulin spike · No appetite effect of note · No autophagy effect · Does not break any fast · Neutral for weight. Prefer Ceylon (Sri Lankan) cinnamon; cassia at high daily doses carries coumarin.

### 5.4 Likely to break a fast

**Honey (1 tablespoon)** · ≈ 64 kcal, ~17 g sugar · Sharp insulin response · Increases appetite within an hour or two · Stops autophagy signalling · **Breaks every type of fast** · Harmful to the deficit. "Natural" does not change the sugar.

**Honey in warm water** · Same as honey with extra water. Popular morning "fat-burner"; it is a sugar drink. Red.

**Milk tea (doodh patti / karak chai with sugar)** · ≈ 80–150 kcal per cup (milk lactose, protein, fat, plus 1–2 teaspoons sugar) · Clear insulin response · Two cups a day is 200–300 kcal, enough to erase half your deficit · **Breaks every fast**. Move chai to the eating window, or drink it black with lemon during the fast. A **splash** (1 teaspoon) of milk is ≈ 10 kcal: technically breaks a strict fast, irrelevant for fat loss.

**Coffee with milk** · A splash: ≈ 10 kcal, yellow. A latte or cappuccino: 120–200 kcal, red.

**Coffee creamer (non-dairy)** · ≈ 20–35 kcal per tablespoon, usually sugar or corn syrup plus hydrogenated oil · Insulin response · **Breaks every fast** and adds the worst kind of calories. Red.

**Fruit juice (250 ml, even fresh)** · ≈ 110–120 kcal, 25–28 g sugar, little fibre · Rapid insulin spike · Increases hunger soon after · **Breaks every fast** · Harmful. Eat the fruit at dinner instead.

**Coconut water (250 ml)** · ≈ 45–60 kcal, 10–15 g sugar, good potassium · Moderate insulin response · **Breaks every fast** · Minor harm. Reasonable as a post-exercise fast-breaker, not during the fast.

**Sugar in tea or coffee (1 teaspoon)** · ≈ 16 kcal, 4 g sugar · Insulin response small per teaspoon but it trains the sweet habit; three sweet teas a day is ≈ 50 kcal of pure sugar · **Breaks every fast**. Red.

### Practical summary: 84 kg to 70 kg using a 21–22 hour daily OMAD fasting schedule

1. **The fast is not the engine.** Your weekly deficit is. A 21–22 hour fast and a 20 hour fast lose fat at the same rate if the plate is the same. Shorten the fast by one small protein starter at 5:45 PM and your muscle, recovery and evening hunger all improve at zero cost to fat loss.
2. **During the fast, drink only green items.** Water, black coffee, plain tea. Keep chai with milk and sugar for the window, or learn to like it black with lemon.
3. **Break the fast with protein, not carbs.** Yogurt and eggs at 5:45 PM, or the first thing on your plate at 7:30 PM is the meat.
4. **One plate, built by hand**: 2 palms protein, 2 fists veg, 1 cupped hand carbs or 1 roti, 1 thumb fat, 1 bowl yogurt. On a true OMAD day, add 1 palm and 1 bowl.
5. **Hit 120 g protein daily, 4 palms minimum.** This is what protects your muscle and your tibia while the deficit runs.
6. **Close the kitchen at 9:30 PM.** Late eating is where OMAD quietly turns into 2,200 kcal.
7. **Expect 0.5 kg a week, 6–7 months total.** Take a maintenance week every 8–10 weeks. Reaching 70 kg in May 2027 and staying there beats reaching it in March and bouncing back.

## 8. Weekly Exercise Plan

### 8.1 Principles for a nailed tibia, two years on

- A healed, nailed tibia/fibula at two years is normally fully load-bearing. Walking, cycling and progressive strength work are **good** for it: bone adapts to load.
- **Low impact first.** No running, jumping, skipping or plyometrics until an orthopaedic surgeon has reviewed you (ideally with a recent X-ray). Those may well be fine later, but they are not needed to reach 70 kg.
- **Symmetry matters.** The left calf and quad are probably smaller and weaker. Start bilateral (both legs), then add gentle single-leg work on both sides, never only the right.
- **Pain rules.** Mild muscle soreness for 24–48 hours is normal. Pain at the front of the knee (nail entry site), at the screw sites near the ankle or knee, swelling that lasts past the next morning, a limp, or pain that worsens over a week are all stop signs: see Section 15.
- **Motorcycle commute is not exercise.** Count only intentional movement.

### 8.2 Walking plan

| Phase | Target | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Beginner (weeks 1–2)** | 6,000 steps/day | 15-min morning walk · two 5-min office walks · 10-min post-dinner walk |
| **Builder (weeks 3–6)** | 7,500 steps/day | Morning walk 20 min · lunch-hour walk 15 min (while others eat) · 10–15-min post-dinner walk |
| **Ideal (week 7 onward)** | 8,000–10,000 steps/day | Add a 45–60-min Sunday walk. 7,000–8,000 steps/day is where most of the health benefit in cohort studies sits; more is bonus, not requirement. |

**Progression:** add ~500 steps/day each week until you reach the ideal band. If the left leg aches, hold the current level for a week rather than regress.

**Post-meal walking (every night, non-negotiable):** 10–15 minutes starting within 30 minutes of finishing dinner. It lowers the post-meal glucose rise, aids digestion of a large single meal, and adds ~1,000 steps. This is the single highest-value habit in the plan after the plate itself.

**Shoes:** cushioned trainers, replaced every ~800 km. Avoid long walks in sandals or flat dress shoes with the nailed leg.

### 8.3 Stationary cycling plan

Cycling is the ideal cardio for your leg: zero impact, controlled range of motion, and it strengthens the quads that protect the knee. Set the saddle so your knee is slightly bent at the bottom of the stroke; a too-low saddle loads the front of the knee where the nail enters.

Intensity by **RPE (rate of perceived exertion, 1–10)**: RPE 3–4 you can talk in full sentences; RPE 5–6 short sentences; RPE 7–8 a few words only.

| Phase | Weeks | Sessions/week | Session | Weekly target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Beginner** | 1–3 | 3 | 15–20 min steady at RPE 3–4. 3-min easy warm-up, 2-min cool-down. | 45–60 min |
| **Intermediate** | 4–7 | 3–4 | 25–35 min at RPE 4–5. From week 5 include 3 × 3 min at RPE 6 with 2 min easy between. | 90–130 min |
| **Interval phase** | 8–12 | 4 (2 steady + 1–2 interval) | **Steady:** 35–45 min RPE 4–5. **Intervals:** 5 min warm-up · 8 × (1 min RPE 7–8 / 2 min RPE 3) · 5 min cool-down ≈ 34 min. | 150–180 min |
| **Maintenance** | 13+ | 3–4 | Mix as above; add 5 min to steady rides every two weeks up to 60 min. | 150–200 min |

**Progression rules:** increase total weekly minutes by no more than ~10–15 %. Never add intervals and extra duration in the same week. If the left shin or knee is sore the next day, repeat the previous week.

### 8.4 Strength training (beginner-friendly, 2–3 sessions/week)

Strength training is what keeps the weight you lose coming from fat rather than muscle, and it is the only part of this plan that loads the tibia in a bone-building way. Minimal equipment: a pair of adjustable dumbbells (or filled water bottles to start), a mat, a sturdy chair, and a resistance band.

**Session structure:** 5 min warm-up (easy cycle or march in place + leg swings + arm circles) → 6 exercises → 5 min walk.

**Full-body session (do the same session 2–3 times a week; alternate A and B if you prefer variety)**

| # | Exercise | Sets × Reps | Notes for the leg |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Box squat or goblet squat (to a chair) | 3 × 8–12 | Both feet even. Sit back onto the chair edge, stand without rocking. |
| 2 | Dumbbell Romanian deadlift (hip hinge) | 3 × 8–12 | Soft knees, hinge at the hips. Strengthens hamstrings/glutes, unloads the knee. |
| 3 | Push-ups (incline on a table, then floor) | 3 × 8–12 | Upper body; no leg issue. |
| 4 | One-arm dumbbell row or band row | 3 × 10–12 each side | Upper body. |
| 5 | Standing calf raises, both legs, holding a wall | 3 × 12–15 | Start bilateral. After 4 weeks, add single-leg sets on **both** legs, 2 × 8–10. Calf strength protects the tibia. |
| 6 | Glute bridge → split squat (week 5+) | 3 × 10–12 | Bridges from week 1. Add bodyweight split squats (rear-foot on floor, not a bench) from week 5, both legs, if pain-free. |
| 7 | Plank | 3 × 20–45 s | Core. |

**Alternate B session** (for variety): dumbbell overhead press 3 × 8–12 · step-ups onto a low step (both legs, start 10–15 cm) 3 × 8 each · band pull-aparts 3 × 15 · side plank 3 × 20–30 s each · dead bug 3 × 10 each.

**Weekly schedule:** Monday, Wednesday, Saturday (or Friday). Minimum 48 hours between sessions. If you only manage two, that still works.

**Progression method (double progression):** start at the low end of the rep range with a weight you could do 3–4 more reps of. Each session, add one rep per set until you hit the top of the range (12). Then increase the weight by the smallest step available (1–2.5 kg) and drop back to 8 reps. For bodyweight moves, progress the version: incline push-up → floor push-up → feet elevated; box squat → goblet squat → heavier goblet.

**Fasted or fed?** With the 5:45 PM starter, you lift 90 minutes after 25–30 g of protein and eat your main meal 20–30 minutes after finishing. That is close to ideal. On a true OMAD day, lifting fasted is safe for a beginner at this intensity; just eat within an hour of finishing.

### 8.5 When to get medical clearance before exercise

- Before starting the interval phase if you have ever had chest pain, fainting, uncontrolled blood pressure, or diabetes.
- Before any running, jumping, football, cricket sprinting or heavy single-leg loading: orthopaedic review of the nailed leg.
- If you have **any** current pain, clicking or swelling in the left knee, shin or ankle, before starting the strength plan.
- If you are on any regular medication that affects heart rate, blood pressure or blood sugar.

## 10. Cheat Meal Strategy

**One cheat meal, not a cheat day.** A planned 1,500–2,000 kcal meal once a week costs you roughly 0.1–0.15 kg of progress and buys you psychological durability for the other 20 meals. A cheat *day* costs the whole week.

**Ideal day: Saturday.** You've trained that afternoon, you're not at work the next day, Sunday is the recovery day and a light plate, and your Monday weigh-in (Section 14) sits 36+ hours later when the water weight has settled.

**Ideal time: 7:00 PM, as your main meal, finished by 8:30.** Not a late-night second dinner. Eating the cheat meal *in place of* the OnePlate dinner, not in addition to it, is what keeps the damage small. Skip the 5:45 PM starter that day unless you are going to a restaurant late.

### Portion rules by food

| Food | Hand-size rule | Add | Skip |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Biryani** | 2 heaped cupped hands of rice max, plus all the chicken/meat you want (2–3 palms) | 2 fists salad + bowl raita eaten **first** | Second helping, cold drink, shami on the side |
| **Pizza** | 3 slices of a large thin-crust (or 2 of thick), ≈ 1 palm each | Side salad, chicken toppings | Garlic bread, stuffed crust, dips, soft drink |
| **Burger** | 1 single-patty burger, hands around it | Grilled chicken instead of fried if offered | Second patty, cheese fries; if fries, 1 fist shared |
| **BBQ** | Unlimited tikka/boti/kebab (protein first) | 2 fists salad, raita | Limit naan to 1; skip paratha and fried items |
| **Nihari** | 1 bowl (one serving), 1 naan or ½ large naan | Lots of ginger, lemon, green chilli; some oil poured off | Extra oil (tari) topping, bone marrow (nalli) every week, second naan |

### Six strategies to enjoy it without undoing the week

1. **Protein first, carbs last.** Eat the meat, kebabs or chicken before the rice, naan or fries. You will eat 20–30 % less of the carbs without trying.
2. **Salad or raita before the main.** Two fists of volume take the edge off.
3. **One plate, no refill.** Serve once, including dessert if you want it. Finish, then stop. Don't eat from the serving dish.
4. **Water or diet drinks only.** A 500 ml cold drink is 200 kcal and makes you hungrier.
5. **Eat slowly, 20 minutes minimum.** Fullness signals lag by about 20 minutes.
6. **Walk 15 minutes afterwards and return to the normal plan the next morning.** No "punishment fast", no skipping Sunday dinner. Expect the scale to be up 1–2 kg Sunday from salt and glycogen; it is gone by Tuesday.

**If the week went badly (two or more cheat meals):** don't add a longer fast. Simply use the low-carb Boti Salad Bowl plate (Meal 13) for the next two dinners and walk an extra 15 minutes each day.

### 11.2 Ordering strategies

**Restaurants**
- Decide before you arrive: "tikka or kebab, salad, one roti." Don't open the menu looking for inspiration.
- Order protein first and let it arrive before the rice or naan.
- Ask for roti instead of naan (120 vs 300+ kcal), and for "kam oil" (less oil) in karahi or sabzi.
- Water, soda water with lemon, or diet drink. Unsweetened chai after.
- Share rice dishes. One biryani between two people, both take extra chicken.

**Office events**
- Eat the 5:45 PM starter earlier (or a yogurt) so you don't arrive empty.
- Scan the whole table before taking a plate. Build the OnePlate: protein first, salad, then one cupped hand of the rice dish.
- Stand away from the food table; hold a water or diet drink.
- Skip the dessert tray or take one piece, not three. Sweets at offices are social, not hungry.

**Family gatherings**
- Serve yourself; don't let the plate be filled for you. Say "pehle thora, phir aur lun ga" (a little first, I'll take more later) and then don't.
- Lead with the meat dishes and salad; one roti or one cupped hand of rice.
- Dessert: one small bowl of kheer or one piece of mithai, eaten slowly, or share.
- If it falls on Saturday, this is your cheat meal. If it falls mid-week, make it an OnePlate dinner with the gathering's food and keep Saturday as planned.

**Weddings**
- Treat a wedding as your cheat meal for that week, whatever day it falls on, and move Saturday to a normal plate.
- Walk the hall; the buffet rewards standing in one place.
- Order of attack: salad and raita → tikka/kebab/karahi meat (2–3 palms) → 1–2 cupped hands of biryani/pulao → 1 small dessert → unsweetened chai.
- Skip cold drinks entirely; a wedding's three cold drinks are 600 kcal.
- Eat before 9:30 PM if you can. If the dinner is served at 11 PM (common), eat a protein starter at 6 PM and keep the wedding plate to protein and salad plus one cupped hand of rice.

| **8:45–9:00 PM** | **Post-dinner walk 10–15 min.** | Lowers post-meal glucose, aids digestion of the main meal, adds 1,000+ steps. |
| **9:00–9:30 PM** | Optional: bowl of yogurt, 1 fruit, or unsweetened chai. **9:30 PM kitchen closed. Log "fast started."** | Hard stop prevents the slow 9:30–11 PM grazing that turns OMAD into 2,200 kcal. |
| **10:00 PM** | Screens dim, phone out of the bedroom. Light stretching 5 min, especially calves and hip flexors. | Sleep quality drives hunger hormones (leptin/ghrelin) tomorrow. |
| **10:45 PM** | Lights out. | 8 hours. Short sleep (< 6.5 h) increases hunger and reduces fat loss in controlled trials at equal calories. |

**Water total:** ≈ 3 L across the day plus whatever you need for heat. In Karachi/Lahore summers on a motorcycle, add 500 ml and a second pinch of salt.

**Caffeine total:** 2–3 cups, all before 2 PM.

## 14. Progress Tracking System

### 14.1 Weekly weigh-in protocol

- **When:** every **Monday** morning (36+ hours after the Saturday cheat meal, so salt and glycogen have settled).
- **How:** after using the toilet, before water, coffee or clothes, same digital scale on the same hard floor.
- **Record** it in Fasting Tracker's weight log or the template below.
- **Read the trend, not the number.** Compare this Monday with the Monday 4 weeks ago. Week-to-week noise of ±1 kg is water, salt, bowel content and glycogen.
- Optional: weigh daily under the same conditions and average the 7 days. Only do this if numbers don't stress you.

### 14.2 Waist measurement protocol

- **When:** every **second Monday**, same session as the weigh-in.
- **Where:** at the navel, tape flat and snug but not compressing, standing relaxed, at the end of a normal exhale.
- **Record** to the nearest 0.5 cm. Take it twice; use the lower.
- **Why it matters more than the scale** in months 2–6: strength training and creatine add or hold lean mass while fat leaves; the waist tape shows that. Expect ≈ 1 cm lost per 1–1.2 kg of fat lost.
- **Target:** at 70 kg a waist under 90 cm (ideally ≈ 85 cm) is the health marker, more than the weight itself.

### 14.3 Realistic expectations

| Checkpoint | Weight (from 84 kg) | Waist | What else you should notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4 weeks** | 80–81.5 kg (−2.5 to −4) | −2 to −4 cm | Hunger waves predictable and short; sleeping better; strength sessions no longer sore for 2 days; 6,000–7,500 steps feels normal |
| **8 weeks** | 78–79.5 kg (−4.5 to −6.5) | −4 to −7 cm | Trousers loose; cycling 30+ min easy; push-ups progressed; starting to add weight on squats and hinges; people comment |
| **12 weeks** | 75.5–77.5 kg (−6.5 to −9) | −6 to −10 cm | Intervals done comfortably; left calf visibly stronger and more symmetric; the 5:45 starter + OnePlate feels like the normal way to eat, not a diet |
| **24–28 weeks** | 70 kg | ≈ 85–88 cm | Shift to maintenance (Section 16) |

### 14.4 Plateau troubleshooting guide

A plateau is **three consecutive Monday weigh-ins with no drop AND no waist change.** Two weeks of a flat scale is noise, not a plateau. Work down this list in order; stop at the first one that applies.

1. **Honest audit of the 7 days.** Count: cheat meals (should be 1), days the kitchen stayed open past 9:30 PM, chai with milk and sugar during the fast, cold drinks, "a few" biscuits, naan instead of roti, a second cupped hand of rice. Most plateaus end here.
2. **Thumbs of oil.** Family cooking usually drifts back to a pour. Re-measure with a tablespoon for a week.
3. **Nuts, dates, dried fruit, peanuts, fruit.** Healthy, calorie-dense and invisible. Limit nuts to one small handful a day, fruit to one piece.
4. **Weekend creep.** Saturday cheat meal plus Sunday brunch plus Sunday family dinner is three cheat meals. Sunday must be the light plate.
5. **Steps.** Check the phone. If you are under 7,000, add a 15-min walk; if already at 10,000, add 1,000 more.
6. **Drop the carb portion on rest days.** On the 3–4 days you don't train, use the Boti Salad Bowl (Meal 13) or skip the roti. Keep carbs on training days.
7. **Sleep and stress.** Under 7 hours increases cortisol and hunger and also makes you hold water. Fix sleep before cutting more food.
8. **Expected slowdown.** Below ~76 kg your maintenance is ≈ 150 kcal lower than when you started. The same plate now gives a smaller deficit. Either add 2,000 steps/day or move to the smaller rest-day plate permanently.
9. **Take a maintenance week** (eat a full OnePlate plus a second roti or cupped hand of rice, keep training). Then resume. Weight loss usually restarts within 10 days of resuming.
10. **If none of this works over 6 weeks**, see a physician: thyroid (TSH), fasting glucose/HbA1c, vitamin D, and a medication review (some antihistamines, antidepressants and steroids cause weight retention).

## 15. Risks and Safety

### 15.1 Could aggressive fasting or excessive restriction hurt you?

Yes, in four specific ways. OnePlate is built to avoid each one.

| Risk | Mechanism | How it shows up | How OnePlate prevents it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Muscle loss** | Deficit + low protein + no resistance training = body breaks down muscle for amino acids and glucose | Falling strength numbers, smaller arms/legs while waist stays, fatigue, weight loss that looks "soft" | ≥ 120 g protein/day across two feedings, 2–3 strength sessions/week, deficit capped at ~700 kcal, creatine |
| **Poor exercise recovery** | Low glycogen and low protein availability around training; a 22-h fast puts training far from the last meal | Soreness > 48 h, performance dropping week to week, irritability, poor sleep after training | Protein starter 90 min before training, dinner within 30 min after, carbs kept on training days, a real recovery day |
| **Bone health** | Low energy availability lowers bone-formation markers within days; low calcium and vitamin D compound it; very fast weight loss (> 1 kg/week) is associated with bone mineral density loss | Nothing you can feel until a stress fracture; the tibia with the nail is the leg to protect | Moderate deficit, 1,000 mg calcium/day from yogurt, paneer, daal and greens, vitamin D 1,000–2,000 IU, walking and strength loading |
| **Sustainability** | The more restrictive the method, the sooner it breaks, and the rebound is rarely to the starting weight but above it | Binge-restrict cycles, "starting again Monday" every week, giving up by month 3 | A 4-hour window instead of 1–2, one planned cheat meal, no banned foods, a plate you can eat with your family |

Other risks worth knowing:

- **Gallstones.** Rapid weight loss (> 1.5 kg/week) and very long gaps between meals raise gallstone risk. Keep the rate moderate and include some fat (the thumb) in your meal so the gallbladder empties daily.
- **Constipation.** Common on one meal a day. Vegetables, daal, ispaghol, water and walking fix it.
- **Orthostatic dizziness.** Standing up too fast while fasted. Salt in your water and standing slowly fix it; persistent dizziness needs a doctor.
- **Gout.** Rapid weight loss and fasting raise uric acid transiently. If you have a history, mention this plan to your doctor.
- **Low blood sugar symptoms.** Shaky, sweaty, confused: in a healthy non-diabetic this is rare, but if it happens, eat (a glass of milk or fruit), and discuss with a physician before continuing to fast.

### 15.2 Nutrition targets specific to you

| Target | Amount | Where it comes from without counting |
|---|---|---|
| **Protein (minimum)** | 120 g/day (≈ 1.7 g/kg of target weight); aim 140 g on training days | 4–5 palms a day total: 1 palm-equivalent in the starter (2 eggs + yogurt, or whey) + 2–3 palms at dinner + bowl of daal or yogurt |
| **Calcium** | 1,000 mg/day | Bowl of yogurt (≈ 300 mg) at dinner + bowl of yogurt in the starter (≈ 300 mg) + paneer once or twice a week + daal, spinach, sesame (til), small bony fish. Two yogurt bowls a day gets you most of the way. |
| **Vitamin D** | 1,000–2,000 IU/day; test if possible | Supplement. Sun through motorcycle gear doesn't count. Egg yolks and oily fish help slightly. |
| **Sodium** | 1.5–2 tsp salt/day total; more if sweating heavily | ¼ tsp salt in morning water, normal salt in cooking |
| **Potassium / magnesium** | Food-based | Yogurt, daal, spinach, tomatoes, bananas, nuts |
| **Fibre** | 25–35 g/day | 2 fists of veg, daal, whole-wheat roti, ispaghol if needed |
| **Water** | 2.5–3.5 L/day; +500 ml on hot commute days | Routine in Section 12 |

### 15.3 Muscle-preservation strategy in one paragraph

Lift two to three times a week and try to get stronger each session (that signal tells the body to keep muscle). Eat 4–5 palms of protein a day, split across the 5:45 PM starter and dinner, with the first thing on your plate always the protein. Take creatine. Keep the deficit moderate. Sleep 7.5–8 hours. Don't stack a true OMAD day with a hard training day.

### 15.4 Recovery strategies

- **Sleep** is 80 % of recovery. 10:45 PM lights out is a training decision.
- **Protein around training**: starter before, dinner after.
- **One full recovery day** (Sunday) and one easy day (Friday) every week.
- **Deload every 6–8 weeks**: one week at half the strength volume and easy cycling only. It often coincides well with a maintenance week.
- **Calf and hip-flexor stretching** nightly; the nailed leg tends to lose ankle mobility.
- **Heat and mobility** for the left ankle and knee: 5 minutes of ankle circles and calf raises before training.

### 15.5 Warning signs and what to change

| Warning sign | Likely cause | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| **Excessive fatigue** lasting more than a week | Deficit too large, protein too low, sleep short | Add a second cupped hand of carbs at dinner for a week; check sleep; add the 3:30 PM starter (18:6) |
| **Dizziness / light-headedness** on standing | Low sodium and fluid during the fast | ¼ tsp salt in 1 L water morning and afternoon; stand slowly. If it persists: see a doctor (blood pressure, blood sugar). |
| **Reduced exercise performance** for 2 weeks | Under-fuelled, under-recovered | Move to 18:6; eat the carb portion on all training days; take a deload week |
| **Poor recovery** (soreness > 48 h, aches that linger) | Protein or sleep short, too much new volume | Confirm 4–5 palms protein; repeat last week's training instead of progressing; add magnesium at night |
| **Sleep disruption** (waking hungry, or too full) | Dinner too late or too large, caffeine after 2 PM, hunger | Move dinner earlier, shift the starter to 3:30 PM, cut caffeine after 2 PM; a bowl of yogurt at 9 PM is fine |
| **Significant muscle loss** (strength dropping, arms thinner, waist not changing) | Protein too low, no lifting, deficit too aggressive | Increase to 5 palms protein, add a maintenance week, confirm 2–3 strength sessions |
| **Persistent leg pain** at the shin, knee or ankle on the left side | Overload of the healed tibia, saddle too low, too much step progression | Stop impact and heavy leg loading, keep cycling easy if pain-free, see an orthopaedic surgeon. Pain over the nail or screws that persists more than 2 weeks, night pain, or swelling needs an X-ray. |
| **Weakness** (general, or in the left leg specifically) | Under-eating; or, for the leg, nerve or hardware issue | General: increase food for a week and re-check. Left-leg specific weakness, numbness or tingling: medical review promptly. |
| Hair thinning, feeling cold, loss of libido, irritability | Classic signs of low energy availability over months | Two-week maintenance break, increase fat and carbs on the plate, check thyroid and vitamin D with a physician |

**Rule for all of the above:** the fix is almost never a longer fast or a smaller plate. It is more protein, more sleep, a maintenance week, or a doctor.

### 15.6 When medical clearance is advisable

- Before beginning if you have diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, kidney or liver disease, gout, an eating-disorder history, or take any daily medication.
- Baseline blood tests worth requesting (cheap in Pakistan): CBC, fasting glucose and HbA1c, lipid profile, 25-OH vitamin D, vitamin B12, TSH, liver and kidney function, uric acid. Repeat vitamin D and lipids at 12 weeks.
- Orthopaedic review of the left tibia before any running, jumping, football or cricket, and immediately for the leg symptoms above.
- Any fainting, chest pain, or shortness of breath out of proportion to effort during training: stop and see a doctor before resuming.

## 16. 12-Week Action Plan

**Week 0 (this week): set up**
- Buy: a tape measure, a tablespoon for oil, a 1 L water bottle, a small lunch box or shaker, 6 eggs and yogurt cups for the office fridge, vitamin D3.
- Baseline weigh-in and waist measurement Monday morning. Fill row 0 of the tracker.
- Set Fasting Tracker to a custom 20-hour fast. Set two reminders: "Starter" at 5:40 PM, "Kitchen closed" at 9:25 PM.
- Tell the family cook the three cooking rules (Section 4.1): measured oil, roti without ghee, salad first.
- Book blood tests if feasible.

**Weeks 1–2: Foundation**
- Switch to the 20:4 Two-Course Day. Starter at 5:45 PM every workday.
- OnePlate dinner by hand every night. Kitchen closes 9:30 PM.
- Fast-window drinks: green items only. Chai with milk only in the window.
- Walking: 6,000 steps/day including the 10-min post-dinner walk.
- Cycling: 3 × 15–20 min easy. No strength yet; learn the movements with bodyweight on Mon/Wed (2 sets of each, no progression).
- Saturday cheat meal from week 1, with the portion rules.

**Weeks 3–4: Add strength**
- Strength: 2 sessions (Mon, Thu or Sat), 3 × 8–12. Start creatine 3–5 g daily.
- Cycling: 3 × 20 min.
- Walking: 7,000 steps.
- Week 4 checkpoint: expect ≈ 80–81.5 kg, waist −2 to −4 cm. If less, run the audit in Section 14.4 steps 1–4.

**Weeks 5–8: Build**
- Strength: 3 sessions. Add split squats and single-leg calf raises (both legs) if pain-free.
- Cycling: intermediate phase, 3–4 × 25–35 min, with 3 × 3-min moderate efforts from week 5.
- Walking: 7,500–8,000 steps; add the Sunday long walk.
- If afternoon focus or recovery is suffering, move the starter to 3:30 PM (18:6).
- Week 8 checkpoint: expect ≈ 78–79.5 kg, waist −4 to −7 cm.

**Week 9 or 10: Maintenance week** (pick one)
- Add a second roti or cupped hand of rice to dinner; keep everything else. Strength volume halved, cycling easy. Weight will hold or rise 0.5–1 kg (water). This is deliberate.

**Weeks 9–12 (excluding the maintenance week): Intensify**
- Cycling: interval phase, 2 steady + 1–2 interval sessions, 150–180 min/week.
- Strength: 3 sessions, progressing weight.
- Walking: 8,000–10,000 steps.
- Week 12 checkpoint: expect ≈ 75.5–77.5 kg, waist −6 to −10 cm. Repeat blood tests.

**Weeks 13–28: Repeat the weeks 5–12 pattern** with a maintenance week every 8–10 weeks, until 70 kg. Below 76 kg, make the rest-day plate the low-carb Boti Salad Bowl (Meal 13) to offset the lower maintenance.

**At 70 kg: Maintenance**
- Keep the 20:4 or shift to a relaxed 16:8 (starter at lunch) if you prefer.
- Add 1 cupped hand of carbs or 1 roti to dinner; keep protein, veg, training, cheat meal and the 9:30 PM close.
- Weigh monthly. If weight rises 2 kg above 70, return to the full plan for 3–4 weeks. This "2 kg rule" is how maintenance actually works.

## 17. The Only 5 Rules I Need

1. **One plate, built by hand, once a day.** 2 palms protein · 2 fists vegetables · 1 cupped hand of rice **or** 1 roti · 1 thumb of fat · 1 bowl of yogurt. Protein goes on first and gets eaten first.

2. **During the fast, drink only what has nothing in it.** Water, black coffee, plain tea. Chai with milk and sugar lives inside the eating window or not at all.

3. **Four palms of protein every day, no exceptions.** One at 5:45 PM before you leave the office (yogurt + eggs, or whey), three at dinner (meat, plus daal or yogurt). This protects your muscle and your leg while the fat leaves.

4. **Move every day, lift three times a week.** 8,000 steps including a 10-minute walk after dinner; stationary cycling Tuesday and Thursday; strength Monday, Wednesday, Saturday. Low impact, both legs, get a little stronger each session.

5. **One cheat meal, Saturday 7 PM, and the kitchen closes at 9:30 every night.** Not a cheat day. Not a second dinner. Then Sunday is a light plate and a long walk, and Monday morning you weigh in and move on.

Follow these five for 26 weeks and you will reach 70 kg without ever counting a calorie.
