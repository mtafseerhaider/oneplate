# Lecture 3 — Source Verification Audit

*A Guide To Islamic Child Raising (Tarbiyah), Part 3 — Shaykh Usama Abdulghani (Light Of Guidance).*
Source video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gmd4W08ZttM (1:22:16). Transcript: YouTube auto-captions,
heavily garbled on Arabic and proper names; every Arabic reconstruction below is flagged as such.

Items already settled in `_verified-index.md` are **not re-researched**; they are cross-referenced with
their index verdict and only the *new* thing this lecture does with them is assessed.

---

## Headline findings

- **The single strongest citation in the lecture is one he throws away in a sentence.** "If you love a
  man, tell him" is **al-Kāfī**, *Kitāb al-ʿIshra*, *bāb iʿlām al-akh bi'l-ḥubb*, h.2 — graded ***ṣaḥīḥ*
  by BOTH al-Majlisī (*Mirʾāt al-ʿUqūl* 12/538) and Behbūdī**. Every narrator is a first-rank
  Imāmī. This is rarer than the material he leans on hardest, and a parent can act on it without caveat.

- **"Kissing your child raises you one degree in paradise, and between each degree is 500 years" is NOT
  LOCATED.** The genuine text (al-Kāfī 6:49, *bāb al-rifq bi'l-awlād*, ch.35, **h.1**) says a *good deed*
  (*ḥasana*) is written — no degree, no 500 years — and al-Majlisī grades that hadith ***ḍaʿīf***, with
  Behbūdī excluding it. The "500 years" figure belongs to a different family of narrations entirely
  (distance between the heavens; the poor entering paradise before the rich). **Do not attribute.**

- **He is unusually precise on one hadith where nearly everyone is sloppy.** "You will never be able to
  encompass people with your wealth, so meet them with an open face and good cheer" — he says the Prophet
  was *"addressing his family members."* Correct: *Wasāʾil al-Shīʿa* 12:161 has it addressed to
  **Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib**. Credit where due.

- **Two attributions to named Imams do not hold in the form given.** (a) "Imām ʿAlī: nothing is more
  effective at attracting the heart than the tongue" — NOT LOCATED in Nahj al-Balāgha, *Ghurar*, or
  *ʿUyūn al-Ḥikam*. (b) "Imām al-Ṣādiq: whoever smiles in his brother's face gets a good deed" — the text
  is real (al-Kāfī 2, ch.82, h.2) but it is from **Imām al-Bāqir**, and al-Majlisī grades it ***ḍaʿīf***
  (chain runs through ʿAmr b. Shimr ← Jābir al-Juʿfī plus an unnamed Kūfan).

- **"Whoever withholds a right Allah has imposed will spend twice as much later" — NOT LOCATED.** Searched
  Nahj al-Balāgha (all 480 ḥikam blocks), *Wasāʾil* abwāb manʿ al-zakāt, and the standard maxim corpus.
  The *pastoral* point (invest the time now or pay double later) is sound and he can keep making it — but
  not as "the hadith from the Imam."

- **His source-book is identifiable, and that is useful.** The hadith set he works through — *akrimū
  awlādakum*, *fal-yataṣāba lahu*, the Prophet stroking his children's and grandchildren's heads from
  *ʿUddat al-Dāʿī*, *tabassum al-rajul*, the 7-7-7 division — matches the Lebanese **Jamʿiyyat al-Maʿārif
  al-Islāmiyya al-Thaqāfiyya** curriculum (*al-Walad al-Ṣāliḥ*, 1st ed. 2006, and its companion
  *al-Tarbiya bi'l-Ḥubb*) almost item for item. That is the "second book" he refers to at [16:12].
  A parent who wants the full list can go straight there.

## Quranic verses

### 30:30 — al-fiṭra ("the way we're hardwired")
**Verse (Arabic).** فَأَقِمْ وَجْهَكَ لِلدِّينِ حَنِيفًا ۚ فِطْرَتَ اللَّهِ الَّتِي فَطَرَ النَّاسَ عَلَيْهَا ۚ لَا تَبْدِيلَ لِخَلْقِ اللَّهِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ الدِّينُ الْقَيِّمُ وَلَٰكِنَّ أَكْثَرَ النَّاسِ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

**Translation (Ali Quli Qarai).** "So set your heart on the religion as a people of pure faith, the origination
(*fiṭrat*) of Allah according to which He originated mankind. There is no altering Allah's creation; that
is the upright religion, but most people do not know."

**Context of revelation.** Makkan; no specific *sabab* is attached to this verse in either al-Mīzān or
Majmaʿ al-Bayān.

**Tafsīr al-Mīzān.** **ALREADY IN THE SHARED INDEX** — see `_verified-index.md`, al-Mīzān table, 30:30.
Established there: *fiṭra* = the invariant human constitution; ***lā tabdīla li-khalq Allāh* is a statement
of fact, not a prohibition** — al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī lists the prohibition reading among *others'* opinions and
explicitly rejects it; parents *obscure* the fiṭra, they cannot change it. **Not in al-Mīzān:** any
instruction to parents in *lā tabdīl*.

**Other Shia scholarship.** Index-covered: the al-Kāfī *bāb fiṭrat al-khalq ʿalā al-tawḥīd* cluster
(2:12-13, h.1-4), of which h.2 and h.3 are *ṣaḥīḥ* on both al-Majlisī's and Behbūdī's reckoning.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct.** He recites only "*fiṭrat Allāh allatī faṭara al-nās ʿalayhā…
lā tabdīla li-khalq Allāh*" and uses it to establish the *category* of a fiṭrī need as against a manufactured
one (his cell-phone example). He does **not** claim the verse itself says love is a fiṭrī need — he argues that
separately from Ziyārat Amīn Allāh. That is a legitimate two-step and he keeps the steps apart. His paraphrase
"there is no changing the creation of Allah *when it comes to this discussion*" is loose but does not tip into
the prohibition reading al-Mīzān rejects.

**Confidence.** STRONGLY VERIFIED (verse and its tafsīr position, via index).

### 3:31 — "If you love Allah, follow me"
**Verse (Arabic).** قُلْ إِن كُنتُمْ تُحِبُّونَ اللَّهَ فَاتَّبِعُونِي يُحْبِبْكُمُ اللَّهُ وَيَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ ذُنُوبَكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

**Translation (Ali Quli Qarai).** "Say, 'If you love Allah, then follow me; Allah will love you and forgive
you your sins, and Allah is all-forgiving, all-merciful.'"

**Context of revelation.** Majmaʿ al-Bayān gives the *sabab* explicitly, and it is a good one for this lecture:
«نزلت الآيتان في وفد نجران من النصارى لمّا قالوا إنا نعظم المسيح حباً لله» — the verse came down concerning
the **Christian delegation of Najrān**, who said "we venerate the Messiah out of love for God." I.e. the verse
is a *test* applied to a love-claim: if the love is real, it will show as following. This is precisely the
speaker's point, and he did not know he had it.

**Tafsīr al-Mīzān (Arabic actually retrieved).**
- «الحب الذي هو بحسب الحقيقة الوسيلة الوحيدة لارتباط كل طالب بمطلوبه»
- «الحب في الحقيقة هو العلقة الرابطة التي تربط أحد الشيئين بالآخر»
- «حب الشيء يقتضي حب جميع ما يتعلق به، ويوجب الخضوع والتسليم لكل ما هو في جانبه»
- «إنما يجذب المحب إلى محبوبه ليجده ويتم بالمحبوب ما للمحب من النقص»
- «ينتهي الحب إلى الإخلاص، والإخلاص إلى مجموع الشريعة»
So: love is the *only* bond between a seeker and what he seeks; loving a thing necessarily entails loving
everything attached to it and *submitting* to whatever is on its side; and love terminates in *ikhlāṣ*, and
*ikhlāṣ* in the whole of the sharīʿa. That is a direct scriptural warrant for "willing obedience follows love."

**Other Shia scholarship.**
- **Majmaʿ al-Bayān (al-Ṭabrisī):** «إن كنتم صادقين في دعوة محبة الله تعالى فاتبعوني فإنكم إن فعلتم ذلك أحبكم
  الله» and, decisively for this lecture, «**ومحبة العبد لله هي إرادته لطاعاته**» — the servant's love for God
  *is* his willing of obedience to Him. Not a consequence of love; an identity.
- **al-Kāfī 2:125**, *bāb al-ḥubb fī Allāh wa'l-bughḍ fī Allāh* (ch.60), **h.5**: Fuḍayl b. Yasār asks whether
  love and hate are part of faith; Imām al-Ṣādiq: «**وهل الإيمان إلا الحب والبغض**» then recites 49:7.
  Chain: ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm ← his father ← Ḥammād ← Ḥarīz ← Fuḍayl b. Yasār. **al-Majlisī: *ḥasan ka'l-ṣaḥīḥ***
  (Behbūdī did not include it).

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct, and under-sourced by him.** He recites the verse for the
proposition that love produces willing obedience — al-Mīzān and Majmaʿ al-Bayān both say exactly that, and
al-Ṭabrisī says something stronger. Two drifts to note, neither serious:
1. He glosses *fattabiʿūnī* as "**obey** me," and at [43:39] as "then obey me, then follow me." The verb is
   *ittibāʿ* (following/adherence), not *ṭāʿa*. The tafsīr traditions treat the two as convertible here, so
   the meaning survives, but the wording is his, not the verse's.
2. He immediately extends it to *wilāya* — "the biggest challenge in the religion is wilāya… how does God
   introduce the idea that we should obey the prophet in this verse?" **Neither al-Mīzān nor Majmaʿ al-Bayān
   takes 3:31 to *wilāya*.** That is a homiletic extension. (A hadith tradition does apply the verse to love
   of the Prophet and his family, but he did not cite it and it is not what these two tafsīrs say.)

**Confidence.** STRONGLY VERIFIED for the verse and both tafsīr positions; the *wilāya* extension is
NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION.

## Narrations

### N1. "The best legacy: three things, the first a righteous child" [01:01]
**Arabic.** «لَيْسَ يَتْبَعُ الرَّجُلَ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهِ مِنَ الْأَجْرِ إِلَّا ثَلَاثُ خِصَالٍ: صَدَقَةٌ أَجْرَاهَا فِي حَيَاتِهِ فَهِيَ تَجْرِي بَعْدَ مَوْتِهِ، وَسُنَّةُ هُدًى سَنَّهَا فَهِيَ تُعْمَلُ بِهَا بَعْدَ مَوْتِهِ، وَوَلَدٌ صَالِحٌ يَسْتَغْفِرُ لَهُ»

**Translation.** "Nothing of reward follows a man after his death except three things: a charity he set
running in his lifetime, which keeps running after his death; a sunna of guidance he established, which is
acted on after his death; and a righteous child who seeks forgiveness for him."

**Reference.** Imām al-Ṣādiq. **Biḥār al-Anwār 71:257**; the same text is standard in the Shia *waṣāyā*
material and is the Imāmī recension of the Prophetic «إذا مات ابن آدم انقطع عمله إلا من ثلاث» (Sunni: **Muslim
1631**, *ṣaḥīḥ*; Abū Dāwūd 2880; Nasāʾī 3651).

**Chain.** Not retrieved for the Biḥār citation.

**Authenticity.** No published Imāmī grading located. The Sunni parallel is *ṣaḥīḥ* (Muslim). The content is
uncontested across both traditions.

**Confidence.** MODERATELY VERIFIED (text and attribution solid; exact Imāmī chain/grade unpinned).

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct in substance, two small drifts.** (a) He calls it "the best
legacy"; the text says "nothing *follows* a man except three" — a narrower and stronger claim. (b) He says
the righteous child is "the first one he mentions"; it is the **third** in the standard ordering (charity,
sunna of guidance, righteous child). Neither drift changes the teaching.

**Practical meaning.** The child is not the parent's project but the parent's continuing account. Note also
that the **second** item — *sunnat hudan sannahā* — is the very thing he cites separately and loosely at
[57:55]; it is sitting in this same hadith.

### N2. "Whoever withholds a right Allah has imposed will spend twice as much later" [05:04]
**Arabic.** Reconstruction attempted from the garbled caption; no fixed Arabic could be matched.

**Reference.** **NOT LOCATED — do not attribute.** Checked: Nahj al-Balāgha short sayings (including the
201-250 block, which is where a "منع حقاً لله / أنفق في باطل مثليه" saying would most plausibly sit — it is
not there); *Wasāʾil al-Shīʿa* vol.9 abwāb manʿ al-zakāt (the natural home for a "withheld a dirham / spent
two" narration); and general searches on «أنفق في باطل مثليه», «أنفق اثنين في غير حقه», «ضعفين». Nothing
matched in Shia or Sunni corpora.

**Chain / Authenticity.** N/A.

**Confidence.** NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION (leaning: not a narration).

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Wrong as attributed.** He introduces it as "the hadith from the Imam."
The nearest attested principle is 30:30-adjacent and Nahj al-Balāgha Ḥikmah 244 («إِنَّ لِلَّهِ فِي كُلِّ نِعْمَةٍ
حَقّاً فَمَنْ أَدَّاهُ زَادَهُ وَمَنْ قَصَّرَ عَنْهُ خَاطَرَ بِزَوَالِ نِعْمَتِهِ») — "In every blessing Allah has a right;
whoever discharges it, He increases it for him, and whoever falls short of it gambles with the loss of his
blessing." That is a real, citable text making a close-enough version of his point.

**Practical meaning.** As pastoral arithmetic ("pay the time now or pay double later") it is fine and
memorable. It just should not carry an Imam's name.

### N3. "Love children and have mercy on them" [19:18]
**INDEX ENTRY — see `_verified-index.md`:** «أحِبُّوا الصِّبْيَانَ وَارْحَمُوهُمْ، وَإِذَا وَعَدْتُمُوهُمْ شَيْئاً فَفُوا لَهُمْ،
فَإِنَّهُمْ لَا يَدْرُونَ إِلَّا أَنَّكُمْ تَرْزُقُونَهُمْ» — **al-Kāfī 6:49-50, ch.35, h.3; al-Majlisī: *majhūl***.

**Verdict on the speaker's use in THIS lecture.** **Meaning truncated, exactly as the index predicts.**
At [19:18] he quotes only "love children and have mercy on them" and builds the whole "message of love"
section on it. The **promise-keeping clause is the distinctive half of the narration** and it is missing
again. Note that the same chapter's **h.8** repeats the promise clause independently, so the omission is not
a textual doubt — it is a habit. Recommend restoring it: *"and when you promise them something, keep it,
because they think it is you who provide for them."*

**Confidence.** MODERATELY VERIFIED (via index; *majhūl* chain).

### N4. "Honour your children and perfect their manners, and you will be forgiven" [19:18]-[20:19]
**Arabic.** «أَكْرِمُوا أَوْلَادَكُمْ وَأَحْسِنُوا آدَابَهُمْ يُغْفَرْ لَكُمْ»

**Translation.** "Honour your children and give them good manners/discipline, and you will be forgiven."

**Reference.** The Prophet. **al-Ṭabrisī, *Makārim al-Akhlāq* 1:254**; *Wasāʾil al-Shīʿa* 21:476;
*Biḥār al-Anwār* 101:95. Sunni parallel: **Ibn Māja 3671** («أكرموا أولادكم وأحسنوا أدبهم»), graded
*ḍaʿīf* by al-Albānī.

**Chain.** None. *Makārim al-Akhlāq* is a **chainless** compilation — the index already flags it as
ungradeable, and that applies here.

**Authenticity.** Chainless, therefore no *isnād*-based grading is possible. The Sunni recension is weak.
Content is uncontroversial and corroborated by the *ikrām*/*adab* material in *bāb ta'dīb al-walad*.

**Confidence.** MODERATELY VERIFIED.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct.** This is the hadith behind his whole *karāma* framework
("Islamic self-worth, dignity, respect, honour"), and he renders it accurately including the *yughfar lakum*
apodosis. He is right that the hadith couples **dignity** and **adab** in a single conditional — that pairing
is the point, and he draws it out well. He should say, though, that it is *Makārim*-class material, not
al-Kāfī-class.

**Practical meaning.** The forgiveness clause is doing real work: raising a child with dignity is presented
as an *act of worship with a reward*, not merely a technique.

### N5. Ziyārat Amīn Allāh — "beloved in Your earth and heaven" [23:23]-[24:25], [72:14]
**Arabic (verified verbatim).** «…مُحِبَّةً لِصَفْوَةِ أَوْلِيَائِكَ، مَحْبُوبَةً فِي أَرْضِكَ وَسَمَائِكَ…»

**Translation (al-Islam.org).** "…loving Your chosen friends, [and let it be] well-liked on Your earth and
in Your heavens."

**Reference.** **Ziyārat Amīn Allāh**, recited by **Imām ʿAlī b. al-Ḥusayn Zayn al-ʿĀbidīn (a)** at the grave
of Amīr al-Muʾminīn; transmitted in **Shaykh al-Ṭūsī's *Miṣbāḥ al-Mutahajjid*** and thence into
*Mafātīḥ al-Jinān*. The clause the speaker quotes is in the *duʿāʾ* portion that follows the address to the
Imam, which is exactly where he places it.

**Chain.** A ziyāra text, not a legal report; the transmission runs through Jābir al-Juʿfī from Imām al-Bāqir
in the standard recension. No rijāl grading is customarily applied.

**Authenticity.** Universally accepted in practice; not gradeable as a legal hadith.

**Confidence.** STRONGLY VERIFIED (wording confirmed character-for-character).

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct on the text; one logical stretch.** He is right that he is
quoting Imām al-Sajjād, right that both clauses are there ("a lover of Your chosen ones" / "beloved in Your
earth and Your heaven"), and right that they sit inside a supplication for oneself. The stretch: he treats
this as *evidence that being loved is a need of the fiṭra*. It is a **request**, not a diagnosis of human
nature. That being beloved is worth asking God for is strong support for its value; it is not a proof text
for a developmental claim. Framed as "even the Imam asks for this — so do not treat your child's hunger for
it as weakness," it holds up perfectly.

**Practical meaning.** The pairing is the useful bit: *muḥibba* (a lover) and *maḥbūba* (beloved) are asked
for together. Loving and being loved are one petition.

### N6. "The Prophet would stroke the heads of his children and grandchildren" [33:32], [73:14]
**Arabic.** «كَانَ إِذَا أَصْبَحَ مَسَحَ عَلَى رُؤُوسِ وَلَدِهِ وَوَلَدِ وَلَدِهِ»

**Translation.** "When he rose in the morning he would pass his hand over the heads of his children and his
children's children."

**Reference.** **Ibn Fahd al-Ḥillī, *ʿUddat al-Dāʿī wa Najāḥ al-Sāʿī*, p.87.**

**Chain.** None. *ʿUddat al-Dāʿī* is a 9th/15th-century devotional manual and is **chainless** at this point
— the same caveat the shared index already records against it for the "*lā taḍribhu, wahjurhu wa lā tuṭil*"
narration.

**Authenticity.** Ungradeable. Late attestation relative to the early corpus. The adjacent well-attested
material is about stroking the head of an **orphan** (Musnad Aḥmad 25722, 25843: «مَنْ مَسَحَ رَأْسَ يَتِيمٍ لَمْ
يَمْسَحْهُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ كَانَ لَهُ بِكُلِّ شَعْرَةٍ مَرَّتْ عَلَيْهَا يَدُهُ حَسَنَاتٌ»), which is a different (and stronger)
tradition.

**Confidence.** NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION for the "children and grandchildren" form.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct-but-overstated in frequency.** The text says he did this
***when he rose in the morning*** — a daily ritual gesture. The speaker generalises it to "when he would see
his children or his grandchildren," which is broader than the source. He also presents it as though it were
a hadith of the same weight as the al-Kāfī material; it is not.

**Practical meaning.** Better preserved as what it is: a fixed morning gesture, which is arguably a more
useful thing for a parent to imitate than a vague instruction to be affectionate.

### N7. "A man is on the religion of his friend" [36:34]
**Arabic.** «الْمَرْءُ عَلَى دِينِ خَلِيلِهِ، فَلْيَنْظُرْ أَحَدُكُمْ مَنْ يُخَالِلُ»

**Translation.** "A man is upon the religion of his close friend, so let each of you look at whom he
befriends."

**Reference.** **Sunan Abī Dāwūd 4833; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhī 2378** (Abū Hurayra). Also al-Baghawī,
*Sharḥ al-Sunna*.

**Chain / Authenticity.** **Sunni.** Graded *ḥasan* by al-Tirmidhī and by al-Albānī. **No Imāmī primary
source was located for this exact wording** — searches of al-Kāfī's *Kitāb al-ʿIshra* chapters on choosing
companions (2/4/3, "People Whose Friendship and Association is Necessary") returned the theme but not the
sentence.

**Confidence.** STRONGLY VERIFIED as a Sunni hadith; NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION as an Imāmī one.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct-but-misattributed by tradition.** He introduces it as "the
hadith from our Prophet Muhammad ṣallā Allāhu ʿalayhi wa ālihi wa sallam" without qualification, in a lecture
whose evidentiary base is otherwise Imāmī. The saying is genuinely Prophetic in the Sunni corpus and no Shia
scholar disputes its content, but a parent citing it should know which shelf it came off. The nearest Imāmī
substitute with the same force is in al-Kāfī's companions chapter: *"Consider whom you associate with —
after death everyone will find the forms of his associates."*

**Practical meaning.** He uses it well: as the *diagnostic* step in the "detective work" case study
(the 5-year-old with foul language whose beloved uncle swears). That inference — trace the behaviour to the
person the child loves, not the person the child fears — is the strongest practical move in the lecture.

### N8. Imām al-Kāẓim to Hārūn al-Rashīd — "I am the imam of hearts" [44:41]
**Arabic.** «أَنْتَ الَّذِي يُبَايِعُكَ النَّاسُ؟ قَالَ: نَعَمْ، أَنَا إِمَامُ الْقُلُوبِ، وَأَنْتَ إِمَامُ الْجُسُومِ»

**Translation.** Hārūn: "Are you the one people pledge allegiance to?" The Imam: "Yes. I am the imam of
hearts, and you are the imam of bodies."

**Reference.** **al-Qāḍī al-Tustarī (al-Marʿashī), *Iḥqāq al-Ḥaqq* 19:543**; a variant is cited in modern
Arabic biographies of Imām al-Riḍā.

**Chain.** None given. *Iḥqāq al-Ḥaqq* is an 11th/17th-century polemical compilation (with al-Marʿashī's
20th-century supplements); it is a *collection of citations*, not an early source with an isnād.

**Authenticity.** **Late and chainless.** Not in al-Kāfī, not in al-Mufīd's *al-Irshād*, not in the early
*ʿUyūn/Kamāl* material as far as could be traced. Widely repeated in modern Shia homiletics.

**Confidence.** NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION.

**Arabic (what the source actually says).** «مَنْ قَبَّلَ وَلَدَهُ كَتَبَ اللَّهُ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ لَهُ حَسَنَةً، وَمَنْ فَرَّحَهُ

وُجُوهُ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ»

**Translation.** "Whoever kisses his child, Allah writes a good deed for him. Whoever gladdens him, Allah
will gladden him on the Day of Resurrection. And whoever teaches him the Qur'an — his parents will be summoned
and clothed in two robes whose light illuminates the faces of the people of paradise."

**Reference.** **al-Kāfī 6:49, *Kitāb al-ʿAqīqa*, *bāb al-rifq bi'l-awlād* (ch.35), h.1.** The Prophet, via

### N17. Two believers who embrace [74:14]-[75:15]
**Arabic.** «إِنَّ الْمُؤْمِنَيْنِ إِذَا اعْتَنَقَا غَمَرَتْهُمَا الرَّحْمَةُ، فَإِذَا الْتَزَمَا لَا يُرِيدَانِ بِذَلِكَ إِلَّا وَجْهَ اللَّهِ…
قِيلَ لَهُمَا: مَغْفُوراً لَكُمَا فَاسْتَأْنِفَا»

**Translation.** "When two believers embrace, mercy engulfs them. And when they hold one another wanting
nothing by it but the face of Allah… it is said to them: *you are both forgiven, so begin anew*."

**Reference.** **al-Kāfī, *Kitāb al-Īmān wa'l-Kufr*, *bāb al-muʿānaqa* (ch.79), h.2.** Imām al-Ṣādiq.
(The full hadith continues: the angels withdraw from two believers in private conversation out of respect
for them.)

**Chain.** ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm ← his father ← **Ṣafwān b. Yaḥyā** ← **Isḥāq b. ʿAmmār** ← Abū ʿAbdillāh (a).

**Authenticity.** ***ḤASAN MUWATHTHAQ*** — **al-Majlisī, *Mirʾāt al-ʿUqūl***. **Behbūdī: *lam yukhrijhu***.
(The *muwaththaq* element is Isḥāq b. ʿAmmār, reckoned a Fatḥī by some rijālists though *thiqa*.)

**Confidence.** STRONGLY VERIFIED.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct, with one caveat he should state.** He renders it accurately,
including the two distinctive elements — *"they don't want anything but Allah's pleasure"* and *"you have both
been forgiven, begin a new."* The caveat: **the hadith is about two believers (*al-muʾminayn*), and the
forgiveness clause is conditioned on the embrace being purely for Allah.** He then applies it directly to
hugging one's own small children ("I come over, I hug my children… our sins are forgiven just by hugging").
That is an *extension by analogy*, not what the text says — a small child is not the *muʾmin* peer the
narration has in view, and the *lā yurīdāni bihi illā wajh Allāh* condition does not translate cleanly.
The extension is homiletically reasonable; it should be marked as an extension.

**Practical meaning.** *Fa'sta'nifā* — "begin again" — is the striking word. The narration treats an embrace
as a reset, not just a reward.

### N19. Nahj al-Balāgha Letter 31 as a letter of written love [76:17]
**Reference.** **Nahj al-Balāgha, Letter 31** — al-Sharīf al-Raḍī's own heading places it as written **to

الصَّبِيُّ، فَيَصِيحُ بَعْضُ مَنْ رَآهُ، فَيَنْهَاهُمْ قَائِلاً: **لَا تُزْرِمُوا** بِالصَّبِيِّ حَتَّى يَقْضِيَ بَوْلَهُ»

**Translation.** "A small child would be brought to him so he might invoke blessing on him, and he would place
him in his lap as an honour to his family. Sometimes the child would urinate, and someone seeing it would cry
out — and he would forbid them, saying: **do not cut the child off** until he has finished."

**Reference.** **al-Ṭabrisī, *Makārim al-Akhlāq*** — the *sunan al-nabī* material on the Prophet's dealings
with people, reproduced in the standard Shia biographical compilations.

**Chain.** None. *Makārim al-Akhlāq* is **chainless**. Sunni parallel with an isnād: **Bukhārī 5468** and
**Muslim 286** (a nursing infant urinates on the Prophet; he calls for water and pours it over the spot
without washing it) — the *lā tuzrimūhu* verb belongs in the Sunni corpus to the bedouin-in-the-mosque
incident (Bukhārī 6025).

**Authenticity.** Ungradeable in the Shia form; the underlying incident is *ṣaḥīḥ* in the Sunni corpus.

**Confidence.** MODERATELY VERIFIED.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct, and he gets the best detail right.** He recounts the family's
panic, the Prophet's "let the baby finish," his completing the duʿāʾ or naming, and — critically — *"after the
parents left, then the prophet would go and he would wash the clothes."* That last detail (washing **after
they had gone**, so as not to shame them) is exactly the point of the *Makārim* version and is often dropped.
One qualification: he tells it as a repeated habit ("people would come… a child would be brought"), which is
how *Makārim* frames it, but the isnād-bearing corpus records specific incidents rather than a general
practice.

**Practical meaning.** The teaching is not about urine. It is that the Prophet took the adults' *embarrassment*
as the thing to be managed, and deferred his own discomfort until it could be handled invisibly.

### N23. "Whoever has a small child, let him become a child for him" [80:23]
**Arabic.** «مَنْ كَانَ عِنْدَهُ صَبِيٌّ فَلْيَتَصَابَ لَهُ»

**Translation.** "Whoever has a small child with him, let him become childlike for him."

**Reference.** **al-Shaykh al-Ṣadūq, *Man Lā Yaḥḍuruhu al-Faqīh* 3:483, h.4707.** The Prophet.

**Chain.** Not retrieved; al-Ṣadūq's *Faqīh* transmits many such items *mursal*.

**Authenticity.** No published grading located. Widely cited in the Sunni corpus too (via al-Daylamī and
Ibn ʿAsākir), where it is treated as weak.

**Confidence.** MODERATELY VERIFIED.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** **Correct.** He renders it faithfully — *"the hadith of Ahl al-Bayt teach us
to become a child while we're playing… I become a playmate for you and I become a child while I'm playing"* —
and draws the right distinction (playing *with* the child as an equal vs supervising the child's play as an
adult). He also correctly says the whole family should be *in* the game (his tag example). The one thing to
add: it is a **Faqīh** citation, not al-Kāfī, and it carries no grading.

**Practical meaning.** *Yataṣābā* is a reflexive verb — "make yourself a child." The instruction is about the
adult's internal state, not the activity chosen.

### N24. Free play in the first seven years [81:25]
**INDEX ENTRY:** the 7-7-7 cluster is fully covered — **al-Kāfī 6:47, *bāb ta'dīb al-walad*, h.3** is the
soundest form (al-Majlisī: ***muwaththaq***; **Behbūdī: *ṣaḥīḥ***) and should be preferred. The index also
records that the claim **"free play in years 0-7 makes the child *ḥalīm* in years 7-14" is NOT LOCATED** in
any Ahl al-Bayt narration.

**Verdict on the speaker's use in THIS lecture.** **Mixed, and he stops just short of the error.** At [42:38]
and [43:39] he asserts that if the freedom of the first seven years is given properly, *"the second seven
years, the child will become rules-y… the rules that you give will be rules that the child will carry out."*
That is the same causal claim the index flags — a developmental *consequence* of first-septennium play — and
**no narration establishes it.** At [81:25] he explicitly announces he is about to explain "what'll happen to
the children" if that freedom is limited, then ends the lecture without doing so, so the claim is asserted but
never argued. He should either drop the causal link or present it as contemporary educational theory.

**Confidence.** NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION (via index: not located).

## Non-scripture claims

**"Being loved is a need of the fiṭra."** [21:19]-[24:25] — His central psychological premise. Scripturally
he supports it with Q 30:30 (which establishes the *category* of fiṭra, not this content) and Ziyārat Amīn
Allāh (a *petition*, not a description). **Verdict: the theological framing is his own construction.** It is
a defensible one and consonant with the corpus, but it is a synthesis, not a transmitted teaching. Developmental
psychology supports the underlying point independently (attachment research from Bowlby onward), and he would
be on firmer ground citing that alongside rather than presenting it as a scriptural finding.

**"Ages 7 to 9 are when being respected matters most."** [63:04] — Presented as though it were a fixed
finding. **NOT SOURCED**, and not in the 7-7-7 hadith, which divides at 7 / 14 / 21 and says nothing about a
7-9 sub-window. The general developmental point (self-worth becomes explicitly comparative and socially
referenced in middle childhood) is real, but the specific 7-9 bracket is his.

**"All children are hardwired to be self-centred."** [60:58] — Broadly correct as description of early
childhood cognition (Piagetian egocentrism, roughly ages 2-7 — a *perspective-taking* limitation rather than
selfishness). He uses it well: he treats the child's demands as *needs Allah placed in the fiṭra* rather than
as misbehaviour, which is the right pastoral move and avoids the trap of moralising developmental normality.

**"Personality is formed by observation from infancy; destruction is easier than building."** [07:05]-[09:07]
— His four-point framework for *tarbiya bi'l-mushāhada*, carried over from Lecture 2. Presented explicitly as
principles from his source book, not as revelation. Fair.

**The case studies** — the Muhammadi family [11:08]-[16:12], the girl with the flower in her hair
[26:31]-[30:32], Zahra and the cabinets [59:58], Ali and the grandmother [45:41], Hassan/Land Rover vs Moʿin
/Honda Civic [48:44]-[52:54], the boy and the basketball player [39:37]-[41:38], the 13-year-old's forgotten
birthday [34:32], the young man who stayed overnight in Tehran [55:55]. **All are pastoral illustrations and
he flags them as such** — he explicitly anonymises ("we're going to change the names… keep them in witness
protection"). No verification is possible or required. **He handles these honestly**; none is presented as a
report from the sources, which is a real virtue given how often such stories migrate into the hadith column.

**"Sayyid Hasan… and also his bodyguard Abu Ali, he also went back to Allah recently."** [21:19] — A dedication,
not a claim. The reference is to Sayyid Ḥasan Naṣrallāh (killed in Beirut, 27 September 2024). The identity of
"Abu Ali" is not determinable from the transcript and was not verified. No action needed.

**The source books.** [16:12] "we've now completed the first book… we're moving on to the second book."
**Identified with reasonable confidence:** the hadith set he works through — *akrimū awlādakum wa aḥsinū
ādābahum* (Makārim 1:254), *man kāna ʿindahu ṣabiyy fal-yataṣāba lahu* (Faqīh 3:483 h.4707), the Prophet
stroking his children's and grandchildren's heads (*ʿUddat al-Dāʿī* p.87), *tabassum al-rajul fī wajhi akhīh*,
and the 7-7-7 sayyid/ʿabd/wazīr division (Biḥār 101:95) — reproduces, in order, the curriculum of the Lebanese
**Jamʿiyyat al-Maʿārif al-Islāmiyya al-Thaqāfiyya**: *al-Walad al-Ṣāliḥ* (1st ed., February 2006) and its
companion module *al-Tarbiya bi'l-Ḥubb wa Asālīb al-Taʿāmul maʿa Awlādinā*. **This is a useful finding for a
parent**: it means the material is a translated and adapted Arabic curriculum with its own footnotes, and the
footnotes are checkable.

**"In many verses of the Qur'an the existence of God is a given; the Qur'an introduces God as He is rather
than proving He exists."** [65:06]-[66:07] — A standard and defensible observation in Shia philosophical
theology (Muṭahharī and Jawādī Āmulī both make versions of it). Correctly framed by him as an observation
about the Qur'an's method rather than as a citation. Fair.

**"Marijuana has been legalised."** [10:08] — Correct in many US states as of 2026; used only as an
illustration of normalisation. No issue.

## Better-sourced substitutes

**For "love is the foundation of my religion" (N11) →**
**al-Kāfī 2:125**, *bāb al-ḥubb fī Allāh wa'l-bughḍ fī Allāh* (ch.60), **h.5**, Imām al-Ṣādiq:
«وَهَلِ الْإِيمَانُ إِلَّا الْحُبُّ وَالْبُغْضُ» — *"Is faith anything but love and hate?"*, followed by Q 49:7.
Chain: ʿAlī b. Ibrāhīm ← his father ← Ḥammād ← Ḥarīz ← Fuḍayl b. Yasār. **al-Majlisī: *ḥasan ka'l-ṣaḥīḥ*.**
This is stronger than what he claimed and it makes love *constitutive of faith*, which is a bigger claim than
"foundation of my ways."

**For "kissing raises a degree, 500 years apart" (N15) →**
(a) **al-Kāfī 6:50, ch.35, h.5**: «إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَيَرْحَمُ الْعَبْدَ لِشِدَّةِ حُبِّهِ لِوَلَدِهِ» — al-Majlisī:
***ḥasan wa ākhiruhu mursal*** (index-covered). (b) **Bukhārī 5997** for the kissing incident itself, *ṣaḥīḥ*.
Both say what he wanted to say without the invented reward-arithmetic.

**For "nothing attracts the heart like the tongue" (N14) →**
He does not need it. **al-Kāfī, *bāb iʿlām al-akh bi'l-ḥubb*, h.2** (N13) — ***ṣaḥīḥ* on both gradings** —
already gives the mechanism explicitly: *fa-innahu **athbatu li'l-mawadda** baynakumā*, "it makes the affection
between you firmer." Use the *ṣaḥīḥ* hadith with the built-in reason rather than an unlocated maxim.

**For "withholding a right → spending double" (N2) →**
**Nahj al-Balāgha, Ḥikmah 244**: «إِنَّ لِلَّهِ فِي كُلِّ نِعْمَةٍ حَقّاً، فَمَنْ أَدَّاهُ زَادَهُ، وَمَنْ قَصَّرَ عَنْهُ خَاطَرَ
بِزَوَالِ نِعْمَتِهِ» — "In every blessing Allah has a right; whoever discharges it, He increases it for him, and
whoever falls short of it gambles with the loss of that blessing." Chainless (Nahj), but canonical, and it
makes the same point about a child being a blessing with a claim attached.

**For "smiling in your brother's face" (N20) →**
Keep the same al-Kāfī hadith but **attribute it to Imām al-Bāqir**, note al-Majlisī's *ḍaʿīf*, and quote the
**third clause**, which is the strong one and independently corroborated across the *idkhāl al-surūr* chapter:
«وَمَا عُبِدَ اللَّهُ بِشَيْءٍ أَحَبَّ إِلَى اللَّهِ مِنْ إِدْخَالِ السُّرُورِ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِ» — *"Allah has not been
worshipped with anything more beloved to Him than bringing joy to a believer."*

**For "call people by their favourite name" (N21) →**
**al-Kāfī 6, *bāb ḥaqq al-awlād* (ch.34)** — the parents' duties chapter, whose first duty is giving the child a
good name. That is the attested obligation; the "favourite name" instruction is not.

**For "the Prophet stroked their heads" (N6) →**
Keep it but cite it honestly as ***ʿUddat al-Dāʿī* p.87, chainless, and specific to the morning**. If a
strongly-graded text is wanted for physical affection, use **Sunan al-Nasāʾī 1141** (*ṣaḥīḥ*, index-covered:
the Prophet prolonging *sujūd* because a grandson was on his back) — same teaching, incomparably better chain.

**For "being loved is a need of the fiṭra" →**
The scriptural anchor that actually works is **al-Kāfī 6:50, ch.35, h.6** (index-covered): the four-point
parenting rubric — accept what the child can manage, overlook what he cannot, do not overburden, do not be
harsh. It grounds the same pastoral programme in an instruction rather than in an inferred psychology.
