## Headline findings

- **The lecture's single best citation is one the speaker gave no source for at all.** The Prophet at the
  fresh grave — "two light rak'ahs… more beloved to him than the rest of your world" — is a real,
  well-attested narration (Abu Hurayra; Ibn al-Mubarak *al-Zuhd* 31, Ibn Abi Shayba *al-Musannaf* 7715,
  al-Tabarani *al-Mu'jam al-Awsat* 920; **al-Albani: *sahih bi-majmu' turuqihi***, *al-Silsila al-Sahiha* 1388).
  His retelling is accurate down to the detail that the Prophet *pointed at the grave*.
- **The "Leader's" Qur'anic argument checks out on the text, even though the attribution could not be
  confirmed.** Al-Mizan independently supports both halves: it assigns **3:169 to the Uhud passage**
  («الآيات من تتمة الآيات النازلة في خصوص غزوة أُحد») while at **2:154** it treats the five verses as one
  non-military *siyaq* about patience, prayer and trial, and **explicitly rejects** restricting the verse to
  the martyrs of Badr. The contextual claim is sound; the attribution to Ayatollah Khamenei at a Raisi
  memorial is **NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION** (khamenei.ir was unreachable this session).
- **Two claims go beyond their sources in the same direction — making a good text say more than it says.**
  (a) Q 2:286 says obligation is *within* capacity; al-Mizan reads it as **equal to** capacity. The hadith
  the speaker adds — that Allah imposes **less** than capacity — **could not be located** in al-Kafi's
  chapter on *istita'a*, in al-Mizan's or Majma' al-Bayan's treatment of 2:286, or in the Sunni corpus.
  (b) The *rukhas/'aza'im* hadith he took from Shaheed Mutahhari is authentic (**sahih**, Ibn Hibban 3568 /
  Ahmad 5866 / Ibn Khuzayma 2027) but its subject is **legal dispensations** (shortening prayer, breaking
  the fast in travel), not "the freedoms Allah has allowed" in general.
- **Three story-details do not survive checking:** the child climbing on the Prophet's back **eight times**
  (no count appears in any version — Nasa'i 1141 is *sahih* and gives none); the children selling the
  Prophet-as-camel for **eight walnuts** (NOT LOCATED, Sunni or Shia); and the **124,000 prophets rising for
  a passing believer** (NOT LOCATED — the speaker himself flagged he never saw the Arabic, and he was right
  to hedge). The **"Chinese proverb"** about planting a tree is **not Chinese** — earliest trace is a 1967
  Ohio newspaper.
- **He is stronger than he claimed twice.** The arrogance/low-self-esteem narration is correctly attributed
  to **Imam al-Sadiq** (al-Kafi, *bab al-kibr*, h.17 — the audit brief's worry about an al-Sajjad mix-up is
  unfounded), and the Jabir "excellent mount" narration really is **from Jabir b. 'Abdillah**, exactly as he
  said — though it is graded weak. And Imam al-Sajjad's du'a for parents (**Sahifa Sajjadiyya, Du'a 24**)
  contains the precise clause he paraphrased about forgiving parents' mistakes.
- **One classical text is used against its grain.** "*La ra'ya li-man la yuta'*" is **Nahj al-Balagha,
  Khutba 27** — Imam Ali's bitter rebuke of followers who would not fight after the raid on al-Anbar. It is a
  lament about *his own* unobeyed authority, not a principle for training obedient children.

## Quranic verses

### 2:154 — "Do not say of those killed in the way of Allah: dead"

**Verse**
> وَلَا تَقُولُوا لِمَن يُقْتَلُ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ أَمْوَاتٌ ۚ بَلْ أَحْيَاءٌ وَلَٰكِن لَّا تَشْعُرُونَ

**Translation** (Ali Quli Qara'i)
> "Do not call those who were slain in Allah's way 'dead.' Rather they are living, but you are not aware."

**Context**
The verse sits inside a block of five verses that share one *siyaq*: 2:153 (*seek help through patience and
prayer*), 2:154 (this verse), 2:155 (*We will surely test you with something of fear, hunger, loss of wealth,
lives and fruits*), 2:156 (*inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un*), 2:157 (*upon them are blessings from their
Lord and mercy*). What precedes 2:153 is the qibla material (2:142-152). **There is no battle narrative
before or after it.** The speaker's report of the Leader's argument is therefore correct as a plain reading
of the page: the verse is not embedded in a war passage.

**Tafsir al-Mizan** (Arabic retrieved from greattafsirs.com, *al-Mizan*, 2:154)
- On the unity of the block: «خمس آيات متحدة السياق، متسقة الجمل، ملتئمة المعاني» — five verses of one
  context, coordinated clauses, interlocking meanings. He traces the movement command (patience/prayer) →
  negation (do not call them dead) → announcement of trial → glad tidings → reward.
- On the nature of the life: «المراد بالحياة في الآية الحياة الحقيقية دون التقديرية» — the life meant is
  **real**, not figurative/reputational.
- On scope, he **rejects** confining the verse to Badr: «ومن أعجب الأمر ما ذكره بعض الناس في الآية: أنها نزلت
  في شهداء بدر، فهي مخصوصة بهم فقط، لا تتعداهم إلى غيرهم» — and argues the *siyaq* itself indicates that
  other believers share in this life: «ولو تدبَّر القائل باختصاص هذه الآيات بشهداء بدر في متن الآيات لوجد أنّ
  سياقها يفيد اشتراك سائر المؤمنين معهم في الحياة».
- This is the site of al-Mizan's celebrated excursus **«كلام في نشأة البرزخ»** — confirmed independently by
  the cross-reference printed at 3:169 (below).

**Other Shia scholarship**
The barzakh-life reading is standard across Imami tafsir. What is *not* standard is extending the
predicate "*yuqtalu fi sabilillah*" — **killed** in the way of Allah — to people who die of natural causes.
Al-Mizan's generalisation is that the **barzakh life** is not confined to Badr's martyrs, and that other
believers share in it; it is not a claim that a parent who dies in bed is "*maqtul fi sabilillah*." The
official Iranian designation of Raisi and his companions as *shuhada-ye khedmat* ("martyrs of service")
belongs to the same broadening impulse and is a **contemporary juridical-homiletic category**, not a Qur'anic
one.

**Verdict**
**Correct on the contextual claim; the extension is a legitimate homiletic move but is one step past what
al-Mizan asserts.** Al-Mizan expands who shares the *life*; the speaker expands who counts as *killed in the
way of Allah*. Parents and teachers dying in the course of a lifetime of *tarbiyah* are not what
"*yuqtalu*" denotes. Present it as the Leader's/scholars' extension, not as the verse's plain sense.

**Confidence: STRONGLY VERIFIED** (verse text, context, al-Mizan positions) / **NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION**
(the attribution of the argument to Ayatollah Khamenei at a Raisi memorial — see "Attributions" below).

### 3:169 — the contrast verse

**Verse**
> وَلَا تَحْسَبَنَّ الَّذِينَ قُتِلُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ أَمْوَاتًا ۚ بَلْ أَحْيَاءٌ عِندَ رَبِّهِمْ يُرْزَقُونَ

**Translation** (Qara'i)
> "Do not suppose those who were slain in the way of Allah to be dead; rather they are living and provided
> for near their Lord."

**Context**
3:169 sits in the Uhud block (roughly 3:121-179), immediately after the verses on the wounded who answered
the call at Hamra' al-Asad and among the verses consoling the community for its dead. This is exactly the
military context the speaker says is present here and absent at 2:154. **He is right.**

**Tafsir al-Mizan** (Arabic retrieved, *al-Mizan*, 3:169)
- «الآيات من تتمة الآيات النازلة في خصوص غزوة أُحد» — these verses complete those revealed specifically
  about the Uhud expedition.
- He cross-refers the theology back to 2:154 rather than repeating it: «وقد فصلنا القول فيه في الكلام على
  نشأة البرزخ في ذيل قوله تعالى: {ولا تقولوا لمن يقتل في سبيل الله أموات}».

**Other Shia scholarship**
The two verses are treated as one doctrine with two settings — which is precisely why the *siyaq* argument
carries weight: the general statement is made at 2:154, and the Uhud verse applies it to a battlefield case.

**Verdict**
**Correct.** The comparative claim is textually accurate and independently confirmed by al-Mizan's own
handling (Uhud at 3:169, a general barzakh treatise at 2:154).

**Confidence: STRONGLY VERIFIED**

### 2:286 — "Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity"

**Verse**
> لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا ۚ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَعَلَيْهَا مَا اكْتَسَبَتْ

**Translation** (Qara'i)
> "Allah does not task any soul beyond its capacity. Whatever it has earned is to its own benefit, and
> whatever it has incurred is to its own harm."

**Context**
Closing verses of al-Baqarah, following 2:284 (*whether you disclose what is in your minds or hide it, Allah
will bring you to account for it*), which caused distress among the Companions. 2:286 is the relief.

**Tafsir al-Mizan** (retrieved, 2:286 and the 2:284-286 block)
Al-Mizan reads *wus'* as **capacity itself**, not something below it: obligation attaches only to what is
within human choice and power — «إجابة داعي الحق بالسمع والطاعة لا تتحقق إلاّ في ما هو اختياري للإنسان تتعلق
به قدرته». **NOT PRESENT:** any statement that the imposed duty is *less* than capacity. The *bahth riwa'i*
attached to this block discusses the Companions' distress at 2:284 and the phrase *illa wus'aha* — it carries
**no narration** with the wording «دون ما يطيقون» or equivalent.

**Other Shia scholarship**
Majma' al-Bayan (al-Tabrisi) on 2:286: also **NOT PRESENT** — he glosses *al-wus'* as what is feasible, with
no "below capacity" formulation. Compare Q 65:7, «لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا مَا آتَاهَا» — again
"what He has given it," not less.

**Verdict**
**The verse is quoted correctly and used well.** The added hadith is a separate problem — see Narration
**N3** below. The pastoral point ("Allah believes you are capable of this") is squarely supported by the
verse; the intensification ("and He actually asks *less* than you can do") is not.

**Confidence: STRONGLY VERIFIED** (verse + tafsir) — but see N3.

## Narrations

### N1. Imam al-Sajjad's supplication for his parents

**Reference**
**Al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya, Supplication 24** — *Du'a'uhu li-abawayhi* ("His Supplication for his Parents").
English text as retrieved from al-Islam.org (William C. Chittick, *The Psalms of Islam*).

**Translation** (Chittick, key clauses)
> "…thank them for my upbringing, reward them for honouring me, and guard them as they guarded me in my
> infancy…"
> "Whatever word through which they have transgressed against me, act through which they have been
> immoderate with me, right of mine which they have left neglected, or obligation toward me in which they
> have fallen short — **I grant it to them and bestow it upon them**, and I beseech Thee to remove from them
> its ill consequence…"
> "…forgive them through their devotion toward me with unfailing forgiveness, be well pleased with them
> through my intercession…"
> "…I can never discharge their right against me, fulfil my obligations toward them, or accomplish the duty
> of serving them."

**Chain**
Al-Sahifa al-Sajjadiyya is transmitted as a book (the Umayri/Zayd b. 'Ali line); individual supplications
carry no separate isnad. Treat as **chainless in the hadith-critical sense**, though the collection's
transmission is far better attested than most chainless compilations.

**Authenticity**
No per-du'a grading exists or is possible. No assessor to name.

**Confidence: STRONGLY VERIFIED** (identification of Du'a 24 and its content).

**Verdict on the speaker's use: CORRECT, and stronger than he realised.** He paraphrased the du'a as
"if they did something right, reward them; if they made mistakes, forgive them" — which is *precisely* the
structure of Du'a 24, and the "I grant it to them and bestow it upon them" clause is the single most exact
warrant in the tradition for what he was urging (do not carry resentment toward parents who did not know
better).
⚠ **One correction of source:** the lines he actually recited — "Allah, forgive me and forgive my parents,
have mercy on them, **they raised me when I was small**" — are **Qur'an 17:24** («رَبِّ ارْحَمْهُمَا كَمَا
رَبَّيَانِي صَغِيرًا») and **71:28** («رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي وَلِوَالِدَيَّ»), not the Sahifa. Both are correct
texts; the attribution slipped.

**Practical meaning**
The remedy he wanted — releasing a claim rather than suppressing a grievance — is a formal act in the du'a
("I grant it to them"), not merely a feeling.

### N2. The Prophet at a freshly dug grave — two light rak'ahs

**Arabic** (as printed in al-Albani, *al-Silsila al-Sahiha* 1388)
> رَكْعَتَانِ خَفِيفَتَانِ مِمَّا تَحْقِرُونَ وَتَنَفَّلُونَ، يَزِيدُهُمَا هَذَا — يُشِيرُ إِلَى قَبْرٍ —
> فِي عَمَلِهِ، أَحَبُّ إِلَيْهِ مِنْ بَقِيَّةِ دُنْيَاكُمْ

**Translation**
> "Two light rak'ahs of the sort you belittle and offer as extras — that **this one** (pointing to a grave)
> should add them to his record — would be **more beloved to him than the remainder of your world**."

**Reference**
Narrated from **Abu Hurayra**. Sources: **Ibn al-Mubarak, *al-Zuhd*, no. 31**; **Ibn Abi Shayba,
*al-Musannaf*, no. 7715**; **al-Tabarani, *al-Mu'jam al-Awsat*, no. 920**. (Not in the Six Books; not on
sunnah.com.)

**Chain**
Multiple routes, individually flawed.

**Authenticity**
**Al-Albani: «صحيح بمجموع طرقه» — *sahih* by the aggregate of its routes** (*al-Silsila al-Sahiha* 1388);
also *Sahih al-Jami'* 3518. No Imami grading located; I found **no Shia primary locus** for it, so it should
be presented as a Sunni-transmitted narration.

**Confidence: STRONGLY VERIFIED** (text, narrator, sources, named assessor).

**Verdict on the speaker's use: CORRECT — one of his best moments.** He got the setting right (a grave
freshly filled, the family still weeping), the gesture right (the Prophet indicating the grave), and the
lesson right ("take advantage of this time").
⚠ Two small precisions worth keeping: (1) the text says "**the remainder of** your world" (*baqiyyat
dunyakum*), not "your entire world"; (2) the desire belongs to **the deceased for his own record**
(*yazīduhumā hādhā fī 'amalih*) — it is about the opportunity *he* has lost, not about thawab you send him.
The speaker's own framing of the second point was correct; his framing a minute earlier (making the dead
"partners in the thawab of this session") is a different and separate matter and does not rest on this text.

**Practical meaning**
The narration prices the smallest deliberate act of worship against everything a living person still has.

### N3. "Allah did not burden the servants except with **less** than they can bear"

**Arabic as the speaker rendered it**
"Allah didn't put any burden on the servants except **less than** you're capable of."
(The circulating Arabic form would be «مَا كَلَّفَ اللَّهُ الْعِبَادَ إِلَّا دُونَ مَا يُطِيقُونَ».)

**Reference**
**NOT LOCATED — do not attribute.**

**Absence checks performed deliberately:**
- **al-Kafi, vol. 1, Kitab al-Tawhid, ch. "The Capabilities" (*al-Istita'a*), all 4 hadiths** — the chapter's

- **al-Mizan on 2:284-286, including its *bahth riwa'i*** — NOT PRESENT.
- **Majma' al-Bayan on 2:286** — NOT PRESENT.
- **al-Durar al-Saniyya Sunni hadith encyclopaedia (dorar.net), query «دون ما يطيقون»** — no results.

**Chain / Authenticity**
None to report; the text was not located in any collection consulted.

**Confidence: NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION** — and, on the strength of four deliberate absence checks,
leaning toward **not a narration at all** but a homiletic intensification of 2:286 common on the minbar.

**Verdict on the speaker's use: MEANING ALTERED (as against the verse he had just read).** Q 2:286 and

Told at [23:17]-[24:19]. Three separable claims:

**Already in `_verified-index.md`: ❌ NOT LOCATED** in any source, Sunni or Shia. The index identifies it as
a conflation of **Nasa'i 1141** (the prolonged sujud) with **Bukhari 5997** (al-Aqra' b. Habis — who is a

**(b) The prolonged sujud itself** — genuine. See N15.

**(c) "He got on eight times" / the onlooker counting.**
**NOT LOCATED — do not attribute.** I re-read **Sunan al-Nasa'i 1141** in full: the Prophet came out

mount and I disliked to hurry him until he had had his fill."* Narrator: **'Abdullah b. Shaddad ← his
father**. **Grading: *sahih* (Darussalam).** **The text specifies no number of times.** No version I
located — Shia or Sunni — carries a count.

**Verdict on the speaker's use: WRONG on the frame (unlocated conflation) and WRONG on "eight times"
(no source has a count); CORRECT on the underlying sujud narration and on its lesson.**

**Confidence:** frame — **NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION / effectively refuted**; "eight times" —
**NOT LOCATED**; sujud — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**.

### N8. Arrogance arises from a lowliness found within

**Arabic** (al-Kafi, as printed on thaqalayn.com)
> مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ يَحْيَى، عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ أَحْمَدَ، عَنْ **بَعْضِ أَصْحَابِهِ**، عَنِ النَّهْدِيِّ،
> عَنْ يَزِيدَ بْنِ إِسْحَاقَ شَعِرٍ، عَنْ عَبْدِ اللهِ بْنِ الْمُنْذِرِ، عَنْ عَبْدِ اللهِ بْنِ بُكَيْرٍ،
> قَالَ: قَالَ أَبُو عَبْدِ اللهِ (ع): **مَا مِنْ أَحَدٍ يَتِيهُ إِلَّا مِنْ ذِلَّةٍ يَجِدُهَا فِي نَفْسِهِ**.
> **وَفِي حَدِيثٍ آخَرَ** عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ اللهِ (ع) قَالَ: **مَا مِنْ رَجُلٍ تَكَبَّرَ أَوْ تَجَبَّرَ
> إِلَّا لِذِلَّةٍ وَجَدَهَا فِي نَفْسِهِ**.

**Translation**
> "No one puffs himself up except out of a lowliness he finds in himself." And in another report:
> "There is no man who is arrogant or overbearing except because of a lowliness he found in himself."

**Reference**
**al-Kafi, vol. 2, Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr, *bab al-kibr* (Chapter of Arrogance), hadith 17**
(commonly cited as al-Kafi 2:312). Source consulted: thaqalayn.com/hadith/2/1/124/17.

**Chain**
Muhammad b. Yahya ← Muhammad b. Ahmad ← **"some of his companions" (unnamed)** ← al-Nahdi ← Yazid b.
Ishaq Sha'ir ← 'Abdullah b. al-Mundhir ← 'Abdullah b. Bukayr ← Abu 'Abdillah (al-Sadiq).

### N11. "Allah loves that His dispensations be taken as He loves His obligations" (via Shaheed Mutahhari)

**Arabic**
> إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ أَنْ تُؤْتَى **رُخَصُهُ** كَمَا يُحِبُّ أَنْ تُؤْتَى **عَزَائِمُهُ**
(variant: «كَمَا يَكْرَهُ أَنْ تُؤْتَى مَعْصِيَتُهُ»)

**Translation**
> "Allah loves that His **dispensations** be acted upon just as He loves that His **strict determinations**
> be acted upon."

**Reference**
**Prophetic, and transmitted in the Sunni corpus**: Ibn 'Umar ← the Prophet — **Sahih Ibn Hibban 3568;
Musnad Ahmad 5866; Sahih Ibn Khuzayma 2027**. A second route from **Ibn 'Abbas** — Ibn Hibban 354, al-Bazzar,
al-Tabarani. A third from 'A'isha is defective (Ibn Hajar, *Lisan al-Mizan*: 'Umar b. 'Ubayd al-Khazzaz
confused it).

**Chain / Authenticity**
**Sahih**, per **al-Nawawi**, **al-Albani** (*Irwa'/Sahih al-Targhib* 1060) and **Ibn Baz** — as recorded in
the al-Durar al-Saniyya encyclopaedia (dorar.net). 25 entries for general readers, 59 for specialists.

**Shia locus:** the wording circulates in Imami works and is routinely attributed to **Imam al-Sadiq**, but
**I could not pin it to a primary Imami collection this session** (it is not in al-Kafi vol. 4, Kitab
al-Siyam, ch. 48 — the travel-fasting chapter where one would expect it). The best-attested *Imami* text on
the same principle is Imam al-Sadiq's «إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَصَدَّقَ عَلَى مَرْضَى أُمَّتِي وَمُسَافِرِيهَا
بِالتَّقْصِيرِ وَالْإِفْطَارِ، أَيَسُرُّ أَحَدَكُمْ إِذَا تَصَدَّقَ بِصَدَقَةٍ أَنْ تُرَدَّ عَلَيْهِ؟»
(al-Kafi, Kitab al-Siyam — Allah's concessions are a *charity*, and returning a charity is an insult).

**Confidence: STRONGLY VERIFIED** as a Prophetic hadith; **NEEDS FURTHER VERIFICATION** for the attribution
to Imam al-Sadiq and for the claim that Shaheed Mutahhari is the route (no Mutahhari passage located).

**Verdict on the speaker's use: CORRECT-BUT-MISATTRIBUTED, and MEANING WIDENED.**
- *Misattributed:* he gave it as a hadith of **Imam al-Sadiq**; the located transmission is **Prophetic, via
  Ibn 'Umar/Ibn 'Abbas, in Sunni collections**.
- *Widened:* ***rukhas*** are **legal dispensations** — shortening prayer in travel, breaking the fast when
  ill or travelling, *tayammum*, wiping over the *khuffs*. They are **not** "the areas Allah has allowed for
  freedom" in the general sense of permissible enjoyments and latitude. The hadith says: *when the law gives
  you a concession, take it — refusing it is not extra piety.* That is a real and useful principle for a
  parent (do not out-strict the Shari'ah with your child), but it is narrower than "children after seven
  still need freedom."

**Practical meaning as actually taught**
Against religiosity-by-severity: God's *easing* provisions are as much His law as His demanding ones.

Pair it with al-Mizan on **17:70** (dignity as intrinsic, not comparative) and the *karamah* case is
complete without leaning on any contemporary attribution at all.

**Verdict: the teaching is sound and classically grounded; the sourcing should be moved from Panahian to
Nahj al-Balagha Hikmah 449 + al-Mizan on 17:70.**

## Better-sourced substitutes

1. **For "Allah asks less than you are capable of"** (N3, unlocated) — use the verse plainly plus a real
   narration that does the same pastoral work:
   - **Q 2:286** and **Q 65:7** as they stand: obligation never exceeds capacity.
   - **al-Kafi 6:50, Kitab al-'Aqiqa, ch. 35, h.6** (already in the index): "May Allah have mercy on one who
     helps his child toward righteousness — **by accepting what he can manage, overlooking what he cannot,
     not overburdening him, and not being harsh with him**." This is a four-point rubric that says about the
     *parent-to-child* relation precisely what the speaker wanted to say about the *God-to-parent* relation,
     and it is in a primary collection.

2. **For "*karamah* is a prerequisite to *taqwa*"** (Panahian, unverifiable) —
   **Nahj al-Balagha, Hikmah 449: «مَنْ كَرُمَتْ عَلَيْهِ نَفْسُهُ هَانَتْ عَلَيْهِ شَهْوَتُهُ»**, plus
   **al-Mizan on 17:70** («التكريم معنى نفسي… والتفضيل معنى إضافي»). Chainless but classical, checkable, and
   states the causal direction explicitly.

3. **For the 124,000 prophets** (unlocated) —
   **al-Saduq, *al-Khisal* 1/27: «الْمُؤْمِنُ أَعْظَمُ حُرْمَةً مِنَ الْكَعْبَةِ»** (index), which makes the
   same point about a believer's standing and is a real text.

4. **For "let your child play seven years"** —
   **al-Kafi 6:47, *bab ta'dib al-walad*, h.3** (**al-Majlisi *muwaththaq*; Behbudi *sahih*** — the soundest
   of the entire seven-year family) rather than h.1 (*mursal*).

5. **For "love is my foundation"** (unlocated) —
   **al-Kafi 2:125, ch. 60, h.5: «وَهَلِ الْإِيمَانُ إِلَّا الْحُبُّ وَالْبُغْضُ»** — ***hasan ka'l-sahih***
   (al-Majlisi).

6. **For "experience is teacher enough"** (chainless maxim) —
   **Nahj al-Balagha, Letter 31**, where Imam Ali explains that he surveyed the record of earlier peoples so
   that his son might have their experience **without living through it**. Better anchored, and closer to the
   lecture's actual thesis about controlled exposure.

7. **For the "eight walnuts" and the Jabir camel narration** — if the parent wants a *sound* text for the
   Prophet playing physically with children, use **Sunan al-Nasa'i 1141** (***sahih***) alone. It carries the
   whole argument: the Prophet let a congregational prostration run long rather than cut a child's play
   short, and named the child's play a **need** (*hajah*).

8. **For "no counsel for one who is not obeyed"** — keep **Nahj Khutba 27** but state its setting. If the
   goal is a text about a child's willing compliance rather than a commander's frustration, the lecture's own
   better material is **al-Kafi 6:50 ch.35 h.6** (accept what he can manage) and **Nahj Hikam 73** (discipline
   yourself first, by conduct before tongue).
