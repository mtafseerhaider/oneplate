# Lecture 12 — Source Verification Audit

*A Guide To Islamic Child Raising (Tarbiyah), Part 12 — Shaykh Usama Abdulghani, "Light Of Guidance"*
Source video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NmjdceUPshY (45:44). Transcript: YouTube auto-captions; Arabic and Persian names heavily garbled, reconstructions flagged throughout.
Prepared for a parent who wants to know which citations hold up. Items already settled in `_verified-index.md` are cited to that file, not re-researched.

## Headline findings

- **The "silent imam" narration is the strongest citation in the lecture, and he is better sourced than he claimed.** It is al-Kafi 1, *Kitab al-Hujja*, ch. 5, h. 1 — **hasan** (al-Majlisi) and **sahih** (Behbudi). The identification of **al-Hasan and al-Husayn**, which is *not* in the hadith text, turns out to be **al-Majlisi's own gloss** in *Mir'at al-'Uqul* 2:294 («كالسبطين عليهما السلام»). What does **not** come from the sources is the "good soldier / followership is a virtue" reading: al-Majlisi defines the silence as silence *about da'wah and claiming the imamate*, not as subordination or teamwork.
- **The Mina haggling story is real and doubly attested in the Four Books** — al-Kafi 4:496 h. 3 and *Tahdhib al-Ahkam* 5:209 h. 702. Two details are the speaker's, not the text's: the text says sacrificial animals had become **scarce/dear** (*fa-'azzat al-adahi*), not that "the businessmen artificially raised the price"; and the maxim *al-maghbun la mahmud wa la ma'jur* is in the primary collections a saying of **the Prophet** (al-Khisal 621/10; *'Uyun akhbar al-Rida* 2:48/184), which Imam al-Sadiq is here quoting.
- **This is the first "the Leader" attribution in the whole series that fully checks out.** Ayatollah Khamenei did write a *taqriz* on the book about this martyr's mother — «تنها گریه کن» (*Tanha Gerye Kon*) by Akram Eslami, on the life of **Ashraf-Sadat Montazeri**, mother of Shahid Mohammad Memarian — unveiled **21 November 2021**. The index records three unverified Khamenei attributions; this one is documented, including on farsi.khamenei.ir.
- **The martyr is identified: Shahid Mohammad Memarian** (b. 20 Mordad 1349 / 11 Aug 1970, Qom; joined the Basij at **12** after his mother intervened; went to the front at **13**; martyred at **16** at Nahr Khayyin, Shalamcheh, in Operation Karbala-4). The 12/13/16 sequence and the mother's intervention are **documented exactly as he told them**. But the "he asked that his body lie on the ground three days as Imam al-Husayn's did" is **not** the documented request — what he asked was *"just pray that I do not go ahead of Imam Husayn."* The body did lie three days at Nahr Khayyin (and seven days in total before burial), so the detail is real; the *request* is not.
- **"If three go on a journey, let them appoint one an amir" is a Sunni hadith with no Imami primary source located.** Abu Dawud 2608–2609, graded *hasan sahih* by al-Albani. Deliberately checked for absence in the Imami *adab al-safar* material and not found. Correctly quoted, wrongly implied to be from our own corpus.
- **The video game revenue claim is true, and by a wide margin, on the obvious reading.** Global games market **$184.3bn** in 2024 (Newzoo) versus global theatrical box office **~$30bn** plus global recorded music **$29.6bn** (IFPI) — roughly a 3:1 ratio. It only fails if "movies" is stretched to include streaming and television (Netflix alone booked $39.0bn in 2024).

## Quranic verses

### 57:21 — racing for forgiveness

**Verse**
> سَابِقُوٓاْ إِلَىٰ مَغۡفِرَةٖ مِّن رَّبِّكُمۡ وَجَنَّةٍ عَرۡضُهَا كَعَرۡضِ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ أُعِدَّتۡ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ بِٱللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِۦۚ ذَٰلِكَ فَضۡلُ ٱللَّهِ يُؤۡتِيهِ مَن يَشَآءُۚ وَٱللَّهُ ذُو ٱلۡفَضۡلِ ٱلۡعَظِيمِ

**Translation** (Sahih International): "Race toward forgiveness from your Lord and a Garden whose width is like the width of the heavens and earth, prepared for those who believed in Allah and His messengers. That is the bounty of Allah which He gives to whom He wills, and Allah is the possessor of great bounty."

**Context** — Surah al-Hadid, a passage on spending in Allah's path, the transience of worldly adornment, and the warning against miserliness and boastfulness (57:20 immediately before, 57:22–24 after). The competition commanded is between believers and its object is forgiveness and the Garden.

**Tafsir al-Mizan** — Present and directly on point. Tabataba'i defines *al-musabaqa* as «المغالبة في السبق للوصول إلى غرض» (contending to outstrip one another in reaching an object), and distinguishes it from *musara'a* (hastening, as in 3:133): «المسابقة الجدّ في تسريع الحركة بحيث تزيد في السرعة على حركة صاحبه» — racing means driving your motion faster *than your companion's*. So al-Mizan explicitly reads a **comparative, other-referencing** competition into the verb, which is the reading the speaker needs.

**Other Shia scholarship** — Majma' al-Bayan reads it in the same register. The verse is the standard proof-text in Shia ethics for *tanafus* / competitive striving being licit when its object is licit.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Sound, and unusually well chosen.** His claim ("one of the verses where Allah encourages us to compete") is exactly what al-Mizan says the verb means, and the tafsir even supplies the distinction he was reaching for (competition, not just hurry). His inference — that the *drive* to compete is part of the human constitution because Allah addresses it — is a homiletic extension, not something al-Mizan states, but it is a reasonable one. He did not quote the Arabic and the caption garbles it; he gave the reference correctly.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified (verse, context, tafsir).

### 5:2 — cooperating in righteousness

**Verse** (the whole ayah; he quoted only the eleventh word onward)
> يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ لَا تُحِلُّواْ شَعَـٰٓئِرَ ٱللَّهِ وَلَا ٱلشَّهۡرَ ٱلۡحَرَامَ وَلَا ٱلۡهَدۡيَ وَلَا ٱلۡقَلَـٰٓئِدَ وَلَآ ءَآمِّينَ ٱلۡبَيۡتَ ٱلۡحَرَامَ يَبۡتَغُونَ فَضۡلٗا مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡ وَرِضۡوَٰنٗاۚ وَإِذَا حَلَلۡتُمۡ فَٱصۡطَادُواْۚ **وَلَا يَجۡرِمَنَّكُمۡ شَنَـَٔانُ قَوۡمٍ أَن صَدُّوكُمۡ عَنِ ٱلۡمَسۡجِدِ ٱلۡحَرَامِ أَن تَعۡتَدُواْ**ۘ **وَتَعَاوَنُواْ عَلَى ٱلۡبِرِّ وَٱلتَّقۡوَىٰۖ وَلَا تَعَاوَنُواْ عَلَى ٱلۡإِثۡمِ وَٱلۡعُدۡوَٰنِۚ** وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ شَدِيدُ ٱلۡعِقَابِ

**Translation** (Sahih International, the quoted portion): "…And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is severe in penalty."

**Context** — Madinan, on the sanctities of the Hajj and on conduct toward the Quraysh who had barred the Muslims from the Sacred Mosque at Hudaybiyya. The clause immediately before the one he quoted is *"do not let hatred of a people who barred you from the Sacred Mosque provoke you to transgress."* The cooperation command is therefore issued **against a background of legitimate grievance**, as a restraint on retaliation.

**Tafsir al-Mizan** — Present. Tabataba'i takes the sentence as foundational rather than needing elaboration ("the meaning is clear, and this is the foundation of Islamic practice"), glosses *al-birr* through the Qur'an's own definition — faith plus excellence in worship and dealings — and *al-taqwa* as «مراقبة أمر الله ونهيه». He reads *ta'awun 'ala al-birr* as **collective association upon faith and righteous action founded on taqwa**: cooperation is a social good only when its object is.

**Does the omitted second half change the sense?** — **Yes, materially, and it matters for the lecture's own thesis.** The verse is not a general endorsement of cooperation; it is a *bounded* one. Half of it is a prohibition: cooperation in sin and aggression is forbidden by the same breath that commands cooperation in righteousness. And the discarded clause before it makes the whole a lesson in *not* letting group solidarity slide into group enmity. For a talk whose point is "teach children to be team players," the unquoted half is arguably the more important half — a child who learns to cooperate without learning the limit on cooperation has learned the peer-pressure half of the lesson only. The speaker's own material later in the lecture (the child who repeats a bad word learned from a playmate) is precisely the case the second half governs.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Correct but truncated, and the truncation costs him.** He said "one part of it is this" and did quote only one part, which is honest labelling. But he presents 5:2 as evidence that "we have a need to cooperate" — the verse commands cooperation, it does not describe a need — and he omits the clause that would have let him make the sharper parenting point.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified.

### 30:41 — corruption from human hands

**Verse**
> ظَهَرَ ٱلۡفَسَادُ فِي ٱلۡبَرِّ وَٱلۡبَحۡرِ بِمَا كَسَبَتۡ أَيۡدِي ٱلنَّاسِ لِيُذِيقَهُم بَعۡضَ ٱلَّذِي عَمِلُواْ لَعَلَّهُمۡ يَرۡجِعُونَ

**Translation** (Sahih International): "Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned so He may let them taste part of [the consequence of] what they have done that perhaps they will return [to righteousness]."

**Context** — Surah al-Rum, in a sequence on divine signs, the fitrah (30:30), and the fate of earlier peoples.

**Tafsir al-Mizan** — Present, and it supports the speaker's reading more strongly than most tafsirs. Tabataba'i takes *al-barr wa'l-bahr* in «معناهما المعروف» — their ordinary meaning, covering the whole surface of the earth — and rejects the narrower allegorical readings. He glosses *al-fasad* as «كل ما يفسد النظام الصالح الجاري في العالم الأرضي» (whatever corrupts the sound order running in the terrestrial world), and states the principle the speaker is after: «بين أعمال الناس والحوادث الكونية رابطة» — **there is a real link between human deeds and cosmic events**. On *li-yudhiqahum ba'da alladhi 'amilu* he notes it is only **part** of the consequence, mercy withholding the rest.

**Other Shia scholarship** — Makarim Shirazi's *Tafsir-e Nemuneh* is the tafsir most often cited for the environmental reading; classical exegetes divide over whether *fasad* is chiefly moral or chiefly physical, and al-Mizan's position is that it is whatever breaks the world's sound order, which comfortably covers both.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Sound.** He used it to say "my physical actions have a negative effect on the universe," which is very close to al-Mizan's own formulation. Worth telling a child: the verse says *ba'da* — you are shown only a fraction of the consequence.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified.

### 7:96 — blessings on the towns

**Verse**
> وَلَوۡ أَنَّ أَهۡلَ ٱلۡقُرَىٰٓ ءَامَنُواْ وَٱتَّقَوۡاْ لَفَتَحۡنَا عَلَيۡهِم بَرَكَـٰتٖ مِّنَ ٱلسَّمَآءِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَلَـٰكِن كَذَّبُواْ فَأَخَذۡنَـٰهُم بِمَا كَانُواْ يَكۡسِبُونَ

**Translation** (Sahih International): "And if only the people of the cities had believed and feared Allah, We would have opened upon them blessings from the heaven and the earth; but they denied [the messengers], so We seized them for what they were earning."

**Context** — Surah al-A'raf, closing the cycle of destroyed nations (Nuh, Hud, Salih, Lut, Shu'ayb). The verse is a *counterfactual about communities that were destroyed*, not a promise to individuals.

**Tafsir al-Mizan** — Present. *Barakat* = «أنواع الخير الكثير», the kinds of abundant good — material and non-material: security, ease, health, wealth, offspring. Tabataba'i explains the mechanism the same way he explained 30:41 in reverse: human deeds are interwoven with the created order, so when a population is upright God «فتحها… فجرى عليهم منها بركات السماء» — rain and atmospheric goods — and the blessings of the earth, vegetation and security; when it corrupts, that channel closes.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Sound in substance, loose in one respect.** He used 30:41 and 7:96 as the negative and positive halves of one principle — exactly how al-Mizan reads them. The looseness is that both verses address **communities** (*ahl al-qura*, *al-nas*), not the individual child's "footprint on the universe"; the individualising move is homiletic. Also note *lam yu'minu* is counterfactual: the verse's actual subject is towns that were destroyed. Neither point damages his use, but a parent quoting it to a child should know the verse is about a town, not a boy.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified.

### 35:14 — none informs you like the All-Aware

**Verse**
> إِن تَدۡعُوهُمۡ لَا يَسۡمَعُواْ دُعَآءَكُمۡ وَلَوۡ سَمِعُواْ مَا ٱسۡتَجَابُواْ لَكُمۡۖ وَيَوۡمَ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ يَكۡفُرُونَ بِشِرۡكِكُمۡۚ وَلَا يُنَبِّئُكَ مِثۡلُ خَبِيرٖ

**Translation** (Sahih International): "If you invoke them, they do not hear your supplication; and if they heard, they would not respond to you. And on the Day of Resurrection they will deny your association. And none can inform you like [one] Acquainted [with all matters]."

**Context** — Surah Fatir 35:13–14. The subject is the **idols the mushrikun invoke**, which "do not possess so much as the membrane of a date-stone." The concluding clause is the seal on an argument about the futility of shirk.

**Tafsir al-Mizan** — Present. Tabataba'i: «لا يخبرك عن حقيقة الأمر مخبر مثل مخبر خبير» — no informer reports the reality of the matter like an informer who is *khabir*. The *khabir* is **God**; the thing being reported is the reality of shirk; and the address is to the Prophet, after turning away from those who lack understanding. There is no reading in al-Mizan of this clause as a general epistemic maxim about human expertise.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Decontextualised.** He cited it as "nobody can inform you like the person who is aware" and immediately used it to introduce what "parents don't know" about the games industry — i.e. as a warrant for deferring to those in the know. The clause does not say that. It says that no informant equals *God* as an informant, and it is saying so about idolatry. The general principle he wants (act on the testimony of one who actually knows) is defensible from the Qur'an in other places; **35:14 is not the verse for it**, and it is the one verse in this lecture where he is bending the text to the sermon rather than the other way round.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified (that the verse says what it says and that al-Mizan reads it as above); high confidence that the speaker's use is not supported.

## Narrations

### N1. The "silent imam" — can there be two imams at once?

**Arabic**
> عِدَّةٌ مِنْ أَصْحَابِنَا عَنْ أَحْمَدَ بْنِ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ عِيسَى عَنْ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ أَبِي عُمَيْرٍ عَنِ الْحُسَيْنِ بْنِ أَبِي الْعَلَاءِ قَالَ: قُلْتُ لِأَبِي عَبْدِ اللهِ (عليه السلام): تَكُونُ الْأَرْضُ لَيْسَ فِيهَا إِمَامٌ؟ قَالَ: لَا. قُلْتُ: يَكُونُ إِمَامَانِ؟ قَالَ: **لَا، إِلَّا وَأَحَدُهُمَا صَامِتٌ.**

**Translation** — "I said to Abu 'Abdillah (a): 'Can the earth be without an Imam in it?' He said, 'No.' I said, 'Can there be two Imams?' He said, **'No — unless one of the two is silent.'**"

**Reference** — al-Kafi, vol. 1, *Kitab al-Hujja*, **bab anna al-arda la takhlu min hujja** ("that the earth is at no time without a person of divine authority"), **hadith 1**. (Thaqalayn: Book 1 / Part 4 / Chapter 5 / Hadith 1.)

**Chain** — *'Iddatun min ashabina* ← **Ahmad b. Muhammad b. 'Isa** ← **Muhammad b. Abi 'Umayr** (one of the *ashab al-ijma'*) ← **al-Husayn b. Abi al-'Ala' al-Khaffaf** ← Imam al-Sadiq.

**Authenticity** — **al-Majlisi (*Mir'at al-'Uqul*): *hasan*. Behbudi (*Sahih al-Kafi*): *sahih*** — i.e. Behbudi retained it, which he does not do lightly. This is one of the two or three best-attested narrations cited anywhere in this series.

**The Arabic term** — Yes, ***samit*** (صامت), "silent," is the word, exactly as the speaker gave it. The correlative term in the tradition is *natiq* (ناطق), "speaking."

**Is the al-Hasan / al-Husayn identification in the text?** — **No** — and this is where the speaker turns out to be *better* sourced than he sounded. The hadith itself names nobody. But **al-Majlisi's own commentary supplies exactly the identification the speaker gave**:

> *Mir'at al-'Uqul fi Sharh Akhbar Al al-Rasul*, **vol. 2, p. 294**: «"إلا وأحدهما صامت" أي ساكت عن الدعوة والتعريف وادّعاء الإمامة، والناطق إمامٌ عليه في الحال **كالسبطين عليهما السلام**.»
> — "'unless one of the two is silent': i.e. silent as regards *da'wah*, making himself known, and claiming the imamate; and the speaking one is imam over him at that time — **as with the two grandsons (al-Hasan and al-Husayn), peace be upon them**."

So the speaker's gloss is not his invention; it is the standard Imami commentary on this very hadith.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified: text, reference, two named gradings, and the commentary that supplies the al-Hasan/al-Husayn identification.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Substantially correct, with one overreach.** He was right that the narration exists, right that the word is *samit*, and right that the Imams intended are al-Hasan and al-Husayn — though he should have said "as al-Majlisi explains" rather than presenting it as the Imam's own words.

The overreach is the **"good soldier / followership is a virtue" reading.** Al-Majlisi's *samit* is silent about **three specific things** — summoning people, making his imamate known, and claiming the office. That is a doctrine about the non-duplication of manifest authority, not a doctrine about teamwork, subordination or learning to take orders. Imam al-Husayn's "silence" during al-Hasan's imamate was not the deference of a junior officer; it was the withholding of a claim he already possessed by divine appointment. Using it as a model for a child learning to let another kid be captain is an **analogy the tradition does not draw** — attractive, and not objectionable, but it should be labelled as the speaker's own image rather than as what the hadith teaches. A parent repeating it should say "there is a beautiful analogy here" and not "the Imam said followership is a virtue."

**Practical meaning** — What the narration actually establishes: the earth is never without a *hujja*, and where two are contemporaneous only one exercises manifest authority. What it can *legitimately* be borrowed for: that not-leading is not a defect, since the greatest of the Ahl al-Bayt spent years not leading. That much is safe.

### N2. "If three go on a journey, let them appoint one an amir"

**Arabic**
> «إِذَا خَرَجَ ثَلَاثَةٌ فِي سَفَرٍ فَلْيُؤَمِّرُوا أَحَدَهُمْ»

**Translation** — "When three set out on a journey, let them appoint one of them as their commander."

**Reference** — **Sunan Abi Dawud 2608** (Abu Sa'id al-Khudri; Book 15, hadith 132), with the companion narration **2609** from Abu Hurayra. Chain of 2608: 'Ali b. Bahr b. Barri ← Hatim b. Isma'il ← Muhammad b. 'Ajlan ← Nafi' ← Abu Salama ← Abu Sa'id al-Khudri. The report closes with Nafi' saying to Abu Salama, "then you are our amir."

**Chain / grading** — **Hasan sahih** (al-Albani); *hasan* in the Encyclopaedia of Hadith rating. Al-Khattabi's classical gloss: the point is that their affair be unified and their counsel not fragment.

**Shia source?** — **NOT LOCATED.** Deliberately searched for as an Imami narration — in al-Kafi, *Man la yahduruh al-Faqih*, and the *abwab adab al-safar* material — and not found with this wording or an equivalent. The Imami travel-etiquette chapters carry a great deal (companionship, kindness to the companion, provisioning, service to the group) but I could not pin a *ta'mir* narration to a primary Imami collection.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified as a **Sunni** hadith; the absence of an Imami parallel is a moderate-confidence negative (absence of proof after a targeted search, not proof of absence).

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Correctly quoted, and he was right to say it is "from the Prophet."** The gap is that he presented it in a Shia lecture without noting that, as far as can be established, it comes to us through the Sunni collections. That is not a fault of substance — Shia scholars cite it — but a parent who repeats it to a Shia audience and is asked "where is that from?" should be able to say "Abu Dawud, *hasan sahih*," not "one of our hadiths."

**Practical meaning** — The teaching is real and the speaker's application (children choosing a leader before starting a game) is a graceful one. Worth adding what al-Khattabi adds: the amir decides *after consulting them* — which is exactly the *shura*-then-leadership sequence the speaker described in his own case study, so his story is closer to the classical commentary than his citation was.

### N3. The wrestling narration — the Prophet cheers al-Hasan, Jibra'il cheers al-Husayn

**Arabic** (as transmitted)
> رُوِيَ عَنْ جَعْفَرِ بْنِ مُحَمَّدٍ الصَّادِقِ (عليه السلام) قَالَ: اصْطَرَعَ الْحَسَنُ وَالْحُسَيْنُ (عليهما السلام) بَيْنَ يَدَيْ رَسُولِ اللهِ (صلى الله عليه وآله)، فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ: «إِيهاً حَسَنُ!» فَقَالَتْ فَاطِمَةُ (عليها السلام): يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، إِنَّ الْحُسَيْنَ لَأَصْغَرُ مِنْهُ وَأَضْعَفُ رُكْناً مِنْهُ. فَقَالَ لَهَا رَسُولُ اللهِ: **«أَلَا تَرْضَيْنَ أَنْ أَقُولَ أَنَا: هِيْ يَا حَسَنُ، وَيَقُولَ جِبْرَئِيلُ: هِيْ يَا حُسَيْنُ؟»**

**Translation** — "al-Hasan and al-Husayn wrestled before the Messenger of Allah, and he said, 'Come on, Hasan!' Fatima said, 'Messenger of Allah, al-Husayn is the younger of the two and the weaker of frame.' He said to her: **'Are you not content that I should say "Go on, Hasan!" while Jibra'il says "Go on, Husayn!"?'**"

**Reference** — **Ibn al-Sabbagh al-Maliki (d. 855 AH), *al-Fusul al-Muhimma fi Ma'rifat al-A'imma*, vol. 2, p. 757**, from Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq. A parallel with a different frame — the narrator, not Fatima, objecting, and the Prophet answering "this is Jibra'il standing and saying *wayhan Hasan, khudh Husayn*" — is transmitted from **Ibn Shahin** and circulates through the *manaqib* literature; it is also carried in the later Shia compendia in the al-Hasan/al-Husayn *fada'il* chapters (Bihar al-Anwar, vol. 43).

**Chain** — **None.** *al-Fusul al-Muhimma* is a 9th/15th-century *manaqib* compilation and reports this as *ruwiya 'an* — "it is related from" — with no isnad. The Ibn Shahin route is likewise transmitted as an *akhbar* item.

**Authenticity** — **Ungradeable.** No rijal assessment exists because there is no chain to assess. This belongs to the same evidentiary class as *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* and *Makarim al-Akhlaq*: valuable devotional material, not probative.

**Confidence** — **Moderately verified.** The narration exists, in more than one recension, and the Fatima-objects version with the Jibra'il answer is genuinely attested in the compilation literature — the speaker did not invent it. But it cannot be traced to any early collection and carries no grading.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Fair, with two small drifts.** (1) He framed it as "we learn from the time of Rasulullah, Imam al-Sadiq telling us" — the attribution to al-Sadiq is right, but the source is a late compilation, not one of the Four Books, and that should be said. (2) In the located text Fatima's objection is that **al-Husayn is younger and weaker**, i.e. an objection about *fairness to the smaller child*; the speaker rendered it as her finding it "kind of strange" that the Prophet was taking sides. The documented objection is the better one for his purposes: it is precisely a mother worrying that the smaller child is outmatched, and the answer is that the smaller child has his own champion.

**Practical meaning** — For the point the speaker was making — that a watched, cheered-on game is a different thing from an unwatched one — this narration is apt and the classical version is stronger than his paraphrase. Cite it as "related in *al-Fusul al-Muhimma* from Imam al-Sadiq," not as "a hadith."

### N4. Imam al-Sadiq haggling at Mina, and *al-maghbun la mahmud wa la ma'jur*

**Arabic** (the story)
> سَوَادَةُ: كُنَّا جَمَاعَةً بِمِنًى **فَعَزَّتِ الْأَضَاحِي**، فَنَظَرْنَا فَإِذَا أَبُو عَبْدِ اللهِ (عليه السلام) وَاقِفٌ عَلَى قَطِيعٍ يُسَاوِمُ بِغَنَمٍ **وَيُمَاكِسُهُمْ مِكَاساً شَدِيداً**، فَوَقَفْنَا نَنْتَظِرُ، فَلَمَّا فَرَغَ أَقْبَلَ عَلَيْنَا فَقَالَ: «أَظُنُّكُمْ قَدْ تَعَجَّبْتُمْ مِنْ مِكَاسِي؟» فَقُلْنَا: نَعَمْ. فَقَالَ: **«إِنَّ الْمَغْبُونَ لَا مَحْمُودٌ وَلَا مَأْجُورٌ.»**

**Translation** — "Sawada said: we were a group at Mina and **sacrificial animals had become scarce/dear**. We looked, and there was Abu 'Abdillah (a) standing over a flock, bargaining over sheep and **haggling with them very hard**. We stood waiting; when he finished he turned to us and said, 'I think you found my haggling surprising?' We said, 'Yes.' He said: **'The one who is cheated is neither praised nor rewarded.'**"

**Reference** — **al-Kafi, vol. 4, p. 496, hadith 3**; and **Tahdhib al-Ahkam, vol. 5, p. 209, hadith 702** (both in the *hady*/*adahi* material of *Kitab al-Hajj*). Collected as item 598 in Muhammad al-Rayshahri, *al-Tanmiya al-Iqtisadiyya fi'l-Kitab wa'l-Sunna*, pp. 204–206, in the chapter *al-Mumakasa 'inda makhafat al-ghabn*.

**Chain / authenticity** — Narrator **Sawada**. **No published grading retrieved** — al-Majlisi's *Mir'at al-'Uqul* treatment of this hadith could not be reached in this session. Note in its favour that the report is **attested in two of the Four Books independently** (al-Kafi and *al-Tahdhib*), which is itself a mark of currency in the early corpus.

**The maxim, separately** — «الْمَغْبُونُ لَا مَحْمُودٌ وَلَا مَأْجُورٌ» is not, in the primary sources, an original saying of Imam al-Sadiq. It is transmitted as a **prophetic** hadith which the Imams quote:
- **al-Saduq, *al-Khisal* 621/10** — from Abu Basir and Muhammad b. Muslim, from Imam al-Sadiq from his forefathers, from the Prophet.
- **al-Saduq, *'Uyun akhbar al-Rida* 2:48, no. 184** — from Dawud b. Sulayman, from Imam al-Rida from his forefathers, from the Prophet.
- ***Tuhaf al-'Uqul* p. 111** — attributed to **Imam Ali**, and with the variant «مُحَاوَرٌ» in place of «مَأْجُورٌ». (Chainless; see the index's standing note on *Tuhaf*.)
- Imam al-Baqir's fuller version, **Man la yahduruh al-Faqih 3:197, no. 3742**: «مَاكِسِ الْمُشْتَرِيَ، فَإِنَّهُ أَطْيَبُ لِلنَّفْسِ وَإِنْ أَعْطَى الْجَزِيلَ؛ فَإِنَّ الْمَغْبُونَ فِي بَيْعِهِ وَشِرَائِهِ غَيْرُ مَحْمُودٍ وَلَا مَأْجُورٍ» — "Haggle with the buyer, for that is more wholesome to the soul even if he then gives generously."
- Imam Ali, *Rabi' al-Abrar* 4:139: «مَاكِسْ عَنْ دِرْهَمَيْكَ، فَإِنَّ الْمَغْبُونَ لَا مَحْمُودٌ وَلَا مَأْجُورٌ.»
- **Sunni corpus:** al-Tabarani, *al-Mu'jam al-Kabir* 3/83/2732 (from Imam al-Hasan, from his father, from the Prophet); Musnad Abi Ya'la 6/182/6750 (Abu Hisham al-Qannad from al-Husayn b. 'Ali, *marfu'*); *Tarikh Baghdad* 4/212/1901. Discussed by al-Sakhawi in *al-Maqasid al-Hasana* p. 179 among the widely-circulating hadiths.

**A better frame story than the one he used** — the classical *mumakasa* material has a second, more pointed episode, in **Tarikh Baghdad 4/180/1863** and **Ibn 'Asakir, *Tarikh Dimashq* 13/245 & 14/112**: Abu Hisham al-Qannad used to bring goods from Basra to Imam al-Hasan (or al-Husayn), who would **haggle him down hard and then give most of it away before he stood up**. Asked why, the Imam quoted this same prophetic hadith. That version answers the obvious objection — that haggling is grasping — better than the Mina story does. There is also a third, at **al-Kafi 4:546, h. 30**: Abu Hanifa said to Imam al-Sadiq, "people marvelled at you yesterday at 'Arafa, haggling so hard over your sacrificial camel," and the Imam replied, "and is it pleasing to Allah that I be cheated in my own property?" — Abu Hanifa conceded the point.

**Confidence** — **Strongly verified** for the story (double attestation, precise references) and for the maxim (multiple named primary sources, both corpora). **Needs further verification** only on the isnad grading of the al-Kafi report.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Good, better than most of the series, with two corrections.**
1. **"The businessmen had artificially raised the price of the sheep" is not in the text.** The Arabic says *fa-'azzat al-adahi* — the sacrificial animals became scarce and therefore dear. Nothing about price-gouging. His moral framing (the Imam pushing back against exploitation) is his own; the plainer reading is the Imam refusing to overpay in a tight market, full stop, which is the harder and more interesting lesson.
2. **The maxim is the Prophet's, quoted by the Imam.** Saying "the Imam says this" is not wrong, but "the Imam quoted the Prophet's words" is right, and it strengthens the point.

**Practical meaning** — The lecture's use — that shelter from ordinary transactions produces adults who cannot handle themselves — is well served by this narration. Note the fuller Baqir version adds the crucial qualifier: bargain hard, *and then be generous*. Haggling is about not being taken; it is not about keeping the money. A parent teaching this to a child should teach both halves, as the Imams did.

### "The Leader" made a comment on the martyr's mother's story

**What he said** — "This story about Shahid Muhammad Marian is a story that's available on the internet. The leader himself wrote a story about the — not wrote, but there was a story about the mother of this Shahid and the leader made a comment on that story."

**Finding — VERIFIED, and it is the first Khamenei attribution in this series that fully checks out apart from the book-fair remark already in the index.**

The book narrates the life of **Ashraf-Sadat Montazeri**, mother of Shahid Mohammad Memarian, from the pre-revolutionary struggle through the war and after.

The *taqriz* was unveiled on **21 November 2021**, at the **11th "Pasdasht-e Adabiyat-e Jihad va Muqavamat"** ceremony held at Talar-e Vahdat during Book Week, coinciding with the national ceremony honouring mothers of martyrs. It is published on **farsi.khamenei.ir** (both a message-content page and a video item) and reported by IRNA, ISNA, Mehr, Mashregh, Hawzah News and Young Journalists Club.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified (primary site plus multiple independent outlets, with a date and an occasion).

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Correct.** His hedging ("wrote a story… not wrote, but there was a story… and the leader made a comment on that story") is a fair, if fumbling, description of exactly what a *taqriz* is. Worth recording in the index as a counterweight to the three failed Khamenei attributions already logged: **this speaker's Khamenei attributions are not uniformly unreliable, and this one is checkable and correct.**

### "Gulpani" / "fatherani" — the ayatollah who authenticated the cloth

**Reconstruction — Grand Ayatollah Sayyid Muhammad-Rida Golpayegani (1899–1993)**, the senior *marja'* resident in Qom at the time of the events (Muharram 1368 sh / August 1989). The Memarian family lived in Qom, which makes him the natural referent, and he is named explicitly in the mother's own published accounts. The caption's second garble ("he sends fatherani") is most likely the speaker naming him and then referring to **his son** — the transcript reads "they ask Gulpani… He sends fatherani at that time and his son to go investigate" — and Ayatollah Golpayegani's household is the likely intended sense. (Ayatollah **Safi** Golpayegani is a different scholar and should not be confused with him.)

**What the sources actually document**
- The family **took the shawl to Golpayegani's house for authentication**: «برای تائید شال به منزل آقای گلپایگانی رفتند» — "they went to Mr Golpayegani's house for the shawl to be verified." Per the mother's account, before she could speak he said something to the effect that whoever had taken a piece of the shawl from her had had it taken from them by God.
- **He gave her some of the *turbah* of Imam al-Husayn** and instructed her on its use: «آیت الله گلپایگانی کمی از تربت امام حسین (ع) را به من داد و گفت یک سانت از این شال را برش بزنید و در داخل بطری به همراه آب و تربت قرار دهید» — "Ayatollah Golpayegani gave me a little of the turbah of Imam Husayn and said: cut one centimetre from this shawl and put it in a bottle together with water and the turbah." She has distributed that water ever since.
- The **scent** is a documented element: the shawl is kept in a small glass vessel and "its particular perfume remains."

**What is NOT documented in the sources retrieved**
- The specific scene the speaker described — **the ayatollah smelling the cloth, pressing it to his face and heart, calling for his own turbah "from under the body of Imam al-Husayn himself, handed down to him," and declaring "these two smell the same, they came from the same place."** That episode does not appear in the mother's published interviews (Defa Press, IRNA, Fars, Navid-e Shahed) or in the biographical write-ups. What those sources have is the authentication visit, the turbah, and the instruction.
- One published version has the family, before Golpayegani, acting **"on the advice of one of the ulama"** (unnamed) in placing part of the shawl in water — so even the identification of Golpayegani as the authenticating scholar is stated in some tellings and left anonymous in others.

**Confidence** — **Strongly verified** that Ayatollah Golpayegani is the correct name and that an authentication visit and a turbah instruction occurred. **Needs further verification** for the smelling-and-matching scene; it is either from a fuller oral telling (the mother speaks frequently at gatherings and the details vary between tellings) or an embellishment picked up in transmission. Treat it as **unattributed** until located.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **The name is right and the substance is right; the scene is not sourced.** A parent retelling this should say "she took the shawl to Ayatollah Golpayegani, who gave her turbah of Imam al-Husayn and told her what to do with it," and stop there.

## Empirical and factual claims

### E1. "The video game industry generates more money than movies and music combined"

**Verdict — TRUE on the natural reading, and by a factor of roughly three. It fails only under a stretched definition of "movies."**

| Sector | Latest verified figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Global games market | **$184.3bn (2024)**; $188.8bn forecast for 2025 | Newzoo, *Global Games Market Report 2025* (9 Sep 2025) |
| Global theatrical box office | **~$30bn (2024)** (Gower Street's pre-year projection was $32.3bn; the year finished near $30bn) | Gower Street Analytics / Comscore |
| Global recorded music (trade revenues) | **$29.6bn (2024)**, +4.8%, tenth consecutive year of growth | IFPI, *Global Music Report 2025* (19 Mar 2025) |

Movies + music on these measures ≈ **$60bn**, against $184bn for games — the claim clears by about 3:1, and has done for roughly a decade.

**The qualification that matters** — "the movie industry" can mean box office, or it can mean the whole film-and-television business including streaming subscriptions and home entertainment. On the broader definition the comparison narrows sharply or reverses: **Netflix alone booked $39.0bn in FY2024 revenue** (up 16% from $33.7bn) — more than the entire world's cinema takings. Nobody serious disputes that games out-earn *cinema plus recorded music*; the widely repeated stronger form ("bigger than movies and music *industries* combined") depends on where you draw the line.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified with named sources and years.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **He is right, and this is the strongest empirical claim in the lecture.** He also drew the correct inference from it — that the asymmetry is about *engagement hours*, not ticket price: "how many times can I really watch a movie… how many hours are consumed by me playing the video game." That is in fact the mechanism behind the revenue gap (recurring in-game monetisation and live-service retention), so his reasoning matches the economics.

### E2. The NBA playing without fans during COVID

**Verdict — TRUE, precisely as described.**

The NBA suspended the 2019–20 season on **11 March 2020** after Rudy Gobert tested positive. Play resumed **30 July 2020** inside the **"NBA Bubble"** — a bio-secure campus at the ESPN Wide World of Sports Complex, Walt Disney World, Bay Lake, Florida — for the final eight regular-season games (22 invited teams) and the whole playoffs, concluding 11 October 2020. **No spectators were admitted.** The league substituted **virtual fans on 17-foot video boards** (a Microsoft Teams "Together Mode" partnership, with Michelob Ultra) and piped-in crowd audio. The following season opened 22 December 2020 with arenas empty or at heavily limited capacity.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified (NBA.com, CNN, the standard reference accounts).

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Correct and well chosen.** "How flat was that" is precisely the widely reported player and broadcast experience, and it makes his point (performance changes when nobody is watching) with a real example. No correction needed.

### E3. Exposure "awakens the sexual faculty early while feeding imagination over intellect"

This is two different claims wearing one sentence, and they need separating.

**(a) The faculty framework (*'aql* / *khayal*) is classical, not invented.** The tripartite scheme he leans on — a rational faculty (*al-quwwa al-'aqliyya*), an imaginative faculty (*al-quwwa al-khayaliyya* / *al-mutakhayyila*), and an appetitive-sexual faculty (*al-quwwa al-shahwiyya*) — is standard Islamic psychology, running from Ibn Sina through al-Ghazali to Mulla Sadra, and it is the framework in which classical Muslim educators discussed child development. **The framework is legitimately his to use, and he should say so:** it is a normative anthropology, not an empirical finding, and it neither needs nor receives support from developmental psychology. Presenting it as though it were a scientific model — which the lecture edges toward, with the promised "presentation on the scientific side" — is the error to avoid.

**(b) The empirical claim, assessed.** Three distinct propositions are being run together:

1. **"Media exposure makes puberty arrive earlier."** — **NOT SUPPORTED.** Puberty *has* been arriving earlier: Eckert-Lind et al., *"Worldwide Secular Trends in Age at Pubertal Onset Assessed by Breast Development Among Girls: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis,"* **JAMA Pediatrics 2020;174(4):e195881** (published 10 Feb 2020), documents a consistent secular decline in age at thelarche across five decades. But the drivers identified in that literature are **nutrition, adiposity/BMI, and possibly endocrine-disrupting chemicals** — not screen content. No credible body of evidence links media exposure to the *biological* timing of puberty.
2. **"Media exposure is associated with earlier sexual interest and behaviour."** — **PARTLY SUPPORTED, with heavy caveats.** The classic findings are Collins et al., *"Watching Sex on Television Predicts Adolescent Initiation of Sexual Behavior,"* Pediatrics 2004, and Brown et al., *"Sexy Media Matter,"* Pediatrics 2006 — both longitudinal, both reporting associations. Both are also observational, confounded by selection (adolescents already interested seek such content) and by family and peer variables, and the effect sizes in later meta-analytic work are modest. The direction of the speaker's claim survives; the strength and the causal language do not.
3. **"Screens build imagination at the expense of intellect."** — **PARTLY SUPPORTED, under a different description.** The nearest solid experimental finding is Lillard & Peterson, *"The Immediate Impact of Different Types of Television on Young Children's Executive Function,"* **Pediatrics 2011;128(4):644–649**: **nine minutes** of a fast-paced fantastical cartoon measurably impaired four-year-olds' executive function immediately afterward, relative to a slow-paced educational programme and to drawing. The proposed mechanism — fast-paced fantastical content captures attention bottom-up, taxing prefrontal resources — is a recognisable cousin of the *khayal*-crowding-*'aql* picture. But note what the study is: an *immediate*, short-lived effect from one genre, not evidence that a childhood of screens permanently swaps intellect for imagination.

**On the addiction framing** — the speaker's strongest empirical ground, and he understates it. **Gaming disorder is a recognised diagnosis**: adopted into **ICD-11** by the World Health Assembly in **2019**, in force from **January 2022**; DSM-5 lists "Internet Gaming Disorder" as a condition for further study. Prevalence is far lower than the lecture implies: Stevens et al., *"Global prevalence of gaming disorder: A systematic review and meta-analysis,"* **Australian & New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry** (online 7 Oct 2020), gives a pooled worldwide prevalence around **3%**, dropping to roughly **2%** in the better-sampled studies. So: a real, classified disorder affecting a small minority — not a near-certainty for any child given a console.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Directionally defensible, rhetorically overdrawn, and analytically conflated.** The "crack cocaine" comparison is a rhetorical move, not a claim about the literature, and a parent should hear it as such. What is genuinely supportable: gaming disorder exists and is classified; fast-paced fantastical content has measurable short-term costs to young children's self-regulation; sexual media content is associated with earlier sexual initiation. What is not: that screens shift the age of biological puberty, or that the classical faculty model has empirical backing.

**Confidence** — Strongly verified for the named studies; the assessment of the speaker's composite claim is a judgement, offered as such.

### E4. The age-14 benchmark and the encouragement of early marriage

**"By the time your kids are 14, when their Karbala is presented they can follow their imam."**
- **This is homiletic, not a fiqh or hadith benchmark.** The implied referent is the youth of Karbala — al-Qasim b. al-Hasan (traditionally about thirteen), 'Abdullah b. al-Hasan, 'Awn and Muhammad — and as an aspiration it is unobjectionable. But **14 is not a marker anywhere in Imami law**: the age of *taklif* in Shia fiqh is **fifteen complete lunar years for boys** and nine for girls, and the seven-year developmental scheme the whole series is built on (al-Kafi 6:47, h. 3 — see index) divides at 7, 14 and 21, with the *second* seven ending at fourteen, not culminating in a test at it.
- **Verdict:** an inspiring frame presented in the grammar of a fact. Treat as the speaker's pastoral goal-setting.

**"We are then encouraging early marriage for children… don't drink the Kool-Aid, don't believe 'wait till the kid's 21.'"**
- **This is normative teaching, and it is a real position in the tradition** — the *istihbab* of hastening marriage is well attested (the prophetic "when one whose religion and character please you proposes, marry him to her" family; the general disapproval of protracted celibacy). He also hedged it correctly: *"when every child is ready — that's their time to marry."*
- **What a parent should hold alongside it:** (i) readiness in the fiqh sense is not the same as readiness in the practical sense, and marjas differ on what a guardian may and may not do; (ii) legal minimum marriage ages in the countries most of this audience lives in are binding regardless; (iii) nothing in the tradition sets 21 as wrong or any earlier age as right — the "Kool-Aid" is a rhetorical foil, not a documented position he is refuting.
- **Verdict:** normative advocacy, clearly his own, and he presented it as such. No source is claimed and none is needed; the audit note is simply that this is a value claim and not a finding.

### E5. "Small fights between children are healthy"

**Verdict — SUPPORTED by the developmental literature, and this is a claim he could have made more strongly than he did.**

- **Shantz, C. U., "Conflicts Between Children," *Child Development* 1987;58(2):283–305** — the foundational review. Its central move is exactly the speaker's: it **distinguishes conflict from aggression**, treats conflict as a normative and frequent feature of children's natural social settings, and situates it as a developmental engine in Piagetian and social-learning theory rather than as pathology.
- **Laursen, Finkelstein & Betts, "A developmental meta-analysis of peer conflict resolution," *Developmental Review* 2001;21:423–449** — resolution strategies shift with age from coercion toward negotiation and disengagement, i.e. **conflict is the occasion on which negotiation is learned**. A child with no conflicts has had no practice.
- **Hartup, W. W., "The company they keep: friendships and their developmental significance," *Child Development* 1996;67:1–13** — conflict within friendships is resolved differently from conflict with non-friends, and friendship provides the safe context for that learning.
- **Pellegrini & Smith, "Physical activity play: the nature and function of a neglected aspect of play," *Child Development* 1998;69:577–598** — rough-and-tumble play, which adults routinely misread as fighting, has its own developmental functions.
- **The essential boundary he did not draw:** this literature is about *symmetric peer conflict*, not about bullying. Bullying is defined by repetition and power imbalance (Olweus), and the finding that conflict is developmentally useful does **not** extend to it. A parent applying "small fights are healthy" needs that line, and the lecture does not supply it.

**Verdict on the speaker's use** — **Correct, and better founded than he claimed.** He offered it as reassurance ("we shouldn't get panicked or paranoid"); it is in fact a well-established position in developmental psychology with forty years of literature behind it. The missing piece is the bullying boundary.

### E6. "I'm 51 years old"

Not verifiable from public sources and not a claim requiring verification — recorded here for **dating purposes only**. If accurate at recording, it places his birth around **1973–75**. Consistent with his reference point ("I remember those old video games"), which would put his own childhood gaming in the early-to-mid 1980s arcade and Atari/NES era. Useful mainly as a rough date-stamp for the series if the upload dates are ever needed.

## Better-sourced substitutes

- **For "learning to follow is a virtue"** — do not build it on the *samit* hadith, which is about manifest authority. Better: **Nahj al-Balagha, Letter 47** ("*bi-taqwa Allah wa nazmi amrikum wa salahi dhati baynikum*" — three things, and speakers routinely drop the third, which is *setting right what is between you*: index entry). That text actually is about ordering a common life. Or **Nahj al-Balagha, Khutba 216**, on the reciprocal rights of leader and led.
- **For the cooperation theme** — quote **all of 5:2**, not half. The prohibition half and the anti-vengeance clause are what make it a parenting verse rather than a slogan.
- **For "one who is cheated is neither praised nor rewarded"** — cite it as the **Prophet's** hadith (al-Khisal 621/10; *'Uyun* 2:48/184) and prefer **Imam al-Baqir's fuller version, Man la yahduruh al-Faqih 3:197 no. 3742**, which adds *"and then give generously"* — the half that stops the teaching curdling into acquisitiveness.
- **For "children need exposure to imperfect situations"** — the **Abu Hisham al-Qannad** episode (Tarikh Baghdad 4/180/1863; Ibn 'Asakir, *Tarikh Dimashq* 13/245) is a better story than the Mina one: the Imam haggles his supplier down hard and then gives most of it back, and quotes the hadith when challenged. It teaches shrewdness and generosity in a single image. The **'Arafa exchange with Abu Hanifa (al-Kafi 4:546, h. 30)** is a third, with the Imam's own one-line justification.
- **For the wrestling story's point** (a watched game is a different game) — the narration is fine but chainless; where a *sahih* text is wanted for parental attentiveness, use **Sunan al-Nasa'i 1141** (*sahih*), the Prophet prolonging his *sujud* because a grandson was on his back, with his own stated reason: he disliked to hurry him until he had had his fill. Already in the index.
- **For "cheering and encouragement"** — **al-Kafi 6:50, ch. 35, h. 6** (index): the four-point rubric — accept what the child can manage, overlook what he cannot, do not overburden, do not be harsh. That is closer to "supervise without hovering" than anything he cited.
- **For "small fights are healthy"** — cite **Shantz 1987** and **Laursen et al. 2001** by name, and add the Olweus power-imbalance criterion so the claim does not swallow bullying.
- **For the video game economics** — cite **Newzoo** and **IFPI** by name and year. The claim is strong enough to survive being made precisely, and precision is what protects it from the "movies means streaming too" rejoinder.
- **For the martyr narrative** — the primary is **«تنها گریه کن» by Akram Eslami** (Hamaseh-ye Yaran, 1399 sh), with Khamenei's *taqriz*. Naming a book with a *taqriz* from the Leader is a far stronger move than "a story that's available on the internet," and it is checkable.
