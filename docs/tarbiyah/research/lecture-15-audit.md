## Headline findings

- **The Qurʾānic spine of this lecture is solid.** 64:6, 24:30–31, 59:10, 41:34 and 23:96 were all
  verified verse-by-verse, and the *readings* he gives match *al-Mīzān* — including his claim that the
  Prophet is told **twice** to repel evil with good (23:96 and 41:34, both singular imperatives). The
  one Qurʾānic problem is an **attribution**, not a text: he sources the 64:6 reading to "the tafsīr of
  the Leader," and no such tafsīr could be reached or confirmed. The content is Ṭabāṭabāʾī's and
  al-Ṭabrisī's, verbatim.
- **The *makārim al-akhlāq* hadith is real but he has the wrong frame.** The best-graded Imami text
  (al-Kāfī 2/1/53 h.1, al-Majlisī: ***ḥasan kaʾl-ṣaḥīḥ***) is the Prophet **in a public sermon** with
  **four** items, not three, and **not addressed to Amīr al-Muʾminīn**. The three-item version addressed
  to ʿAlī exists only in the **Sunnī** chains (Bayhaqī, Ṭabarānī) and is graded ***ḍaʿīf***.
- **Two items should not be attributed at all.** The Prophet's "**four signs of a *khāʾin***" is
  **NOT LOCATED** anywhere (dorar returns nothing; al-Kāfī's trust chapter checked) — and the speaker
  himself can only supply one of the four. The **captured assassin spared for five traits** is likewise
  **NOT LOCATED**: absent from the Sunnī corpus and absent from al-Kāfī's own dedicated *ghayrah*
  chapter (5/3/177, all nine ḥadīth checked). His repeated gloss of the captives as "Daesh-type
  terrorists" is a modern overlay he supplies; no version frames them that way.
- **The *ghayrah* restriction is defensible — and it has a real textual basis he never cites.**
  Nahj al-Balāgha **Ḥikmah 124** is confirmed verbatim, and the operative principle behind the co-wife
  reading is **al-Kāfī 5, Kitāb al-Nikāḥ, ch.178** — a one-ḥadīth chapter titled *"No ghayrah in what is
  lawful"*, «**لا غَيْرَةَ فِي الْحَلالِ**», on a chain of the strongest type. What he leaves out is the
  other limb: al-Kāfī 5/3/177 **h.9** and Nahj **Letter 31** condemn *misplaced* ghayrah as driving a
  sound woman to sickness and an innocent one to suspicion.
- **"Parental controls are a failed experiment" is his best-supported empirical claim — for filters.**
  Przybylski & Nash 2018 (EU n=13,176 plus a preregistered UK sample n=1,004) found filtering had
  "inconsistent and practically insignificant" links to encountering online sexual material. But Collier
  et al. 2016 (meta-analysis, 57 studies) puts restrictive mediation at **r+ = −.06** — small, but not
  nothing — and co-viewing at **r+ = +.09**, i.e. *worse*. The arm that works is the one he then
  recommends: active, instructive mediation.
- **The maxim corpus does the heavy lifting and cannot be graded.** Nine of this lecture's citations
  (*al-kadhibu wa'l-khiyānah*, *khiyānat al-wadīʿah*, *ifshāʾ al-sirr*, *sunnat al-kirām*, the three
  *ḥayāʾ* sayings, *sabab al-fitan*, *al-musāraʿah ilā'l-ʿafw*, *man lam yujāzi'l-isāʾah*) belong to
  *Ghurar al-Ḥikam* / *ʿUyūn al-Ḥikam*, which carry **no isnād at all**. Four of them were
  absence-checked against the al-Kāfī chapters that ought to contain them and are not there.

## Quranic verses

### 64:6 — al-Taghābun: "Shall human beings guide us?"

**Verse** — ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُۥ كَانَت تَّأۡتِيهِمۡ رُسُلُهُم بِٱلۡبَيِّنَٰتِ فَقَالُوٓاْ أَبَشَرٞ يَهۡدُونَنَا فَكَفَرُواْ وَتَوَلَّواْۖ وَّٱسْتَغۡنَى ٱللَّهُۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَنِيٌّ حَمِيدٞ

**Translation** (Dr Mustafa Khattab, *The Clear Quran*, as served by quran.com) — "That was because their
messengers used to come to them with clear proofs, but they said ˹mockingly˺, 'How can humans be our
guides?' So they persisted in disbelief and turned away. And Allah was not in need ˹of their faith˺. For
Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy."

**Context** — Sūrat al-Taghābun, Madinan; vv.5–6 recall the fate of earlier rejecting nations before the
sūrah turns to the believers. The speaker's recitation of the verse in the lecture is **accurate**,
including *fa-kafarū wa tawallaw wa'staghna'llāh*.

**Tafsīr al-Mīzān** (Arabic retrieved) — «**والتنكير للتحقير، والاستفهام للإنكار أي قالوا على سبيل
الإنكار: أآحاد من البشر لا فضل لهم علينا يهدوننا**». The indefinite *basharun* is used **to belittle**;
the interrogative is **rejection**, i.e. "are mere individuals of humankind, who have no superiority over
us, to guide us?"

**Other Shia scholarship** — *Majmaʿ al-Bayān* (al-Ṭabrisī) on the same phrase: «**أبشر يهدوننا …
استصغاراً منهم للبشر أن يكون رسلاً من الله**» — "out of their belittling of human beings, that they
should be messengers from God."

**Verdict** — **Verse and reading VERIFIED; attribution NOT VERIFIED.** The substance of what he says —
that the objection is contempt for the very idea that a human being can carry divine guidance, and that
this is an ancient pattern rather than something that began with the Prophet — is exactly *al-Mīzān* and
*Majmaʿ al-Bayān*. But he sources it to "**the tafsīr of the Leader**," and Ayatollah Khamenei has no
published Qurʾān commentary that could be reached or confirmed; `english.khamenei.ir` and `leader.ir`
are 403-blocked at the egress proxy (re-tested this session), so this is an **unreachable archive**, not
a completed negative search. Two further cautions: (1) both mufassirūn locate the contempt in the
*messengers'* humanity — the extension to "the enemies of mankind don't believe human beings are as good
as they are," i.e. a general denial of human dignity, is the speaker's homiletic widening, defensible but
not in the tafsīr; (2) his "not the regular garden-variety kuffār" framing is the same phrase the shared
index has already flagged as an **unverified Khamenei attribution** (on Sūrat al-Nūr 47–50). This is now
the **fifth** unverified Khamenei attribution in the series against two verified ones.

**Confidence** — Verse: high. Tafsīr content: high. Attribution to the Leader: **low / unresolved.**

### 24:30–31 — al-Nūr: lowering the gaze

**Verse** — 24:30 قُل لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ يَغُضُّوا۟ مِنْ أَبْصَـٰرِهِمْ وَيَحْفَظُوا۟ فُرُوجَهُمْ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ أَزْكَىٰ لَهُمْ ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ خَبِيرٌۢ بِمَا يَصْنَعُونَ
· 24:31 وَقُل لِّلْمُؤْمِنَـٰتِ يَغْضُضْنَ مِنْ أَبْصَـٰرِهِنَّ وَيَحْفَظْنَ فُرُوجَهُنَّ …

**Translation** (Khattab / and Pickthall for v.31 via the Quranic Arabic Corpus) — "Tell the believing men
to lower their gaze and guard their chastity. That is purer for them…" / "And tell the believing women to
lower their gaze and be modest…"

**Context** — Madinan; the passage follows the *ifk* verses and the etiquette-of-entry verses, so the
gaze instruction sits inside a block about household and communal decency.

**Tafsīr al-Mīzān** (Arabic retrieved) — «**الغضّ إطباق الجفن على الجفن**» (*ghaḍḍ* is the closing of the
eyelid upon the eyelid); and on the particle: «**ومن هنا يظهر أن {من} في {من أبصارهم} لابتداء الغاية لا
مزيدة**» — *min* marks the **starting point** of the act, not a partitive and not redundant.
*al-Mīzān*'s **baḥth riwāʾī** on this passage cites **al-Kāfī** (Abū ʿAmr al-Zubayrī ← Imam al-Ṣādiq):
«**كل شيء في القرآن من حفظ الفرج فهو من الزنا إلا هذه الآية فهو من النظر**» — every Qurʾānic mention of
*ḥifẓ al-farj* concerns fornication **except this verse, which concerns looking** — and the narration of
the young Anṣārī struck in the face while staring at a woman, after which Jibrīl brought these verses.

**Other Shia scholarship** — the *al-Kāfī* gloss above is itself the standard Imami anchor for treating
24:30–31 as the gaze verses specifically.

**Verdict** — **VERIFIED, including the numbering exactly as he states it.** He says the Qurʾān "addresses
men first" and gives 24:31 as the women's verse: both correct. Two trivial caption-level slips:
the women's verb is *yaghḍuḍna* (يَغْضُضْنَ), not "yaghudna"; and *ghaḍḍ* is properly "restraining/lowering,"
which is what he says. His pairing of the verse with the tarbiyah point (that a child already habituated
to forbidden looking is harder to reach) is his own pastoral extension, not tafsīr — see the note under
"Non-scripture claims."

**Confidence** — High.

### 59:10 — al-Ḥashr: the *ghill* supplication

**Verse** — وَٱلَّذِينَ جَآءُو مِنۢ بَعْدِهِمْ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا ٱغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَٰنِنَا ٱلَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِٱلْإِيمَـٰنِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِى قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِّلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ رَبَّنَآ إِنَّكَ رَءُوفٌ رَّحِيمٌ

**Translation** — "And those who came after them say, 'Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded
us in faith, and put not in our hearts any rancour toward those who have believed. Our Lord, You are
indeed Most Compassionate, Most Merciful.'"

**Context** — Sūrat al-Ḥashr, after the Muhājirūn (v.8) and the Anṣār (v.9); *al-Mīzān* identifies "those
who came after them" as those whose belief occurred **after the cessation of hijrah following the
conquest** — a third, later cohort.

**Tafsīr al-Mīzān** (Arabic retrieved) — «**والغل العداوة**» — *ghill* is **enmity**. The supplication asks
that such enmity not be placed in their hearts toward *any* of the believers.

**Other Shia scholarship** — not separately retrieved this session.

**Verdict** — **VERIFIED.** The speaker gives no reference; the reference is **Qurʾān 59:10 (al-Ḥashr)**,
and his rendering ("forgive us and forgive those brothers of ours who've gone before us; don't put hatred
and spite in our hearts towards those who believed") is accurate. One honest caveat for a Shia listener:
this verse is a live text in the intra-Muslim argument about the Companions, and *al-Mīzān* reads it as a
description of a specific historical cohort. Using it as a **family** supplication against carrying
grudges is a legitimate application of a general moral, but it is an application, not the verse's
referent. Say "the Qurʾān teaches us to ask this," not "this verse is about your household."

**Confidence** — High for text and *al-Mīzān*; the family application is homiletic.

### 41:34 (with 23:96) — repelling evil with good

**Verse** — 41:34 وَلَا تَسْتَوِى ٱلْحَسَنَةُ وَلَا ٱلسَّيِّئَةُ ۚ ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا ٱلَّذِى بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُۥ عَدَٰوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُۥ وَلِىٌّ حَمِيمٌ
· 23:96 ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ ٱلسَّيِّئَةَ ۚ نَحْنُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا يَصِفُونَ

**Translation** — 41:34 "Good and evil are not equal. Repel evil with what is better, and then the one
between whom and you there was enmity will become as though he were a devoted friend." · 23:96 "Repel evil
with what is best. We know well what they claim."

**Context** — 41:34 is in Fuṣṣilat, addressed to the Prophet amid Meccan hostility; 23:96 closes a passage
of the same kind in al-Muʾminūn. **Both are singular imperatives addressed to the Prophet.**

**Tafsīr al-Mīzān** (Arabic retrieved, 41:34–35) — «**ادفع بالخصلة التي هي أحسن الخصلة السيئة**» — repel the
bad trait with the better trait, whether truth against falsehood, forbearance against ignorance, or pardon
against wrongdoing; the second clause is «**بيان لأثر الدفع بالأحسن**», a statement of the *effect* of so
repelling. On 41:35 («وما يُلقّاها إلا الذين صبروا وما يُلقّاها إلا ذو حظٍّ عظيم») he glosses the "great
share" as «**نصيب وافر من كمال الإنسانية**» — an abundant share of human perfection.

**Other Shia scholarship** — not separately retrieved.

**Verdict** — **VERIFIED, and his count is right.** He says "twice the prophet is told to meet a bad deed
with a good deed": **23:96 and 41:34** are precisely the two places the Prophet is given this imperative
in the second person singular. (13:22 and 28:54 carry the same idea but describe believers generally —
I was not able to pull their texts this session, so treat that as my recollection, not a verification.)
His paraphrase of the payoff — "it'll turn those people who have enmity towards us … around" — is 41:34's
own clause. Worth adding for a parent: *al-Mīzān* on 41:35 attaches an explicit condition, **ṣabr**, and
calls the capacity rare. That qualification would strengthen, not weaken, his point.

**Confidence** — High.

## Correction to the 41:34 entry above — 13:22 and 28:54 are now verified

The Qurʾānic-verse section above left one item standing as recollection rather than verification: the
remark that **13:22** and **28:54** "carry the same idea but describe believers generally." That has now
been checked directly against quran.com and the reading is **confirmed, with the grammatical point
exactly right**:

- **13:22** — «وَٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُواْ ٱبْتِغَآءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِمْ وَأَقَامُواْ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَأَنفَقُواْ مِمَّا رَزَقْنَٰهُمْ سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَةً **وَيَدْرَءُونَ بِٱلْحَسَنَةِ ٱلسَّيِّئَةَ**»
  — "…and who repel evil with good." The verb **يَدْرَءُونَ** is **third-person plural**, one item in a list
  of the traits of *ulū'l-albāb*, not an imperative.
- **28:54** — «أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ يُؤْتَوْنَ أَجْرَهُم مَّرَّتَيْنِ بِمَا صَبَرُواْ **وَيَدْرَءُونَ بِٱلْحَسَنَةِ ٱلسَّيِّئَةَ** وَمِمَّا رَزَقْنَـٰهُمْ يُنفِقُونَ»
  — same verb, same third-person plural, describing the People of the Book who believed.

**Consequence for the speaker's claim.** It stands. The Qurʾān uses this instruction in **four** places;
in **two** of them (**23:96**, **41:34**) it is a **singular imperative — ٱدْفَعْ — addressed to the Prophet**,
and in the other two (**13:22**, **28:54**) it is a **descriptive third-person plural** about believers.
So "twice the Prophet is told to meet a bad deed with a good deed" is **precisely accurate**, and the
audit no longer rests any part of that verdict on memory. Note also the lexical difference worth telling
a parent: 23:96 and 41:34 use **دفع** (*idfaʿ*, "push away"), 13:22 and 28:54 use **درء** (*yadraʾūn*,
"ward off / avert") — the same manoeuvre described from the Prophet's side and from ours.

**Confidence** — High. (quran.com, verses 13:22 and 28:54, Arabic + Khattab.)

## Narrations

### N1. "The *sunnah* of the base is ugly speech" — [06:37]
**Arabic.** «سُنَّةُ الْكِرَامِ تَرَادُفُ الْإِنْعَامِ · **سُنَّةُ اللِّئَامِ قُبْحُ الْكَلَامِ**»
**Translation.** "The way of the noble is favour upon favour; the way of the base is ugliness of speech."
**Reference.** ⭐ **Located and numbered.** Imam ʿAlī, ***Ghurar al-Ḥikam wa Durar al-Kalim*** (al-Āmidī),
**entries 5550–5551** — a matched pair, *kirām* first and *liʾām* second. Reproduced with those numbers by
al-Shīʿa's topical compilation *al-Karam fī aqwāl al-Nabī Muḥammad wa Āli Baytih*.
**Chain.** **None.** *Ghurar al-Ḥikam* carries no isnād for any of its ~11,000 entries.
**Authenticity.** **Ungradeable** — there is nothing to grade. See the standing note in `_verified-index.md`.
**Confidence.** **Moderately verified** — text and entry number pinned; provenance unverifiable in principle.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ✅ **Wording exactly right**, including the rhyme *liʾām / kalām* that makes
it memorable. ⚠ He introduces it as "the hadith of the Imam," which flattens the distinction: this is a
**maxim from a 6th/12th-century thematic collection**, not a transmitted report with a chain. Corroborative,
not probative. ⚠ He also quotes only the second half; the paired first half (*sunnat al-kirām tarāduf
al-inʿām*) is the one that would give a child something to *do* rather than something to avoid.
**Practical meaning.** The pairing is the teaching device: nobility shows in what you *give*, baseness in
what you *say*. For a lecture whose whole argument is that karāmah produces behaviour automatically, the
positive limb is the more useful half.

### N2. "Do not speak to one another with obscenity — it is not worthy of us" — [06:37]
**As delivered.** «لَا تَتَكَلَّمُوا بِالْفُحْشِ…» + "this is not worthy of us … it is also beneath our Shīʿa."
**Reference.** ⚠ **NOT LOCATED with this wording.** Searched in Arabic for the *lā tatakallamū bi'l-fuḥsh*
construction against Nahj al-Balāgha, *Ghurar*, and the al-Kāfī *fuḥsh/badhāʾ* material; nothing returned
that sentence with the "not worthy of us / of our Shīʿa" clause attached.
**What IS attested and says the same thing.**
- **Nahj al-Balāgha, Khuṭba 206** — when he heard some of his men reviling the people of Shām at Ṣiffīn:
  «**إِنِّي أَكْرَهُ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَكُونُوا سَبَّابِينَ**» — "I dislike that you should be revilers." This is the
  genuine text for "foul speech is beneath the followers of ʿAlī," it is famous, and it is stronger ground.
- The al-Kāfī chapter *bāb al-badhāʾ* (Kitāb al-Īmān wa'l-Kufr) is the Imāmī home of this theme.
**Confidence.** **NOT LOCATED** for the quoted wording (limited search); **strongly verified** for the
substitute at Nahj Khuṭba 206.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ⚠ The **content is entirely sound and thoroughly Imāmī**; the **specific
sentence he puts in Amīr al-Muʾminīn's mouth is not one I could find**. Because he delivers it as a direct
quotation in Arabic, this matters. **Recommend he switch to Khuṭba 206**, which carries the same force,
comes with an actual occasion (Ṣiffīn), and is checkable by any listener in one minute.

### N3. ⭐ "Return the trust to the one who entrusted you, and do not betray the one who betrayed you" — [09:40]
**Arabic.** «أَدِّ الْأَمَانَةَ إِلَى مَنِ ائْتَمَنَكَ، وَلَا تَخُنْ مَنْ خَانَكَ»
**Translation.** "Discharge the trust to the one who entrusted it to you, and do not betray the one who
betrayed you."
**Reference.** ⚠ **This is a Sunnī text, and it is one of the most heavily contested hadiths in the entire
Sunnī corpus.** It is at **Abū Dāwūd 3535**, **al-Tirmidhī 1264**, Musnad Aḥmad, al-Dāraquṭnī and al-Bayhaqī,
from Abū Hurayrah (with variant chains from Anas and Ubayy b. Kaʿb).
**Chain.** Multiple; every one of them criticised.
**Authenticity — the assessors, named.** From the dorar.net takhrīj table:
- **Imām Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal: «بَاطِل»** — *false/void* (via Ibn al-Mulaqqin, *Khulāṣat al-Badr al-Munīr* 2/150,
  and *al-Muqarrar* 2/393).
- **Abū Ḥātim al-Rāzī: «مُنْكَر»** (*al-Muḥarrar fī'l-Ḥadīth* 328).
- **al-Shāfiʿī, via al-Bayhaqī, *al-Sunan al-Kubrā* 10/271: «لَيْسَ بِثَابِت»** — not established.
- **Ibn al-Jawzī, *al-ʿIlal al-Mutanāhiya* 2/592–593: «جَمِيعُ طُرُقِهِ لَا يَصِحّ»** — *none* of its chains is sound
  (he says this of the Abū Hurayrah, Anas **and** Ubayy b. Kaʿb versions separately).
- **Ibn al-Mulaqqin:** "it has six routes, **all of them weak**."
- **Ibn Ḥazm: «لَا يَصِحّ»**; **Ibn al-Mundhir: «غَيْرُ ثَابِت»**; **Ibn al-Qayyim** and **Ibn Ḥazm** treat the
  al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī route as ***mursal***.
- **The one favourable verdict: al-Tirmidhī himself — «حَسَنٌ غَرِيب»** at *Sunan* 1264.
**Imāmī status.** No al-Kāfī / al-Ṣadūq / al-Ṭūsī placement located for this wording within the search budget.
**Confidence.** **Strongly verified as a finding** (the takhrīj is unambiguous and multiply attested);
**the hadith itself is NOT established.**
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ⭐ ⚠ **This is the most significant single problem in the lecture, and it
sits at the top of the argument.** He introduces it as "This is from Rasūlullāh" and uses it as the anchor
for the whole *khiyānah* section — the proposition that betrayal is so grave that you may not even return it
in kind. But the text he is quoting is one that **Imām Aḥmad called *bāṭil*** and Ibn al-Jawzī judged
unsound by every route; it is **Sunnī** in provenance, with no Imāmī chain located; and he gives it to a Shia
audience with no source at all. Two honest options: (1) drop the attribution and keep the principle, which
is independently sound; or (2) use a text that carries it properly — see **Better-sourced substitutes**,
where **Q 4:58** and the *amānah* material of al-Kāfī's *Kitāb al-Īmān wa'l-Kufr* are set out.
**Practical meaning.** Worth saying plainly: the *ethic* here — you do not become a traitor because someone
was a traitor to you — is Qurʾānic (5:8, «وَلَا يَجْرِمَنَّكُمْ شَنَآنُ قَوْمٍ عَلَىٰٓ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُواْ») and needs no weak
hadith to prop it up.

**Reference.** Imam ʿAlī; belongs to the chainless maxim corpus — ***Ghurar al-Ḥikam*** / ***ʿUyūn al-Ḥikam

**Authenticity.** **Ungradeable.**
**Confidence.** **Moderately verified** — attribution and wording stable, reference unpinned.

### N5. "Among the most obscene forms of betrayal is betrayal of a deposit" — [12:45]

### N6. ⭐ "Your disclosing your brother's secret is treachery" — [13:46]
**Arabic.** «الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ، وَ**إِفْشَاؤُكَ سِرَّ أَخِيكَ خِيَانَةٌ، فَاجْتَنِبْ ذَلِكَ**، وَاجْتَنِبْ مَجْلِسَ الْعَشِيرَةِ»
**Translation.** "Gatherings are held in trust; your divulging your brother's secret is treachery, so avoid
that — and avoid the gathering of the clan [that engages in it]."
**Reference.** ⭐ **LOCATED, and the speaker's attribution is right.** It is from the **waṣiyyah of the Prophet
to Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī** — the long counsel preserved in **Makārim al-Akhlāq** (al-Ṭabrisī) and
**al-Amālī of al-Ṭūsī**, and gathered into **Biḥār al-Anwār vol. 74** (*bāb mawāʿiẓ al-Nabī*). Text confirmed
verbatim on the Sayyid al-Shuhadāʾ Center's edition of the waṣiyyah.
**Chain.** The waṣiyyah as transmitted through *Makārim al-Akhlāq* is **chainless**; al-Ṭūsī's *Amālī* recension
has a chain, but no rijāl verdict for it was retrieved in this audit.
**Authenticity.** Not gradeable from *Makārim* (see the standing note on chainless works). The waṣiyyah as a
whole is a famous and much-commented text (Shahīd al-Thānī, *Munyat al-Murīd*, and modern commentaries treat
it seriously) but it is late-attested.
**Confidence.** **Strongly verified** as to text, source-family and attribution to the **Prophet**;
**needs further verification** as to grading.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ✅ **His best-executed narration in the *khiyānah* section.** He says "this
one is from our Prophet" — correct. He quotes it accurately, including *fa-ijtanib dhālik*. ⚠ Two things he
leaves out that strengthen his own point: the opening clause «**الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ**» ("gatherings are held
in trust"), which is precisely the principle behind his later case study of the boy who broadcasts what
happens in the house; and the fact that it is **advice to a single named companion**, which is why it reads
as pastoral rather than legislative.
**Practical meaning.** This is the narration that does the work his "four signs of a *khāʾin*" (N8) cannot.
It establishes an *immaterial* amānah — information — in an unimpeachably Prophetic voice.

### N7. "The way of the noble is fidelity to covenants" — [13:46]
**Arabic.** «سُنَّةُ الْكِرَامِ الْوَفَاءُ بِالْعُهُودِ · سُنَّةُ اللِّئَامِ الْجُحُودُ»
**Translation.** "The way of the noble is keeping covenants; the way of the base is repudiation."
**Reference.** ⭐ **Located and numbered.** Imam ʿAlī, ***Ghurar al-Ḥikam*, entries 5556–5557** — again a matched
*kirām / liʾām* pair, sitting four entries after N1 in the same *sunnah* cluster.
**Chain.** None. **Authenticity.** Ungradeable.
**Confidence.** **Moderately verified** — text and number pinned.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ✅ **Correct wording, correct sense, and his extension is textually
defensible**: he says the Ahlul Bayt "go a little deeper — sometimes there are unwritten agreements." The
Arabic word is **ʿuhūd**, covenants, which in Islamic usage covers far more than signed instruments; nothing
in the maxim restricts it to documents. ⚠ Again cited as "the ḥadīth says," and again the paired half —
*sunnat al-liʾām al-juḥūd* — is dropped.

**Reference.** ❌ **NOT LOCATED — do not attribute.** This verdict was reached in the first half of this audit

**Confidence.** **NOT LOCATED.**
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ❌ ⚠ **The tell is in his own delivery: he announces four signs and supplies

the Prophetic «مَا زَالَ جِبْرِيلُ يُوصِينِي بِالْجَارِ حَتَّى ظَنَنْتُ أَنَّهُ سَيُوَرِّثُهُ» (Bukhārī 6014 / Muslim 2624,

### N9. "The *zinā* of the eye is the look" — [21:49]
**Arabic.** «الْعَيْنَانِ زِنَاهُمَا النَّظَرُ» / «زِنَا الْعَيْنِ النَّظَرُ»
**Translation.** "The two eyes commit *zinā*, and their *zinā* is the look."
**Reference.** **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6612** (Kitāb al-Qadar, b.82 h.18); **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim 2657b** (b.46 h.32);
also Abū Dāwūd 2152, al-Tirmidhī 2786, and *Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn* 1622. Narrated by **Abū Hurayrah**, inside
the longer hadith that assigns each limb its share of *zinā* and ends "and the private part confirms it
or denies it."
**Authenticity.** ***Ṣaḥīḥ*, and *muttafaq ʿalayh*** — agreed upon by al-Bukhārī and Muslim. Named graders
are unnecessary at this level.
**Imāmī status.** The wording is at home in the Shia preaching tradition too, but the canonical placement
is Sunnī; the standard Imāmī text for the same point is «**النَّظْرَةُ سَهْمٌ مِنْ سِهَامِ إِبْلِيسَ مَسْمُومٌ**» — "the
look is a poisoned arrow from Iblīs's arrows" — in al-Kāfī's *Kitāb al-Nikāḥ, bāb al-naẓar*, and the
al-Kāfī gloss on 24:30 already cited in the Qurʾān section above («كل شيء في القرآن من حفظ الفرج فهو من
الزنا إلا هذه الآية فهو من النظر»).
**Confidence.** **Strongly verified** (Sunnī, *muttafaq ʿalayh*); Imāmī parallel located by family, not
pinned to a hadith number in this audit.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ✅ Wording and sense exactly right. ⚠ Same pattern the index has flagged
four times now: he says "the Prophet has told us" to a Shia audience while quoting a **Sunnī-canonical**
text with no Imāmī chain given. Here it costs him nothing — the al-Kāfī *sahm min sihām Iblīs* narration
says the same thing, is more vivid, and is Imāmī. **This is a recurring habit worth naming.**

### N10. ⭐ THE SPINE — "Three things indicate a person's *karam*" — [23:54], reprised [41:05]
**Arabic.** «**ثَلَاثَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى كَرَمِ الْمَرْءِ: حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ، وَكَظْمُ الْغَيْظِ، وَغَضُّ الطَّرْفِ**»
**Translation.** "Three things indicate a man's nobility: good character, swallowing anger, and lowering
the gaze."
**Reference.** ⭐ **LOCATED AND PINNED.** Imam **Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq**, ***Tuḥaf al-ʿUqūl* of Ibn Shuʿba
al-Ḥarrānī, p. 319** — in the section «ومن كلامه عليه السلام سماه بعض الشيعة **نثر الدرر**», the collection
of the Imam's short maxims. Confirmed independently at almerja.com's *iḍāʾāt* index and in Jamʿiyyat
al-Maʿārif's *al-Karam fī aqwāl al-Nabī wa Āli Baytih*, which cites it as *Tuḥaf al-ʿUqūl: 319*.
**Chain.** ❌ **None.** *Tuḥaf al-ʿUqūl* **omits every isnād in the book** — this is Ibn Shuʿba's declared
editorial method, stated in his own preface.
**Authenticity.** **Ungradeable in principle.** No assessor has graded it and none can; there is nothing to
grade. This is the same standing limitation the index records for *Tuḥaf*, *Ghurar*, *ʿUyūn al-Ḥikam*,
*Nahj al-Balāgha*, *Makārim al-Akhlāq* and *Miṣbāḥ al-Sharīʿa*.
**Confidence.** **Strongly verified** as to text, wording, source, page and attribution to **Imam al-Ṣādiq**;
**ungradeable** as to authenticity.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ⭐ **This is the structural spine of the lecture and he uses it well.**
Everything after [23:54] hangs on it: *ghaḍḍ al-baṣar* becomes section 2 and 3, *ḥusn al-khuluq* becomes
section 5 (the Ḥaydar case study), and *kaẓm al-ghayẓ* is the bridge into the forgiveness material at the
end. He is right that it is Imam al-Ṣādiq, right that there are three, and right about all three items.
Three cautions, in descending order of importance:
1. ⚠ **It carries no chain, and he presents it as "hadith now."** For the load-bearing text of a
   59-minute argument, the listener deserves to know it comes from a chainless 4th/10th-century *maxim*
   collection. It is corroborative, not probative — and the corroboration is genuinely strong, because
   each of the three limbs is independently attested in graded material.
2. ⚠ The Arabic is **غَضُّ الطَّرْفِ** (*ghaḍḍ al-ṭarf*), not *ghaḍḍ al-baṣar*. Same meaning, but he then
   pivots straight to Q 24:30–31, where the Qurʾānic word **is** *abṣār*. Worth saying that the maxim and
   the verse converge rather than implying the maxim quotes the verse.
3. ⚠ At [41:05] he reprises it as "there are three things which indicate if a person has karāmah" and then
   loses the thread ("the first one that the Imam mentions he says…") without completing it. On the second
   pass the three items never actually get restated.
**Practical meaning.** The three are a **behavioural** definition of dignity, which is exactly what a
parent can work with: how you speak, what you do with anger, where you let your eyes go. Note the internal
logic he does not draw out — all three are forms of *self-restraint under pressure*, which is why he can
argue that karāmah is the upstream cause and compliance the downstream effect.

**Reference.** Imam ʿAlī; chainless maxim corpus — ***Ghurar al-Ḥikam*** / ***ʿUyūn al-Ḥikam wa'l-Mawāʿiẓ***.
⚠ **Entry number NOT PINNED.** Two Arabic phrase searches and two fetches of Shia topical *ḥayāʾ*

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ✅ Wording accurate, attribution to Amīr al-Muʾminīn accurate, and the

### N13. Nahj al-Balāgha Ḥikmah 124 — a woman's *ghayrah* and a man's — [29:59]
**Arabic.** «**غَيْرَةُ الْمَرْأَةِ كُفْرٌ، وَغَيْرَةُ الرَّجُلِ إِيمَانٌ**»
**Translation.** "A woman's jealousy is *kufr*; a man's jealousy is faith."
**Reference.** **Nahj al-Balāgha, Ḥikmah 124** (Ṣubḥī Ṣāliḥ). ✅ Confirmed verbatim in the first half of
this audit.
**Chain.** **None** — Nahj al-Balāgha carries no isnād.
**Authenticity.** Ungradeable as transmitted by al-Sharīf al-Raḍī.
**Confidence.** **Strongly verified** as to text and location; **ungradeable** as to chain.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ✅ **He handles a genuinely difficult text responsibly**, and this deserves
saying plainly. He raises it *unprompted* — "there's that ḥadīth … that women [are] not supposed to be
having ghayrah, cuz that's in Nahj al-Balāgha" — and immediately restricts it: "that's a different type of
ghayrah … that's ghayrah [when] the husband is trying to marry again. That ghayrah, no. **This kind of
ghayrah is for everybody.**" That restriction is the mainstream Imāmī reading and it has a real textual
basis, which is N14. ⚠ What he does not tell the audience is that the restriction is **an interpretation**,
not something the ḥikmah itself says; the text as al-Raḍī gives it is unqualified.
**Practical meaning.** The commentators (Ibn Abī'l-Ḥadīd; Muḥammad ʿAbduh; and among the moderns Makārim
Shīrāzī's *Payām-e Imām*) all read *ghayrah* here as the jealousy that *objects to what the Sharīʿah has
permitted*, which is why it can be called *kufr* — it is objection to a divine ruling, not an emotion.

### N14. ⭐ The text behind the restriction — "There is no *ghayrah* in what is lawful" — [29:59], unquoted
**Arabic.** «**لَا غَيْرَةَ فِي الْحَلَالِ**»
**Reference.** ⭐ **al-Kāfī vol. 5, Kitāb al-Nikāḥ, chapter 178** — a **one-ḥadīth chapter whose title is the
ḥadīth**. Confirmed in the first half of this audit, on a chain of the strongest type.
**Confidence.** **Strongly verified.**
**Verdict.** ⭐ **This is the missing citation of the whole *ghayrah* section.** The speaker makes exactly
the right move and then makes it on his own authority. Al-Kulaynī gave the restriction a chapter heading
eleven centuries ago. Cite it: it converts "that's a different type of ghayrah" from an assertion into a
report. **See also the limb he omits** — al-Kāfī 5/3/177 **h.9** and **Nahj al-Balāgha, Letter 31** both
condemn *misplaced* ghayrah, warning that it drives a sound woman to sickness and an innocent one to
suspicion. A lecture recommending *ghayrah* to parents raising teenagers needs that brake in it.

### N15. ⭐ The captured assassin spared for five traits — [30:59]
**As delivered.** "These terrorists, these Daesh-type terrorists, were captured. Apparently they had planned
to kill Rasūlullāh … All of them were executed except one … Rasūlullāh said, 'Jibrāʾīl told me from God …
you have five traits that Allah and His Messenger love. The first one, *al-ghayratu shadīdatun ʿalā
ḥaramik*.'"
**Reference.** ❌ **NOT LOCATED — do not attribute.** Absence-checked in the first half of this audit
against the Sunnī corpus via dorar.net and against **al-Kāfī 5, chapter 177**, the dedicated *ghayrah*
chapter, **all nine ḥadīth read**. A further Arabic phrase search this session on «الغيرة الشديدة على حرمك»
paired with the five-traits frame returned nothing but modern Friday-sermon pages.
**Confidence.** **NOT LOCATED.**
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ❌ ⚠ **A high-colour narrative, told in circumstantial detail, that no
source supports.** Three separate problems:
1. **The narration itself is unlocated** — neither the frame (prisoners condemned, one released) nor the
   five-trait list nor the *ghayrah* clause could be found in any collection.
2. **He supplies only one of the five**, exactly as in N8. He says "and he mentions the four others" and

3. ⚠ **"Daesh-type terrorists" is his own overlay, repeated five times.** No version of any comparable

al-Kāfī 5 ch.177 itself — «إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَيُورٌ يُحِبُّ كُلَّ غَيُورٍ» ("God is *ghayūr* and loves every *ghayūr*"),

### N17. "If good character were a creation you could see…" — [42:05]
**As delivered.** «مَا كَانَ مِمَّا خَلَقَ اللهُ شَيْءٌ أَحْسَنُ مِنْ[هُ]» / «مَا كَانَ مِمَّا خَلَقَ اللهُ شَيْءٌ أَقْبَحُ مِنْ[هُ]» —
"if *ḥusn al-khuluq* were a creation of God that we could see, there would not be anything out of all the
things Allah has created more beautiful than it; and if bad akhlāq were a creation we could see, there
would be nothing more hideous."
**Reference.** ⚠ **NOT LOCATED with this wording — do not attribute.** Checked this session:
**dorar.net** phrase search (Sunnī takhrīj with named graders); the al-Shīʿa topical compilation
*al-Khuluq fī aqwāl al-Nabī Muḥammad wa Āli Baytih* (43 entries, none matching); and two further Arabic
phrase searches. Nothing in either corpus carries the double comparative — "most beautiful of what Allah
created" / "most hideous."
**The nearest attested text, and it is not the same.** dorar.net returns «**لَوْ كَانَ حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ رَجُلًا
يَمْشِي فِي النَّاسِ لَكَانَ رَجُلًا صَالِحًا**» — "if good character were a man walking among people he would be a
righteous man" — graded ***ḍaʿīf* to *ḍaʿīf jiddan*** by the Sunnī critics indexed there. That is a much
weaker claim (a righteous man, not the most beautiful thing in creation) and it has no ugliness clause.
**Confidence.** **NOT LOCATED.** The construction belongs to a known homiletic *family* — cf. the genuine
al-Kāfī saying on obscenity, «**لَوْ كَانَ الْفُحْشُ مِثَالًا لَكَانَ مِثَالَ سُوءٍ**» ("if obscenity were an image it
would be an evil image," *Kitāb al-Īmān wa'l-Kufr*, *bāb al-fuḥsh*) — but the specific sentence he quotes
was not found.
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ⚠ **He says "the Prophet says this" and gives Arabic for both halves.**
That is a stronger claim than the evidence supports. This matters more than usual here, because the
narration is the hinge into the Behrouz Azimi near-death story — the whole "bad akhlāq is objectively
hideous, and someone has *seen* it" sequence rests on it. ⚠ Note too that his rendering slides between two
different propositions: "if it were a creation we could **see**" (a thought experiment) and the claim that
the near-death experiencer **did** see it (a report). The narration, even if located, would only license

### N22. ⭐ *Makārim al-akhlāq* — three items, or four? — [58:14]
**As delivered.** "Sometimes these ḥadīth that talk about … *makārim al-akhlāq* — the Prophet advising

you**; two, you **reconnect with the person who cut you off**; three, you **forgive the person who
oppressed you**."
**The best-graded Imāmī text.** ***al-Kāfī*, vol. 2, Kitāb al-Īmān wa'l-Kufr, chapter 53, ḥadīth 1** —
al-Majlisī in *Mirʾāt al-ʿUqūl*: ***ḥasan kaʾl-ṣaḥīḥ***. Established in the first half of this audit. In
that text the Prophet is **addressing a public gathering**, not ʿAlī, and he gives **four** items, not
three: *ṣilat man qaṭaʿak*, *iʿṭāʾ man ḥaramak*, *al-ʿafw ʿamman ẓalamak*, **and** — the fourth, which the

**Confidence.** **Strongly verified** (both limbs).
**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ⭐ ⚠ **The narration is real; the frame is the weaker one.** He has taken
the *three-item, addressed-to-ʿAlī, ḍaʿīf* recension in preference to the *four-item, public-sermon,
ḥasan kaʾl-ṣaḥīḥ* one that sits in al-Kāfī. Two costs:
1. **He loses the fourth item — truthfulness even against oneself.** That is a serious loss in *this*
   lecture, whose opening third is about a boy who lies and steals and whose thesis is that karāmah shows
   up as honesty. The al-Kāfī text hands him the connection between his first section and his last, and he
   drops it.
2. **The "advising Amīr al-Muʾminīn" frame is not the Imāmī one.** For a Shia audience this is an odd
   inversion: the version he prefers is the Sunnī weak one, and the version he omits is the Imāmī strong one.
**Recommendation.** ⭐ **Cite al-Kāfī 2/53/1 and give all four.** It is the single easiest upgrade available
in this lecture.

### C1. ⭐ "Parental controls are a failed experiment" — [24:54]
**Claim.** "I'll give my children a device, but I'm so clever, I'm going to put controls on it … Children
are a lot smarter than we think. That solution — that's a failed experiment."
**Status.** ⭐ **His best-supported empirical claim, and it is genuinely well supported — for filters
specifically.** Established in the first half of this audit: **Przybylski & Nash (2018)**, *Internet
Filtering and Adolescent Exposure to Online Sexual Material*, *Cyberpsychology, Behavior and Social
Networking* (PMID **29995533**) — an EU sample of **n = 13,176** plus a **preregistered** UK replication
(n = 1,004) — found filtering's association with encountering online sexual material "**inconsistent and
practically insignificant**," with the authors noting that even in the best case a very large number of
households would need filters to prevent one adolescent's exposure.
⚠ **The qualification that keeps this honest.** **Collier et al. (2016)**, a meta-analysis of **57 studies**
of parental media mediation, finds **restrictive mediation r+ = −.06** — small, but **not zero and in the
protective direction** — and, strikingly, **co-viewing r+ = +.09**, i.e. sitting and watching *with* the
child is associated with *worse* outcomes. **The arm that works is active, instructive mediation** — talking
about content — which is exactly what he then spends the rest of the lecture recommending.
**Verdict.** ✅ **Right for the right reason, and right about the alternative.** ⚠ "Failed experiment" is a
shade stronger than "small effect"; the accurate version is *filters buy far less than parents think, and
much less than a conversation does*. That version actually strengthens his argument, because it makes the
karāmah strategy the evidence-based one rather than merely the pious one.
**Confidence.** Strongly verified.

### C2. "Sin (*maʿṣiyah*) weakens willpower" — [22:53]
**Claim.** "There's a general rule in tarbiyah: sin, *maʿṣiyah*, weakens our willpower. So if somebody has
gotten used to sinning, they're going to have a weaker willpower … my spiritual inclinations will now be
weakened."
**Status as tradition.** ✅ **Thoroughly Islamic and easy to source** — the classical formulation is Q 83:14,
«**كَلَّا بَلْ رَانَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِم مَّا كَانُوا يَكْسِبُونَ**» ("rather, what they earned has rusted over their
hearts"), with the well-known Prophetic gloss on the black spot that spreads with each sin (al-Kāfī 2,

general rule that we have in tarbiyah" makes a Qurʾānic doctrine sound like a technique.
**Status as psychology.** ⚠ **Do not lean on the science here.** The nearest empirical construct is
**ego depletion** — the idea that self-control is a depletable resource — and it has taken heavy replication
damage: **Hagger et al. (2016), "A Multilab Preregistered Replication of the Ego-Depletion Effect,"
*Perspectives on Psychological Science* 11(4):546–573**, DOI 10.1177/1745691616652873, ran the paradigm
across ~2,000 participants in 23 labs and found **essentially no effect**. Habituation to a behaviour making
it easier to repeat is well established; "willpower is a fuel tank that sin drains" is not.
**Verdict.** ✅ Sound as doctrine, ⚠ unsourced, and ⚠ do not dress it in psychological language.
**Confidence.** Doctrine: strongly verified. Psychological mechanism: **not supported**.

**Claim.** Gaze control matters especially "for those of us who are raising boys because of how visual men
are, but even it's a problem for girls too."
**Status.** ✅ **Broadly supported, with an important nuance.** The standard review is **Rupp & Wallen
(2008), "Sex Differences in Response to Visual Sexual Stimuli: A Review," *Archives of Sexual Behavior*
37(2):206–218**, DOI 10.1007/s10508-007-9217-9, PMID **17668311**. It finds men report greater arousal to
visual sexual stimuli and show differences in neural response — **but** it also finds that women show
comparable *genital* response, that much of the reported sex difference tracks **stimulus content and
context** rather than a raw sensitivity difference, and that self-report and physiological measures diverge
far more in women than in men.
**Verdict.** ✅ The claim as he actually phrases it — a difference of degree, with the issue present for
girls too — is the defensible version, and better than the folk version ("men are visual, women are not").
⚠ Worth knowing that the sharper form of the claim is not what the literature says.
**Confidence.** Moderately verified.

### C6. The behavioural signs of a resentful child — [50:07]-[52:08]
**Claim.** Deliberate stubbornness, disobedience and insubordination *as a means of punishing the parent*,
"rushing to get revenge," wanting to see the parents flounder.
**Status.** ⚠ **Clinically recognisable, and he is describing it accurately** — the cluster (angry/irritable
mood, argumentative/defiant behaviour, **vindictiveness**) is the DSM-5 description of **oppositional
defiant disorder**, in which "spiteful or vindictive at least twice in the past six months" is an explicit
criterion. He is not claiming to diagnose, and his framing — *the child is punishing you, and every repeated
command is another opportunity* — is a genuinely useful reframe that clinicians would recognise as the
coercive-cycle model (Patterson).
⚠ **The causal claim is where to be careful.** He asserts that this "happens when a child is raised to not
have that deep sense of how worthy I am to God." Parenting style is *one* well-evidenced contributor; ODD is
multiply determined, with substantial temperamental and heritable components. Telling a parent that their
child's defiance is the product of how they spoke to him is powerful pastoral leverage and also, in a
subset of cases, false and cruel. The lecture's own case study resolves entirely onto the mother's conduct.
**Confidence.** Description: well supported. Sole causation: **overstated**.

### C7. The Sheikh's four diagnostic questions — [54:10]-[56:11]
**Claim.** Ask: when did you last ask his opinion *and follow it*? Do you correct him in public or private?
Do you use the name he likes? Are your instructions orders or consultation?
**Status.** ⚠ **Modern parenting practice in classical dress — but three of the four have real Imāmī
warrant**, and he would be stronger citing them:
- **Consultation** — *mushāwarah* is Qurʾānic (3:159, 42:38) and the *tarbiyah* application is standard.
- **Correcting in private** — **Nahj al-Balāgha, Ḥikmah 222**, *«مِنْ أَشْرَفِ أَعْمَالِ الْكَرِيمِ غَفْلَتُهُ عَمَّا
  يَعْلَمُ»* (*taghāful*), already in the index; and the Prophetic practice of correcting «ما بال أقوام» —
  addressing the fault without naming the person.
- **Using the name he likes** — ❌ **the index already records this one as NOT LOCATED as a saying.** The
  Prophet's *kunya* practice is real; the maxim is not. He does not quote it as a hadith here, which is an
  improvement on an earlier lecture.
- **Tone of command** — no classical source; his own observation, and a sensible one.
**Confidence.** Practice sound; **attribution not claimed**, which is why this passes.

### C8. "A society is made up of these little families" — [50:07]
⚠ Commonplace, unsourced, and unobjectionable. No verification applicable.

## Addendum — two refinements to the Headline findings above

The headline summary at the top of this file was written before the narration-by-narration work below.
Two of its statements need adjusting in the light of what that work found. Both make the lecture look
*better* in one respect and *worse* in another, so they are recorded rather than quietly absorbed.

1. **"Nine of this lecture's citations belong to *Ghurar al-Ḥikam* / *ʿUyūn al-Ḥikam*."**
   ⚠ **Eight, not nine.** ***Ifshāʾ al-sirr* is not a maxim** — it is a clause of the **Prophet's waṣiyyah to
   Abū Dharr** (Makārim al-Akhlāq / Amālī al-Ṭūsī → Biḥār 74), and the speaker's attribution of it to the
   Prophet is **correct**. See **N6**. Of the remaining eight, **five now carry pinned *Ghurar* entry
   numbers** (5550–5551, 5556–5557, 1566–1567, 7490, 8958) and three remain unpinned (*al-kadhib
   wa'l-khiyānah*, *khiyānat al-wadīʿah*, the two *ḥayāʾ* sayings, *sabab al-fitan*).
2. **"Two items should not be attributed at all."**
   ⚠ **Three.** The two named in the headline — the **four signs of a *khāʾin*** (N8) and the **captured
   assassin spared for five traits** (N15) — are now joined by **N17, "if *ḥusn al-khuluq* were a creation
   you could see."** That third one matters more than its length suggests, because it is the hinge into the
   Behrouz Azimi near-death sequence, and because the nearest text that *does* exist («لو كان حسن الخلق
   رجلاً يمشي في الناس») is graded ***ḍaʿīf*** and makes a much smaller claim.

**Net assessment of the lecture.** Of 22 narrations: **6 strongly verified** (four of them Qurʾānic or
Prophetic and well placed), **9 moderately verified** (accurately quoted chainless maxims, five now pinned
to entry numbers), **3 NOT LOCATED**, **1 not established and declared *bāṭil* by Aḥmad**, and **2 correctly
located but misattributed or taken in the weaker recension**. The pattern is consistent across the series:
**he quotes accurately and sources loosely.** His Arabic is reliable; his "the Prophet says" is not always.
