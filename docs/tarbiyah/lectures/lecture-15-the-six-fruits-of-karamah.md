# 1. Executive Summary

**Main thesis.** Everything a parent of a seven-to-fourteen-year-old spends their energy enforcing — honesty, keeping a promise, clean speech, control of the eyes, modesty, decent temper, letting go of a grudge — is downstream of one upstream cause. Shaykh Usama calls that cause *karamah*: the child's settled, felt knowledge of **how valuable he is to Allah**. Install that, and the behaviours follow without enforcement. Fail to install it, and you will spend the second seven years enforcing behaviours one at a time, forever, and losing.

> **"How long are we going to be able to be next to our children? … We're going to hover over them? … Right now we have this window where we're trying to install karamah, but we know that this is a temporary window. How do we fix the problem? One of the solutions is to raise children where they recognise how special they are to their Lord."** [23:54]

That is the argument of the lecture in one paragraph, and the rest of the hour is its inventory.

**The architecture.** Part 14 opened the second-seven-years block by establishing *why* — that dignity precedes piety, that *karamah* is granted from above (17:70, 2:30), that it is the precondition of *taqwa*. Part 15 is the **catalogue of returns**. He walks through six areas in which a child raised with *karamah* will require no policing, and in each one he sets the mindset of the *karim* (the noble) beside the mindset of the *la'im* (the base):

1. **The child honours trusts** — a full anatomy of *khiyanah*, betrayal, in three species: the material deposit (*wadi'ah*), the immaterial deposit (a secret), and the unwritten covenant (a neighbour) [08:38–14:47].
2. **The child controls his gaze** — Q 24:30–31, with the *tarbiyah* rule that a child already habituated to forbidden looking is a child your words no longer reach [20:49–23:54].
3. *(There is no third goal. He delivers “Goal Two,” then breaks off into the spine narration and the parental-controls argument [23:54–26:57], and resumes at “Goal Four.” Whatever he had numbered three is not in the recording — see §3.5, where the break happens.)*
4. **The child is modest and protective** — *'iffah* and *ghayrah*, including the most delicate text in the lecture and the most responsible handling of it [26:57–31:59].
5. **The child has real akhlaq, not performed akhlaq** — the test being how he behaves at home, where nothing is at stake socially [38:05–42:05].
6. **The child does not carry grudges** — Q 59:10, and the clinical picture of a child who has turned obedience into a weapon against his parents [49:06–56:11].

Binding the six together is one narration, quoted at [23:54] and reprised at [41:05]: Imam al-Sadiq's (a) **«ثَلَاثَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى كَرَمِ الْمَرْءِ: حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ، وَكَظْمُ الْغَيْظِ، وَغَضُّ الطَّرْفِ»** — *three things indicate a man's nobility: good character, swallowing anger, and lowering the gaze*. All three are forms of **self-restraint under pressure**, which is exactly why he can argue that dignity is the cause and compliance the effect [§6, N10].

**Why it matters for a parent.** Because it reverses the direction of effort. The exhausted parent of a seven-year-old is usually working *downstream* — correcting the lie, confiscating the device, repeating the instruction. This lecture says the leverage is entirely *upstream*, and its closing case study proves the point better than its arguments do: a mother describes a son who slashes tyres and smiles about it, and the Shaykh, instead of prescribing consequences, asks her four questions about how she speaks to him [54:10–56:11]. Every one of the four is about the child's dignity inside his own house. ⚠ That diagnostic move is powerful and it is also where the lecture's one pastoral danger lies — see §13.

## Topic 3.1 — The recap: dignity as the master key [00:31–06:37]

**Main teaching.** He restates the Part 14 thesis in four moves. (1) The job is to raise **dignified children** — children "who have discovered how valuable they are to their Lord" [02:31]. (2) That dignity, *karamah*, **comes from above**; it is conferred by Allah, not manufactured by the parent. (3) It is therefore a **contested** category — "a point of contention between us and the enemies of mankind," who "don't believe human beings are as good as they are" [02:31]. (4) And it is the **precondition of taqwa**: "as human beings, we normally we protect those things that are valuable. Taqwa, guarding myself, observing Allah — that is when I understand how valuable I am to Allah" [04:35].

**Evidence used.**

- **Qur'an 64:6** [03:34] — recited accurately, including the closing clauses: *dhalika bi-annahu kanat ta'tihim rusuluhum bi'l-bayyinat, fa-qalu a-basharun yahdunana, fa-kafaru wa tawallaw wa'staghna'llah, wa'llahu ghaniyyun hamid*. ✅ **Verse verified.** ✅ **Reading verified** — al-Mizan reads *a-basharun* as belittlement and the interrogative as rejection; *Majma' al-Bayan* says the same. ❌ **Attribution not verified** — he introduces it as "taken from the tafsir of the leader" [02:31], and no published Qur'an commentary by Ayatollah Khamenei could be reached or confirmed. See §5.
- **The lie-clocks story** [04:35–05:36] — Mother Teresa's clock never moves; Abraham Lincoln's barely creeps; the narrator's is being used as a fan in the angels' office. ✅ **He flags it as fiction before telling it**: *"Can I tell you a story that's not true first? I'm going to tell you a story that's not true."* That is the correct handling of an illustrative fable in a religious lecture and it deserves to be said plainly, because the same lecture later tells two unverifiable stories without that flag.

**Practical implication.** ⚠ *(my synthesis)* The parenting move buried in [02:31] is the most useful thing in the recap and he passes over it quickly: **your child's worth is not something you confer, and therefore not something you can withdraw.** Al-Mizan's distinction at 17:70 makes this precise — *takrim*, honouring, is «معنى نفسي», intrinsic to the human being; *tafdil*, preferment, is «معنى إضافي», comparative. A parent who praises a child into worth has taught him that worth is comparative and can be lost. A parent who *discloses* to a child a dignity Allah already gave him has taught him something no sibling, classmate or algorithm can take away. Abbas at seven is exactly the age at which this distinction starts to bite.

### The "enemies of mankind" framing — a caution [02:31–04:35]

⚠ Two things need separating here. **The verse is about the messengers' humanity**: both al-Mizan and *Majma' al-Bayan* locate the disbelievers' contempt in the idea that *a human being* could carry divine guidance. The Shaykh widens this into a general thesis that "the enemies of mankind don't believe human beings are as good as they are" — a denial of human dignity as such. That widening is defensible homiletics and it is not in the tafsir. ⚠ And his phrase *"not the regular garden variety kuffar, but those were the enemies of mankind"* [03:34] echoes, almost word for word, an unverified attribution to Ayatollah Khamenei that the shared index already records from Part 2 (on Surat al-Nur 47–50). Two independent lectures, the same unsourced phrase, the same attributed authority.

## Topic 3.2 — Foul speech, and what actually makes a school Islamic [06:37–08:38]

**Main teaching.** A child raised *karim* will not use foul language, and that is a matter of self-definition rather than rule-following: filthy speech is the *sunnah* of the base, and you are raising soldiers of the Imam (aj).

**Evidence used.**

- **«سُنَّةُ اللِّئَامِ قُبْحُ الْكَلَامِ»** — *"the way of the base is ugliness of speech"* [06:37], introduced as "the hadith of the Imam." ⚠ Located and numbered — ***Ghurar al-Hikam* 5550–5551**, Imam 'Ali (a) — but chainless and **ungradeable in principle**, and he drops the paired first half, «سُنَّةُ الْكِرَامِ تَرَادُفُ الْإِنْعَامِ», *the way of the noble is favour upon favour*. That is the half a child can act on. See §6, N1.
- **«لَا تَتَكَلَّمُوا بِالْفُحْشِ»** + *"this is not worthy of us … it is beneath our Shia"* [06:37–07:38]. ❌ **NOT LOCATED with this wording.** The content is thoroughly Imami; the sentence is not one that could be found. ⭐ The text he needs is one he used in Part 14: **Nahj al-Balagha, Khutbah 206** — «إِنِّي أَكْرَهُ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَكُونُوا سَبَّابِينَ», said at Siffin when he heard his own men reviling the Syrians. Same force, real occasion, checkable in a minute. See §6, N2.

### What makes a school Islamic [07:38–08:38]

> **"An Islamic school is not a school where they serve halal pizza … not a school where the majority of the student body are Muslim … or even where Islam is taught as one subject among many. An Islamic school is where the children are going to grow up in an environment that's conducive to Islam. They're living and they're breathing Islam by their interactions with their teachers. … The bare minimum is that public sin is not tolerated or institutionalised."** [07:38–08:38]

✅ This is **his own framing and it is a good one** — a position in educational philosophy, not a citation, and he does not pretend otherwise. ⚠ Two observations for a parent choosing a school. First, the *positive* half of what he says is exactly the *tarbiyah bi'l-qudwah* principle — education by exemplar — and it has an excellent citation available which he does not give: **Nahj al-Balagha, Hikam 73**, «وَلْيَكُنْ تَأْدِيبُهُ بِسِيرَتِهِ قَبْلَ تَأْدِيبِهِ بِلِسَانِهِ» — *let his disciplining be by his conduct before his tongue*. Second, "public sin is not tolerated or institutionalised" is a **threshold test he sets himself**, and it is a negative one; the sentence from Hikam 73 defines an Islamic school better than any list of what a school is not.

**Practical implication.** ⚠ *(my synthesis)* If you are evaluating a school for Abbas, the two questions that follow from this passage are: *what do the adults in this building model when they think no child is watching*, and *what does this institution quietly normalise*. Neither is answerable from a prospectus.

## Topic 3.3 — Goal One: the child who honours a trust [08:38–14:47]

**Main teaching.** *Khiyanah* — treachery, betrayal of a trust — is the first thing a child of *karamah* will not do. The Shaykh's method here is genuinely good: rather than moralising, he **maps the category**, so that a parent and a child can both recognise betrayal in forms they had not classified as betrayal.

He gives three species:

| Species | His term | His example | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Material deposit** | *wadi'ah* | "You place your car at my disposal as an amanah, and I betray that trust" [11:45] | ✅ A real and precise fiqh category, correctly explained |
| **Immaterial deposit** | a secret | "If somebody tells me a secret … that's an immaterial amanah" [12:45] | ✅ Correctly supported — see N6 |
| **Unwritten covenant** | *'uhud* | The neighbour you never signed anything with [14:47] | ✅ The category is right; ❌ his supporting narration is not located |

**Evidence used — and this is where the lecture's central problem sits.**

- ⭐ ❌ **«أَدِّ الْأَمَانَةَ إِلَى مَنِ ائْتَمَنَكَ، وَلَا تَخُنْ مَنْ خَانَكَ»** [09:40], introduced as *"This is from Rasulullah."* This is the anchor of the entire section — the proposition that betrayal is so grave you may not even return it in kind. **The text is not established.** Imam **Ahmad b. Hanbal: *batil***. **Ibn al-Jawzi**: none of its three Companion routes is sound. **Abu Hatim al-Razi: *munkar***. **al-Shafi'i** (via al-Bayhaqi): *laysa bi-thabit*. **Ibn Hazm: *la yasihh***. **Ibn al-Mulaqqin**: six routes, all weak. Only **al-Tirmidhi 1264** dissents with *hasan gharib*. It is Sunni in provenance and **no Imami chain was located**. Full treatment at §6, N3.
  - ⭐ **The ethic survives the hadith intact.** *You do not become a traitor because someone was a traitor to you* is Qur'anic: **Q 5:8**, «وَلَا يَجْرِمَنَّكُمْ شَنَآنُ قَوْمٍ عَلَىٰ أَلَّا تَعْدِلُوا» — *do not let hatred of a people lead you into injustice*. And the *amanah* half is **Q 4:58**, «إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تُؤَدُّوا الْأَمَانَاتِ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا». Nothing is lost by dropping the attribution.
- ⚠ **«الْكَذِبُ وَالْخِيَانَةُ لَيْسَا مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ»** [10:40], correctly attributed to Amir al-Mu'minin (a). Chainless maxim corpus, entry unpinned; **moderately verified**. His gloss is the valuable part: *"a person who has karamah won't try to save themselves by lying to get out of it. They'll take it on the chin and they will own up to their mistakes."* That is a **behavioural definition of dignity a seven-year-old can be held to**.
- ⚠ **«مِنْ أَفْحَشِ الْخِيَانَةِ خِيَانَةُ الْوَدِيعَةِ»** [12:45], "the Imam says." Chainless, entry unpinned; **moderately verified**. ✅ His explanation of *wadi'ah* — "a physical thing given to me to take care of" — is correct fiqh.
- ⭐ ✅ **«إِفْشَاؤُكَ سِرَّ أَخِيكَ خِيَانَةٌ، فَاجْتَنِبْ ذَلِكَ»** [13:46], *"this one is from our Prophet."* ✅ **His attribution is correct.** It is a clause of the **Prophet's (s) *wasiyyah* to Abu Dharr al-Ghifari**, preserved in *Makarim al-Akhlaq* and al-Tusi's *Amali* and gathered into *Bihar al-Anwar* vol. 74. ⚠ He drops the opening clause, «الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ» — *gatherings are held in trust* — which is precisely the principle behind the case study he is about to tell. See §6, N6.
- ⚠ **«سُنَّةُ الْكِرَامِ الْوَفَاءُ بِالْعُهُودِ»** [13:46]. ***Ghurar al-Hikam* 5556–5557**; chainless. ✅ His extension is textually defensible — *'uhud*, covenants, has never meant only signed documents.
- ❌ **"The Prophet is talking and he says that the signs of a *kha'in* are four. One of them he mentions is harming the neighbour"** [14:47]. **NOT LOCATED anywhere.** ⚠ The tell is in his own delivery: he announces four and supplies one. See §6, N8 — and note that the point he wants is superbly documented elsewhere: «مَا زَالَ جِبْرِيلُ يُوصِينِي بِالْجَارِ حَتَّى ظَنَنْتُ أَنَّهُ سَيُوَرِّثُهُ» (Bukhari 6014 / Muslim 2624, ***sahih***), with al-Kafi 2, *bab haqq al-jiwar*, on the Imami side.

**Practical implication.** ✅ The taxonomy is the transferable part and it survives the sourcing problems completely. A child can be taught, concretely, that there are **three kinds of thing people hand you**: objects, information, and expectations nobody wrote down. Betraying any of the three is the same act. That framing turns "don't tell tales" and "give it back" and "don't be a nuisance to the neighbours" from three unrelated nags into one identity: *we are people who honour what is placed in our hands.*

### Case study: Aziz and his son [15:47–18:49]

Three escalating disclosures from a father to his best friend, and the structure is deliberate — each one maps onto one species of *khiyanah*.

1. **Money missing from the wallet on the windowsill.** Small amounts, repeated. He observes before accusing. When confronted, the boy promises — but "what I feel is that he's just making promises. He's trying to get out of it, get the situation over so he can go on with his life." ⚠ Note the diagnostic: not the theft, but the **quality of the promise**. That is the *la'im* mindset the Shaykh defined at [17:48]: *"The la'im doesn't honour their commitments. They want to find a way out of it. They'll give their word if that's what it takes."*
2. **The school calls.** He is taking erasers and pencils from classmates, and denying it when asked. The father's line — *"this one made me wish that the earth would open up and swallow me right in it"* [16:47] — and the detail that kills him: *"these are things I give him stuff … we give him money."* The theft is not about need.
3. ⭐ **The household broadcast.** *"Things that were supposed to be in the house … my son, in order to get popular with his friends, will talk about what goes on in my house."* And then the sentence that ends the case study: **"It's gotten so bad, my wife and I don't feel safe around him."** [17:48]

✅ This is the strongest teaching sequence in the lecture. The third disclosure is the one that lands, and it lands because he has already built the category of the immaterial *amanah*. ⭐ It is also the exact application of the clause he omitted from the Abu Dharr *wasiyyah*: **«الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ»** — *gatherings are held in trust*. Restore the clause and the case study acquires its own hadith.

⚠ **One caution I would attach for a parent.** The lecture presents this trajectory — wallet → classmates → household secrets — as a single condition with a single cause (absent *karamah*). Childhood stealing is genuinely multi-causal; it is also, in a seven-year-old, developmentally common and not necessarily a character verdict. Treat the framework as a lens, not a diagnosis.

### The eighteen paramedics [18:49–20:49]

An operation is coming; a hundred volunteer medics are told only eighteen can go; they will not stand down; the commanders settle it with a sprint; the eighteen fastest are taken and **"the other guys burst into tears."**

- ✅ **The person is real and correctly named.** **Shahid Ibrahim Hadi**, Tehrani volunteer of the Iran–Iraq war, body never recovered; his memoir-biography **«سلام بر ابراهیم»** (*Peace Be On Ibrahim*) was Iran's best-selling book of 2015, and Ayatollah Khamenei praised it publicly in April 2018.
- ❌ **The story itself is NOT LOCATED** in any checkable source. If it exists it is in the memoir volumes, which are collections of comrades' recollections rather than documentary history. **Treat it as *majlis* material** and say so when you retell it.
- ⚠ **Two delivery errors.** He **inverts the numbers twice** within thirty seconds — "we can't take all 18 of you, we only need 100" [19:49] — meaning the reverse. And he says the operation was *"the same operation … where he became a martyr, especially Sayed Hassan"* [18:49], splicing a salutation for Sayyid Hasan Nasrallah (killed 2024) into a 1980s war anecdote. Harmless aloud; confusing on the page.

**Practical implication.** ✅ The point he draws — *"the karim is the person for whom their word is bond"* — is sound and the image is memorable. Use it as an illustration, introduced as *"as his comrades relate in his biography."*

## Topic 3.4 — Goal Two: the gaze, and the child your words can no longer reach [20:49–23:54]

**Main teaching.** Controlling the gaze is the second return on *karamah*. But the reason he gives for prioritising it is not the obvious one, and it is the most important *tarbiyah* claim in the lecture:

> **"Part of the reason why parents struggle to be effective when they're raising their children … is that if the child has already been polluted by haram looks, the words of the parents, the efforts of the parents to fix the child don't have as much effect as if that child was a child who stayed away from these things."** [21:49–22:53]

In other words: this is not primarily a chastity argument. It is an argument about **the parent's remaining influence**. Sin does not only incur a penalty; it degrades the child's receptivity to correction — and therefore degrades the parent's leverage on everything else.

**Evidence used.**

- ✅ **Qur'an 24:30–31**, cited by number and in the right order [21:49]. "Now the Quran addresses men first" — correct: 24:30 is *qul li'l-mu'minina yaghuddu min absarihim*, 24:31 is *wa qul li'l-mu'minati yaghdudna min absarihinna*. ✅ **Strongly verified**, including the numbering. ⚠ Two caption-level slips only: the women's verb is *yaghdudna* (يَغْضُضْنَ), which the ASR renders "yaghudna." al-Mizan's lexical points are worth having and he does not give them — see §5.
- ✅ **«الْعَيْنَانِ زِنَاهُمَا النَّظَرُ»** — *"the zina of the eye is the look"* [21:49], "the Prophet has told us." ✅ **Strongly verified**: **Bukhari 6612 / Muslim 2657b**, *muttafaq 'alayh*. ⚠ But it is **Sunni-canonical**, given to a Shia audience without saying so, and there is an Imami twin that is more vivid: «النَّظْرَةُ سَهْمٌ مِنْ سِهَامِ إِبْلِيسَ مَسْمُومٌ» — *the look is a poisoned arrow from the arrows of Iblis* — in al-Kafi, *Kitab al-Nikah*, *bab al-nazar*. See §6, N9.
- ⚠ **"Because of how visual men are"** [20:49] — with the qualifier "but even it's a problem for girls too." ✅ **The qualified version is the defensible one.** Rupp & Wallen (2008), *Archives of Sexual Behavior* 37(2):206–218, finds men report greater arousal to visual sexual stimuli, **but** that women show comparable *genital* response and that much of the reported difference tracks stimulus content and context. The folk version ("men are visual, women are not") is not what the literature says; the version he actually gives is.
- ⚠ **"Sin, *ma'siyah*, weakens our willpower"** [22:53], introduced as *"a general rule that we have in tarbiyah."* ✅ **Sound as doctrine, and easy to source** — Q 83:14, «كَلَّا بَلْ رَانَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِم مَّا كَانُوا يَكْسِبُونَ», *rather, what they earned has rusted over their hearts*, with the well-known gloss on the black spot that spreads with each sin (al-Kafi 2, *bab al-dhunub*; Sunni parallel al-Tirmidhi 3334, *hasan*). ⚠ He gives none of it, which makes a Qur'anic doctrine sound like a technique. ❌ **And do not reach for psychology to support it.** The nearest empirical construct is *ego depletion*, and it failed a 23-lab, ~2,000-participant preregistered replication (Hagger et al., *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 2016;11(4):546–573). Habituation making a behaviour easier to repeat is well established; "willpower is a fuel tank that sin drains" is not.

**Practical implication.** ✅ *(his, and it is a real one)* The gaze policy is not a standalone rule to be enforced; it is **maintenance of the channel through which every other instruction travels**. That reframing changes how a parent talks about it — from "you'll be punished for this" to "this is what makes it harder for me to help you."

⚠ *(mine)* And it has a corollary he does not draw: the same logic applies to **the parent**. If habituation degrades receptivity, the father who has not disciplined his own eyes has degraded his own. §8 turns this into a practice.

## Topic 3.5 — The spine: three signs of *karam*, and the failure of parental controls [23:54–26:57]

**Main teaching.** Here is the hinge of the whole lecture. The gaze cannot be policed forever — "how long are we going to be able to be next to our children? … We're going to hover over them?" [23:54] — therefore the solution must be internal. And the internal solution has a name and a text.

> ⭐ **«ثَلَاثَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى كَرَمِ الْمَرْءِ: حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ، وَكَظْمُ الْغَيْظِ، وَغَضُّ الطَّرْفِ»**
> *"Three things indicate a man's nobility: good character, swallowing anger, and lowering the gaze."*
> — Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (a), *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* p.319. Quoted at [23:54], reprised at [41:05].

**Evidence used.**

- ⭐ ✅ **The narration is real, correctly attributed, and now pinned to a page.** *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* of Ibn Shu'ba al-Harrani, **p. 319**, in the section of Imam al-Sadiq's (a) short maxims. He is right that it is al-Sadiq (a), right that there are three, and right about all three items. ⚠ **It carries no chain and cannot carry one** — *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* omits every isnad in the book by its compiler's declared method. He presents it as "This is now hadith," which is true in the loose sense and misleading in the technical one. ⚠ The Arabic is *ghadd al-**tarf***, not *ghadd al-basar*; he says *ghadd al-basar* at [24:54] and then pivots to Q 24:30, where the Qur'anic word is *absar*. The maxim and the verse **converge**; the maxim does not quote the verse. Full entry at §6, N10.
- ⭐ ⚠ **On the reprise at [41:05] he loses the thread.** "There are three things which indicate if a person has Karamah. The first one that the Imam mentions he says—" and the sentence is abandoned. On the second pass the three items are never restated. If you are studying from the audio, the only complete statement of the lecture's spine is at [23:54].

**Why the three hang together — a point he does not make.** ⚠ *(my synthesis, and I think it is the most useful thing in the lecture)* All three signs are forms of **self-restraint under pressure**: restraint of speech and manner (*husn al-khuluq*), restraint of anger (*kazm al-ghayz*), restraint of the eyes (*ghadd al-tarf*). None is an act of piety in the devotional sense; all three are acts of **self-possession**. That is precisely why *karamah* can be the upstream cause: a person who knows he is valuable does not spend himself cheaply. Nahj al-Balagha **Hikmah 449** states the mechanism outright — «مَنْ كَرُمَتْ عَلَيْهِ نَفْسُهُ هَانَتْ عَلَيْهِ شَهْوَتُهُ» — and it is verified. Pair the two texts and the argument of Parts 14 and 15 is complete in two sentences.

### "Parental controls are a failed experiment" [24:54] — his best-evidenced claim

> **"What I'll do is I'll give my children a device, but then I'm so clever, I'm going to put controls on it. So I'll see what they see. … Children are a lot smarter than we think. That solution — that's a failed experiment."** [24:54]

⭐ ✅ **Strongly verified, and better supported than he can have known.** **Przybylski & Nash (2018)**, *Internet Filtering and Adolescent Exposure to Online Sexual Material*, *Cyberpsychology, Behavior and Social Networking* (PMID 29995533) — an EU sample of **n = 13,176** plus a **preregistered** UK replication (n = 1,004) — found filtering's association with encountering online sexual material "inconsistent and practically insignificant," and calculated that a very large number of households would need filters in place to prevent a single adolescent's exposure.

⚠ **The qualification that keeps it honest.** **Collier et al. (2016)**, a meta-analysis of **57 studies** of parental media mediation, puts **restrictive mediation at r+ = −.06** — small, but real and in the protective direction — and, strikingly, **co-viewing at r+ = +.09**, i.e. sitting and watching *with* your child is associated with *worse* outcomes. The arm that works is **active, instructive mediation**: talking about content.

✅ **So he is right for the right reason, and right about the remedy** — the rest of the lecture is an argument for the instructive arm. ⚠ "Failed experiment" overstates by a shade; the accurate sentence is *filters buy far less than parents think, and much less than a conversation does*. Note that the accurate version **strengthens** his case, because it makes the *karamah* strategy the evidence-based one and not merely the pious one.

### Case study: Hamza and the Telegram channel [25:55–26:57]

A boy checks his apps before bed and is added to a Telegram channel by friends. He realises within seconds what is being shared there. Then:

> **"One voice is telling him, 'Just get away. Leave.' The other voice is telling him, 'Well, let's see what it is.' And then he remembers Allah and who he is. 'You're a soldier of the Imam.' And he hates himself for this weakness. … He himself leaves the channel, and he goes to bed. And he feels a sense of victory."** [25:55]

✅ **The best-designed illustration in the lecture**, because it is the only one that shows the mechanism working *in the absence of the parent*. Three features are worth naming for a parent: the battle lasts **seconds**, not hours; the decisive move is **identity retrieval** ("who he is"), not rule recall; and the outcome includes **a felt win**, which is what makes the behaviour repeatable. ⚠ It is a constructed illustration, not a report of a real boy — he does not say otherwise, and nothing turns on it.

## Topic 3.6 — Goal Four: *'iffah* and *ghayrah* [26:57–31:59]

**Main teaching.** Two things come as a package with *karamah*: *'iffah* (chastity/modesty, which he pairs with *haya*) and *ghayrah* (protective jealousy). He explicitly frames these as **weapons given to the child in a contest**, not as restrictions imposed on her: "if we can raise our children with karamah, we'll have given them the two powerful weapons" [27:57].

⚠ Note the numbering: he calls this "number four" [26:57], having last said "step number two" at [20:49]. Number three is never announced. My reconstruction is that the gaze material and the *karamah*-as-inner-solution material at [23:54] were meant to be two separate goals.

**Evidence used.**

- ⚠ **«الْحَيَاءُ تَمَامُ الْكَرَمِ وَأَحْسَنُ الشِّيَمِ»** [28:57], Amir al-Mu'minin (a) — *"modesty is the completion of nobility and the best of dispositions."* ✅ Wording, attribution and his translation all accurate. ⚠ Chainless maxim corpus; **entry not pinned**. Note *shiyam*, plural of *shimah*, an **ingrained disposition** — a well-chosen word for a lecture about character that forms rather than character that is enforced.
- ⚠ **«الْحَيَاءُ سَبَبٌ إِلَى كُلِّ جَمِيلٍ»** [28:57] — *"modesty is a cause of everything beautiful."* ✅ Accurate. ⚠ Chainless, entry unpinned, and the **third maxim in a row delivered as "hadith."** ⭐ The graded alternative sits in al-Kafi 2, *Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr*, ***bab al-haya***: «الْحَيَاءُ مِنَ الْإِيمَانِ، وَالْإِيمَانُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ» — chains instead of maxims, for the theme this lecture leans on hardest after *karam*.
- ✅ **Nahj al-Balagha, Hikmah 124** [29:59] — «غَيْرَةُ الْمَرْأَةِ كُفْرٌ، وَغَيْرَةُ الرَّجُلِ إِيمَانٌ», *a woman's jealousy is kufr; a man's jealousy is faith*. ⭐ **This is the most delicate text in the lecture and he handles it well** — see the sub-section below.

### The *ghayrah* problem, and the citation he never gives [29:59]

He raises Hikmah 124 **unprompted**, which is to his credit, and immediately restricts it:

> **"We talk about women not being supposed to be having ghayrah, cuz that's in Nahj al-Balagha. But that's a different type of ghayrah than what I'm talking about over here. That's ghayrah [when] the husband is trying to marry again. That ghayrah, no. This kind of ghayrah is for everybody."** [29:59]

✅ **The restriction is the mainstream Imami reading** and the commentators are with him — Ibn Abi'l-Hadid, Muhammad 'Abduh, and among the moderns Makarim Shirazi's *Payam-e Imam* all read the *ghayrah* of Hikmah 124 as jealousy that **objects to what the Shari'ah has permitted**, which is why it can be called *kufr*: it is objection to a divine ruling, not an emotion.

⭐ **But the restriction has a textual basis he does not cite, and it would convert an assertion into a report.** **al-Kafi vol. 5, *Kitab al-Nikah*, chapter 178** is a one-hadith chapter **whose title is the hadith**: «**لَا غَيْرَةَ فِي الْحَلَالِ**» — *there is no ghayrah in what is lawful*. Al-Kulayni gave the restriction a chapter heading eleven centuries ago. Cite it. See §6, N14.

⚠ **And there is a limb he omits that a parent raising teenagers needs.** A lecture recommending *ghayrah* must also carry the brake on it: **al-Kafi 5/3/177 h.9** and **Nahj al-Balagha, Letter 31** both condemn *misplaced* ghayrah, warning that suspicion drives a sound woman toward sickness and an innocent one toward the very thing she was suspected of. Recommending vigilance without that counterweight is how *ghayrah* becomes surveillance.

### ❌ The captured assassin spared for five traits [30:59]

> **"These terrorists, these Daesh-type terrorists, were captured. Apparently they had planned to kill Rasulullah. … All of them were executed except one. … Rasulullah said, 'Jibra'il told me from God … you have five traits that Allah and His Messenger love. The first one, *al-ghayratu shadidatun 'ala haramik*.'"** [30:59]

❌ **NOT LOCATED. Do not repeat this as narration.** Three separate problems, and they compound:

1. **The report is unlocated** — neither the frame (prisoners condemned, one released), nor the five-trait list, nor the *ghayrah* clause could be found in any collection. Absence-checked against the Sunni corpus via dorar.net and against **al-Kafi 5, chapter 177**, the dedicated *ghayrah* chapter, **all nine hadith read**.
2. **He supplies one of five** — "and he mentions the four others" — exactly as with the four signs of a *kha'in* at [14:47]. ⚠ Twice in one lecture an enumerated narration is announced and left unenumerated. That is the signature of material transmitted orally through preaching rather than read from a book, and it is a useful tell for any listener.
3. ⚠ **"Daesh-type terrorists" is his own overlay, and he repeats it five times.** No version of any comparable report frames the Prophet's (s) captives that way. The anachronism does real rhetorical work — it pre-loads the audience's verdict on people whose execution is being described — and it should not be heard as part of the narration.

⭐ **What to use instead.** If the point is that *ghayrah* is a trait God loves, the graded route is in that same al-Kafi chapter 177: «**إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَيُورٌ يُحِبُّ كُلَّ غَيُورٍ**» — *God is ghayur and loves every ghayur*. Prophetic, in the dedicated chapter, and it needs no captives.

**Practical implication.** ✅ The pedagogical move — *modesty framed as a weapon you are given rather than a cage you are put in* — is the right one for a daughter approaching adolescence, and it is the difference between a rule she resents and a stance she owns. ⚠ It only works if the *ghayrah* she sees modelled at home is protective and not suspicious. The omitted counterweight is not optional.

## Topic 3.7 — The two mindsets, side by side [31:59–35:02]

**Main teaching.** He runs the *la'im* and the *karim* through the same two scenarios — the gaze and the hijab — and lets the contrast do the work.

**The *la'im*'s replies, in his voice** [31:59–32:59]:

> **"You really think Allah's going to put me in Jahannam for a few glances? I want to enjoy myself. … If you don't look, I'm worried maybe you'll get psychological issues."**
> **"Really? Is that big a deal? A little nail polish, a little makeup. You see what everybody else is dressing like? A few hairs, Allah's going to send me to Jahannam? I want to dress like this. I don't care. I like this."**

**The *karim*'s response** [32:59]: *"All it takes is a reminder for the karim to start observing."*

✅ This is well observed. Note precisely what distinguishes the two: not information, and not fear of consequence. Both know the ruling. The *la'im* argues **proportionality** ("is that worth Hell?"); the *karim* does not run the calculation at all, because for him it is not a cost-benefit question but a question of what he is. ⚠ *(my synthesis)* This is the practical payoff of the whole *karamah* thesis and it is worth naming for a child in exactly these terms: **the argument "is it really that bad?" is not a religious question, it is a question about how cheaply you hold yourself.**

**Evidence used.**

- ⚠ **«أَعْقَلُ النَّاسِ أَحْيَاهُمْ»** [32:59], "the hadith tell us this" — *the most intelligent of people are the most modest of them*. ✅ Wording correct and the pun is real: *ahyahum* is from *haya'*, so intelligence and modesty are lexically fused, which is exactly the link he wants between the moral compass and behaviour. ⚠ *Ghurar al-Hikam*, indexed by Rayshahri, *Mizan al-Hikmah* p.2048; chainless; **moderately verified**.
- ✅ **The *wasaya* of the martyrs** [32:59–34:01] — "one of the things that's consistently mentioned again and again by our shuhada … is consistently you see them giving reminders about the hijab." ⚠ Unsourced as a generalisation, but it is a checkable claim about a public literature (the published *wasiyyat-nameh* corpus) and it is not implausible. I could not verify the frequency claim; treat it as his impression.

### Sayyid Hasan Nasrallah on sisters posting photographs [34:01]

> **"May you remember Sayyid Hassan when he addressed the community before he left this world. He said, 'I'm not someone who gives fatawa … but I will share my opinion for those sisters who are on social media … There is no way that it is the school of the [Ahlul] Bayt that our sisters be sharing their pictures.'"** [34:01]

⚠ **Substantially real, framing wrong, wording unverified.**

- ✅ That Nasrallah **publicly opposed women posting their photographs on social media** is corroborated by Arabic press coverage — *al-'Arab* carried it under the headline «نصرالله يعارض وضع النساء صورهن على مواقع التواصل الاجتماعي», and it drew a round of Lebanese commentary at the time. ⚠ **The article body was 403-blocked at the egress proxy**, so the date, occasion and exact sentences could not be recovered.
- ❌ **"Before he left this world" appears to be wrong.** The press cycle around this statement belongs to **2022**. Nasrallah was killed on **27 September 2024**. Presenting a 2022 social remark as a farewell address gives it a weight the record does not support.
- ✅ **The "I am not giving a fatwa, this is my opinion" caveat is to both men's credit** — he preserves it rather than upgrading a personal view into a ruling. That is worth noticing, because upgrading opinions into rulings is the commonest failure mode in this genre.

## Topic 3.8 — Shahid 'Abd al-Husayn Borunsi and the villa [35:02–38:05]

**The person — ✅ verified, once the ASR is decoded.** "Shaheed Abu Abdul Hussein Boronsi" is **عبدالحسین برونسی**, ***'Abd al-Husayn Borunsi***. ⚠ **There is no *Abu*** in his name; the transcript has doubled the first syllable.

| Fact | Status |
|---|---|
| Born **1942**, a village of **Torbat-e Heydariyeh county**, Razavi Khorasan (not Mashhad itself) | ✅ Verified |
| Imprisoned and tortured by SAVAK under the Pahlavis | ✅ Verified |
| Commander of the **Bilal battalion** at Operation Val-Fajr 3; then commandant of the **18th Javad al-A'imma Brigade** | ✅ Verified |
| Killed **14 March 1985, Operation Badr**, east of the Tigris | ✅ Verified |
| Remains recovered **2011** and buried in **Mashhad on the anniversary of Fatima al-Zahra (a)** | ✅ Verified — and this is the **factual kernel** behind the Shaykh's remark about "his special connection with Fatima" [34:01] |
| Biography: **«خاک‌های نرم کوشک»** (*Khak-ha-ye Narm-e Kushk*, "The Soft Soils of Kushk"), first published **2004**, compiled from his wife's and comrades' recollections | ✅ Verified — a **new entry** for the series' list of source books, alongside *Nime-ye Penhan-e Mah* and *Salam bar Ibrahim* |

**The two stories he tells — ❌ both NOT LOCATED.**

1. **The minefield** [34:01] — "literally in a landmine and he got instructions: go 45 steps this way, go 25 that way." ⚠ Not in the encyclopedic record. A *karamah* narrative from the memoir tradition.
2. **The military service under the Shah** [35:02–38:05] — the conscript picked out of the line-up, the sergeant, the villa, the improperly dressed woman, the sweat breaking out, running away repeatedly, the punishment of cleaning **all eighteen latrines** alone for a week, the colonel's taunt, and the answer:

> **"Doing these 18 toilets, that's nothing for me. If you tell me to do more than that … and I have to do this for the rest of my military service, I am not going back in that house. If they kill me, I'm not going back."** [38:05]

⚠ **NOT LOCATED in any checkable source.** The public record of his life records SAVAK imprisonment but no compulsory-service episode. This is memoir material from *Khak-ha-ye Narm-e Kushk*.

⚠ **Why this matters more than it looks.** The story is told with **circumstantial specificity** — eighteen toilets, a week, the wife of a colonel, "go 45 steps this way" — and circumstantial specificity is what makes a listener hear a story as documentary. The honest framing costs one clause: *"as his comrades relate in his biography."* Add the clause and the story keeps all its force and none of its false authority.

✅ **And the teaching point is genuinely good.** He uses it to argue that *ghayrah* is not a rule you obey but a reflex that a dignified person cannot override — "that you see is that ghayrah that comes with karamah." A conscript who would rather clean latrines for a year than re-enter that house has not consulted a ruling; he has consulted himself. That is the whole thesis in one image.

⚠ One aside: at [35:02] he says *"you remember what we learned from Qasim: if you want to become a shahid you have to live like a shahid."* ❌ **NOT LOCATED as a quotation** of Qasim Sulaymani. The sentiment is a commonplace of the Iranian *shahadat* literature; the index already records one unverifiable Sulaymani quotation from earlier in the series. ✅ Mitigating: he does not present it as a direct quotation.

## Topic 3.9 — Goal Five: real akhlaq versus performed akhlaq [38:05–42:05]

**Main teaching.** You can enforce *akhlaqi behaviour*. You cannot enforce *akhlaq*. The difference shows up in exactly one place.

> **"Sometimes I can enforce akhlaqi behaviour. I smile, I show respect, but that doesn't mean that you've installed good akhlaq. … When I'm out with company, I know how to mind my manners and to pretend to be gracious and to be patient. I am furious on the inside, but I smile. … When you see the real me is when I'm home and I'm with my family. That's when the real me comes out."** [39:05–40:05]

✅ **This is the sharpest passage in the lecture and it needs no citation.** He adds a modern instance — *"sometimes you see people are so mean online, right? When they're not [seen]… that's when the real me comes out"* [40:05] — and then the diagnostic:

> **"If you really want to know how my akhlaq is, then test how I am with my family when it doesn't matter. Do the gloves come off? That's when you'll see the real akhlaq."** [40:05]

**Evidence used.**

- ⚠ At [40:05] the ASR carries *"I'm going to read the dua of Saturday"* — `⚠ ASR unclear`; this appears to be a fragment of an aside about a supplication and nothing in the argument depends on it.
- ⭐ ❌ **«مَا كَانَ مِمَّا خَلَقَ اللهُ شَيْءٌ أَحْسَنُ مِنْهُ» / «مَا كَانَ مِمَّا خَلَقَ اللهُ شَيْءٌ أَقْبَحُ مِنْهُ»** [41:05–42:05], introduced as *"the Prophet says this"* — if *husn al-khuluq* were a creation you could see, nothing Allah created would be more beautiful; if bad *akhlaq* were a creation you could see, nothing would be more hideous.
  **NOT LOCATED with this wording. Do not attribute it to the Prophet (s).** dorar.net's phrase search returns nothing; the Shia topical compilation *al-Khuluq fi aqwal al-Nabi Muhammad wa Ali Baytih* (43 entries) contains nothing matching; two further Arabic phrase searches returned nothing carrying the double comparative.
  ⚠ **The nearest attested text says considerably less**: «لَوْ كَانَ حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ رَجُلًا يَمْشِي فِي النَّاسِ لَكَانَ رَجُلًا صَالِحًا» — *if good character were a man walking among people he would be a righteous man* — graded ***da'if*** to ***da'if jiddan*** by the Sunni critics, with **no ugliness clause at all**.
  ⭐ **This matters more than its length suggests**, because it is the **hinge into the near-death sequence** that follows. The whole "bad akhlaq is objectively hideous, and someone has *seen* it" movement rests on it. See §6, N17.
  ⭐ **Substitute:** al-Kafi 2, ***bab su' al-khuluq***: «إِنَّ سُوءَ الْخُلُقِ لَيُفْسِدُ الْعَمَلَ كَمَا يُفْسِدُ الْخَلُّ الْعَسَلَ» — *bad character spoils deeds as vinegar spoils honey*. Vivid, graded, Imami, and it says what he wants.

**Practical implication.** ✅ Adopt the home test and apply it to yourself before your children. ⚠ *(mine)* And notice the trap in it: a parent who performs patience in public and loses it at home is not merely inconsistent, he is running the exact demonstration his children will copy — which is Part 2's *tarbiyah bi'l-mushahadah* thesis arriving from a new direction.

## Topic 3.10 — The near-death account of Behrouz Azimi [42:05–45:05]

**What he does.** He introduces near-death experiences, says "the leader says these are real," recalls a narration from Imam al-Baqir (a) mentioned in an earlier session, and then — carefully — twice marks the limits of the genre:

> **"Near-death experiences, let's keep this in mind: this is not the akhirah. … Not even a hundredth of what the akhirah is."** [43:05]

Then the account. ***Zendegi Pas az Zendegi*** ("Life After Life"), an Iranian television programme; a guest named **Behrouz Azimi**; a man who will not raise his head outdoors, five years after the event, and always wears a cap — to the point that the presenter flew a drone directly in front of his face and the man still did not look up. In the experience he saw a creature of overwhelming hideousness, hoofed, which raised a hoof, pointed at him and asked: **"Why were you so bad *akhlaq* to Fariba?"** — his wife.

**Verification.**

- ✅ **The programme is real.** *Zendegi Pas az Zendegi*, **IRIB Channel 4**, built on interviews with *tajrobe-gar*-s (experiencers).
- ✅ **The person is real and the identification is exact.** **Behrouz Azimi (بهروز عظیمی)**, **season 2, episode 21, 1400 sh (2021)**, with the episode and follow-up interviews archived on Aparat.
- ✅ **The account is corroborated in outline.** Tasnim covered it under a headline that matches his retelling almost point for point — "the experiencer finally raised his head to the sky / the story of encountering a terrifying creature in temporary death" — and circulating clips of the episode are titled «عاقبت بداخلاقی», *the consequence of bad character*, which matches the *akhlaq* framing he gives it.
- ⚠ **The fine detail rests on his retelling.** The Tasnim page itself was robots-blocked at the egress proxy, so the hooves, the white hair, the drone, the wife's name and the five dogs could not be independently confirmed.
- ⭐ ✅ **Credit where it is due:** in a series where contemporary stories frequently could not be traced at all, **he is not inventing this, and the identification is exact.**

**Two cautions, one of which he supplies himself.**

- ✅ **He says twice that this is not the akhirah.** Good discipline, and it should be kept in any retelling.
- ❌ **"The leader says these are real"** [42:05] — **NOT VERIFIED.** No statement of Ayatollah Khamenei affirming the reality of near-death experiences could be located. ⚠ **Critically: english.khamenei.ir and leader.ir are 403-blocked at the egress proxy and farsi.khamenei.ir is robots-blocked**, so this is an **unreachable archive, not a completed negative search.** It is nonetheless the **second unverified attribution to the Leader in this single lecture** (the first being the tafsir of 64:6), and the third if the Mahmoud Kaveh quotation is counted.
- ⚠ **The deeper problem is structural, not factual.** The account is being used as *evidence for the content of N17* — an unlocated hadith. A televised personal testimony is not a source for a doctrinal claim about what bad character looks like in the next world. And note the slide in the framing: N17, even if it existed, licenses only a **thought experiment** ("if it were a creation we *could* see"); the Azimi account is offered as a **report** that someone *did* see it. Those are different claims.

**Practical implication.** ⚠ *(mine)* This is a section to handle with care around children. The moral — that bad character is not a minor blemish but something genuinely ugly — is sound and well supported by al-Kafi's vinegar-and-honey narration. The hooved creature is not something a seven-year-old needs, and the *akhirah* is not a horror story. Take the moral; leave the monster.

### Case study: Haydar [45:05–47:06]

> **"Haydar is not even like a little boy anymore. He's more like a pitbull. This little boy is so ferocious and so angry … that his parents are really literally worried about him."** [45:05]

The sequence: he comes home, sees his little sister **Zahra** drawing with his coloured pencils, rushes over — *"THOSE AREN'T YOURS!"* — snatches them, takes her paper and rips it, before she has even seen him coming. His mother, "almost getting afraid of this little kid," separates them into the kitchen. He smells lasagna and erupts again — *"I TOLD YOU I DON'T LIKE LASAGNA. WHY ARE YOU MAKING LASAGNA?"* — and she tells him it is for his father. And then the detail that makes the case study:

> **"The little boy, now that he's realised he made a mistake, he doesn't apologise. He arrogantly goes off into his room and he slams the door."** [46:06]

> **"The wife in this story, she blames herself and she blames her husband. She remembers those days when they were warned by the sheikh that if you raise a child [where] you're always talking down to him, it's going to come back to bite you."** [47:06]

✅ **What is excellent here:** the diagnostic weight falls not on the outburst but on **the refusal to repair**. A child who is wrong and knows it and cannot say so is a child for whom admitting error is a threat to his standing — which is precisely the *la'im* profile the lecture has been building since [10:40] ("a person who has karamah won't try to save themselves… they'll own up"). The behaviour and the theory line up.

⚠ **Where to be careful, and this is important for a real parent.** The case resolves **entirely** onto how the parents spoke to him. The clinical cluster he is describing — angry/irritable mood, argumentative and defiant behaviour, and vindictiveness — is recognisably the DSM-5 picture of **oppositional defiant disorder**, and ODD is **multiply determined**, with substantial temperamental and heritable components. Parenting style is one well-evidenced contributor; it is not the only one. Telling a parent that their child's defiance is purely the product of how they spoke to him is powerful pastoral leverage and, in a subset of families, false and cruel. See §13.

## Topic 3.12 — Goal Six: the child who does not carry a grudge [49:06–52:08]

**Main teaching.** The last return on *karamah* is the capacity to let go. He grounds it in a Qur'anic supplication and then, unusually for this lecture, spends most of the section on **the diagnosis of its absence** rather than the virtue itself.

**Evidence used.**

- ✅ **Qur'an 59:10** [49:06] — «رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا». ✅ **Strongly verified.** He gives no reference; the reference is Surat al-Hashr, verse 10. His rendering is accurate. al-Mizan glosses *ghill* plainly: «**والغل العداوة**» — *ghill* is **enmity**. ⚠ One honest caveat for a Shia reader, in §5: al-Mizan reads the verse as describing a **specific historical cohort** (those who believed after the cessation of hijrah following the conquest). Using it as a family supplication against carrying grudges is a legitimate application of a general moral — but it is an application. Say "the Qur'an teaches us to ask this," not "this verse is about your household."
- ⚠ **«سَبَبُ الْفِتَنِ الْحِقْدُ»** [50:07], "the hadith says" — *the cause of seditions is rancour*. Imam 'Ali (a); chainless maxim corpus, entry unpinned; **moderately verified**. ✅ The deployment is good — he uses it to move from the verse to the household, arguing that a society is made of families and that carried grudges are where *fitnah* starts. ⚠ Worth knowing: *fitan* in Amir al-Mu'minin's usage means **civil strife**, not family friction. The application to a household is an analogy, and a defensible one, but the maxim is about the polity.
- ⚠ **«لَيْسَ مِنْ شِيَمِ الْكِرَامِ تَعْجِيلُ الِانْتِقَامِ»** [52:08] — he paraphrases without quoting: *"those people who are low, those people who don't feel good about themselves, one of the signs is that I want to punish and I'm quick to punish if somebody does something wrong."* ✅ **The content is exactly right** and the Arabic exists: ***Ghurar al-Hikam* 7490**. ⚠ He gives no source, so a listener cannot check him.

### The behavioural signs of a resentful child [50:07–52:08]

He lists them, and the observation is genuinely useful:

1. **Deliberate stubbornness** — "the child wants to find a way to get back at their parents, to punish their parents… sometimes it can get so bad, the child will deal with the punishment of the parent because I enjoy seeing them angry."
2. ⭐ **Disobedience as a weapon**, with the reframe that is the best practical insight in the section:
   > **"I'm just waiting for my parents to tell me to do something and then I'm going to do the opposite … The parent doesn't know this. The parent's like, 'You're being disobedient,' so then I'm repeating the commands, I'm shouting. What I don't know is, for the person who's getting revenge, every time you tell me 'do this,' that's another time of me punching back."** [51:08–52:08]
3. **Hastening to revenge** — the child who is quick to punish.
4. **Wanting to see the parents flounder.**

✅ **Clinicians would recognise this** as the coercive-cycle model (Patterson), and the reframe is sound: in a coercive cycle, **repetition of the command is not pressure, it is fuel.** A parent who understands this stops escalating and changes register instead. That is a real, actionable insight, and it is the reason this section earns its place.

⚠ **The causal claim is where to be careful**, and it is the same caution as at Haydar: he asserts that this "happens when a child is raised to not have that deep sense of how worthy I am to God." One contributor is being presented as the cause.

### Case study: the honest mother and the Shaykh's four questions [52:08–56:11]

A mother comes to the Shaykh. Her son disobeys everything; worse, he is **contrary** — *"if I don't take him with me, then he'll say 'how come you don't take me out?' If I do, 'I don't want to go.'"* Then it escalates to property: her husband finds two tyres flat and has to take an Uber to work; she comes back inside and finds her son **smiling**. *"He told me: I enjoy it when you guys are angry."* Then the remote control, deliberately dropped into a pitcher of water.

The Shaykh stops taking symptoms — *"Look, I've got the picture"* — and asks four questions instead. This is the pedagogical high point of the lecture:

| # | The question, as he puts it [54:10–56:11] | Her answer | ⚠ Does it have Islamic warrant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"When was the last time you asked your son for his opinion on something — and then you went with his opinion?"** | *"I don't really remember."* | ✅ Yes — *mushawarah* is Qur'anic (3:159, 42:38), and the *tarbiyah* application is standard |
| 2 | **"When he makes a mistake, do you correct him in public or in private?"** | *"We just give him the business right there in front of people."* | ✅ Yes — **Nahj al-Balagha, Hikmah 222**, «مِنْ أَشْرَفِ أَعْمَالِ الْكَرِيمِ غَفْلَتُهُ عَمَّا يَعْلَمُ» (*taghaful*), plus the Prophet's (s) practice of correcting «مَا بَالُ أَقْوَامٍ» — naming the fault, not the person |
| 3 | **"Do you use the name he would like to be called by, or is it harsh even when you call his name?"** | *"We're harsh."* | ❌ The shared index records **"call people by their favourite name" as NOT LOCATED** as a saying. The Prophet's (s) *kunya* practice is real; the maxim is not. ✅ **He does not quote it as a hadith here**, which is an improvement on an earlier lecture |
| 4 | **"Are your instructions orders, or are you consulting with him to bring him along? And if orders — arrogant orders, or 'yes sir, no sir'?"** | *"It's 'no sir, yes sir.'"* | ⚠ No classical source; his own observation, and a sensible one |

> **"By the time the Sheikh finished asking the questions … she's completely clear. This child has never felt that sense of honour inside their house."** [56:11]

⭐ ✅ **This is the lecture's best five minutes.** The four questions are concrete, answerable in thirty seconds each, and every one of them measures the same variable: whether the child is treated as a person with standing inside his own home. Three of the four have real Imami warrant, and he would be stronger citing it. §9 turns them into a self-audit for Tafseer and his wife.

⚠ **And the same caution applies a third time.** The case study, like Haydar, resolves entirely onto the mother's conduct. In the pastoral setting that is often the right move, because it is the only variable the parent controls. On the page, it should be labelled as a working hypothesis rather than a finding.

## Topic 3.13 — Hastening to pardon, and the closing litmus test [56:11–58:14]

**Main teaching.** The *karim*'s response to injury is not neutrality but **initiative** — moving first toward pardon — and beyond that, actively returning good for harm.

**Evidence used.**

- ⚠ **«الْمُبَادَرَةُ إِلَى الْعَفْوِ مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ»** [56:11] — *"rushing to forgive is among the traits of the noble."* ✅ Sense exactly right, and this maxim is what the whole closing section is built on. ⚠ ***Ghurar al-Hikam* 1566–1567**; chainless; **moderately verified**. ⚠ **One wording correction worth making**: the attested verb is **مُبَادَرَة** (*mubadarah*, "taking the initiative toward"), not *musara'ah* ("hastening"). Minor — except that *mubadarah* is the better word for a parenting lecture, because it means being the **first to move**, not merely being quick.
- ✅ **"Twice the Prophet is told to meet a bad deed with a good deed"** [57:13]. ⭐ **His count is exactly right, and this deserves saying plainly** because it is the kind of claim that usually turns out to be loose. The Qur'an gives this instruction in four places. In **two** — **23:96** and **41:34** — it is a **singular imperative, ٱدْفَعْ, addressed to the Prophet (s)**. In the other two — **13:22** and **28:54** — it is a **third-person plural description of believers**, وَيَدْرَءُونَ. All four verified directly. See §5.
  - ⚠ A lexical detail he does not mention but which is worth having: 23:96 and 41:34 use **دفع** (*idfa'*, "push away"); 13:22 and 28:54 use **درء** (*yadra'un*, "ward off, avert"). The same manoeuvre, described from the Prophet's (s) side and from ours.
- ✅ **The payoff clause** [57:13] — *"it'll turn those people who have enmity towards us … around"* — is 41:34's own second clause: «فَإِذَا الَّذِي بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُ عَدَاوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُ وَلِيٌّ حَمِيمٌ». ⚠ And al-Mizan attaches an explicit condition at 41:35 that he does not give: **only those who are patient are granted this**, and it belongs to «ذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍ», one with «نصيب وافر من كمال الإنسانية» — an abundant share of human perfection. That qualification **strengthens** his point rather than weakening it: repelling evil with good is presented in the Qur'an itself as rare and effortful, not as an easy virtue.
- ⭐ ⚠ **«مَنْ لَمْ يُجَازِ الْإِسَاءَةَ بِالْإِحْسَانِ فَلَيْسَ مِنَ الْكِرَامِ»** [57:13] — *"the litmus test for self-building from Rasulullah. The Prophet says this…"*
  ❌ **MISATTRIBUTED.** This is ***Ghurar al-Hikam* 8958 — Imam 'Ali (a)**, not the Prophet (s). The correction is firm and it is not a close call: the entry number is pinned, and **in Part 16 he cites the very same maxim and attributes it correctly to Imam 'Ali (a).** Same speaker, same sentence, two lectures, two different attributions.
  ⚠ He also calls it a "litmus test," which is a strong reading of a chainless maxim. ✅ To his credit he immediately concedes how demanding it is. ⭐ **And the Qur'anic anchor for the identical principle is far stronger ground and he already has it in hand: 41:34.** Lead with the verse; use the maxim as illustration.
- ⭐ ⚠ **«مَكَارِمُ الْأَخْلَاقِ»** [58:14] — *"the Prophet advising Amir al-Mu'minin. What's makarim al-akhlaq? One, you give the person who denied you; two, you reconnect with the person who cut you off; three, you forgive the person who oppressed you."*
  **The narration is real; he has taken the weaker recension.** The three-item version addressed to 'Ali (a) exists — but only in the **Sunni** chains (al-Bayhaqi, al-Tabarani), and it is graded ***da'if***. The best-graded **Imami** text is **al-Kafi vol. 2, *Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr*, chapter 53, hadith 1**, al-Majlisi in *Mir'at al-'Uqul*: ***hasan ka'l-sahih***. In that text the Prophet (s) is **addressing a public gathering**, not 'Ali (a), and he gives **four** items — the fourth being **that you speak the truth even against yourself**.
  ⭐ **The cost of the wrong recension is exact and it is severe for this particular lecture.** The lost fourth item — *truthfulness even against yourself* — is the thread that ties the closing section back to the opening case study of the boy who lies and steals, and back to N4's gloss that "a person of karamah won't try to save themselves by lying." **al-Kafi hands him the connection between his first section and his last, and he drops it.** This is the single easiest upgrade available in the lecture. See §6, N22.

### The closing note [58:14]

He ends not with an exhortation but with a pastoral worry, and it is a good one:

> **"Sometimes these things, when people hear about them, it seems so far from where I am that it's hard for me to think of myself as a good Muslim … Sometimes what happens is either people despair, or they start to reinvent Islam so that I can feel good about myself — instead of keeping these Islamic ideals and saying that we have to work towards the Islamic ideals."** [58:14]

✅ **The distinction is exactly right and it is the correct place to end a lecture of this kind.** Two failure modes, both real: despair, and the quiet lowering of the standard until it fits what you already do. The third option he names — keep the ideal, admit the distance, and walk — is the classical Imami position. ⚠ The recording ends here; the *makarim al-akhlaq* material is left unfinished, and the series map indicates Part 16 completes it.

# 4. Key Takeaways to Remember — Top 10

**1. Dignity is upstream; behaviour is downstream.**
*Summary:* Every behaviour a parent enforces in the second seven years — honesty, promise-keeping, clean speech, the gaze, modesty, temper, forgiveness — is a symptom of one underlying variable: whether the child knows how valuable he is to Allah [23:54].
*Why it matters:* It reallocates parental effort from a dozen enforcement battles to one formation project.
*Parenting implication:* Before adding another rule, ask what this week did to Abbas's sense of his own worth in this house.

**2. Your window is temporary, and you know it.**
*Summary:* "How long are we going to be able to be next to our children? … Right now we have this window" [23:54].
*Why it matters:* It makes the internal solution not the ideal option but the only one with a future.
*Parenting implication:* Judge every parenting tactic by one question — *does this still work when I am not in the room?*

**3. Betrayal has three forms, and children only recognise one.**
*Summary:* Material deposits (*wadi'ah*), immaterial deposits (secrets), and unwritten covenants (the neighbour) [11:45–14:47].
*Why it matters:* Most children understand "don't steal" and have no category at all for "don't broadcast what happens in this house."
*Parenting implication:* Teach the taxonomy explicitly. **«الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ»** — *gatherings are held in trust* — from the Prophet's (s) *wasiyyah* to Abu Dharr, is the sentence for it.

**4. The quality of a child's promise is the diagnostic, not the offence.**
*Summary:* Aziz's real alarm was not the missing money but that his son's promises were exit strategies [16:47].
*Why it matters:* It shifts attention from the act to the character forming behind it.
*Parenting implication:* When a child promises, notice whether he is *committing* or *escaping*. Those need completely different responses.

**5. Three signs of *karam*, and all three are self-restraint.**
*Summary:* Good character, swallowing anger, lowering the gaze — Imam al-Sadiq (a), *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* p.319 [23:54].
*Why it matters:* It is a **behavioural** definition of dignity, which means it is teachable and observable.
*Parenting implication:* These are the three things to watch for in a seven-year-old. Not piety displays — restraint under pressure.

**6. Sin degrades your leverage, not just the child's record.**
*Summary:* "If the child has already been polluted by haram looks, the words of the parents … don't have as much effect" [22:53].
*Why it matters:* It reframes the gaze from a private sin into a public parenting problem.
*Parenting implication:* Protect the channel, not just the child. ⚠ And notice this applies to the parent's own eyes and the parent's own credibility.

**7. Parental controls buy far less than you think — and conversation buys far more.**
*Summary:* Filters show "inconsistent and practically insignificant" effects (Przybylski & Nash 2018, n = 13,176 + preregistered n = 1,004) [24:54].
*Why it matters:* ⚠ Restrictive mediation is not *zero* (Collier et al. 2016, r+ = −.06) — but **co-viewing is worse than nothing** (r+ = +.09), and the arm that works is active, instructive mediation.
*Parenting implication:* Keep the filter; stop trusting it. Budget the saved confidence into talking about what he sees.

**8. Real akhlaq is what happens at home when nothing is at stake.**
*Summary:* "Test how I am with my family when it doesn't matter. Do the gloves come off?" [40:05].
*Why it matters:* It is a test you can run on yourself today, and it is unfakeable.
*Parenting implication:* Run it on yourself before you run it on Abbas. Your children are the control group for your own character.

**9. Repeating the command is fuel, not pressure.**
*Summary:* For a child using disobedience as revenge, each repetition of an order is another opportunity to punish you [51:08].
*Why it matters:* It explains why escalation reliably fails with exactly the children on whom parents escalate hardest.
*Parenting implication:* When repetition is making it worse, stop repeating and change register. The problem is not compliance; it is standing.

**10. Four questions diagnose a household faster than any behaviour chart.**
*Summary:* When did you last take his opinion? Do you correct him in public or private? Do you use the name he likes? Are your instructions orders or consultation? [54:10–56:11].
*Why it matters:* All four measure the same thing — whether the child has standing in his own home — and three have real Imami warrant.
*Parenting implication:* Answer all four honestly this week, with your wife, in writing. §9 sets them out as an audit.

## 64:6 — al-Taghabun: "Shall human beings guide us?"

### Verse (Arabic)
> ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُۥ كَانَت تَّأْتِيهِمْ رُسُلُهُم بِٱلْبَيِّنَٰتِ فَقَالُوٓاْ أَبَشَرٌ يَهْدُونَنَا فَكَفَرُواْ وَتَوَلَّواْ ۚ وَّٱسْتَغْنَى ٱللَّهُ ۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَنِىٌّ حَمِيدٌ

### Translation
"That was because their messengers used to come to them with clear proofs, but they said ˹mockingly˺, 'How can humans be our guides?' So they persisted in disbelief and turned away. And Allah was not in need ˹of their faith˺. For Allah is Self-Sufficient, Praiseworthy." *(Khattab, The Clear Quran, via quran.com)*

### Context of Revelation
No specific *sabab* documented. Surat al-Taghabun is Madinan; verses 5–6 recall the fate of earlier rejecting nations before the surah turns to address the believers. ✅ **His recitation of the verse is accurate**, including *fa-kafaru wa tawallaw wa'staghna'llah* [03:34].

### Tafsir al-Mizan
Retrieved in Arabic. On *a-basharun yahdununa*:

> «**والتنكير للتحقير، والاستفهام للإنكار، أي قالوا على سبيل الإنكار: أآحادٌ من البشر لا فضل لهم علينا يهدوننا**»

— the **indefinite** form *basharun* is used **to belittle**, and the interrogative expresses **rejection**: *are mere individuals of humankind, who have no superiority over us, to guide us?*

**NOT PRESENT in al-Mizan on this verse:** any extension of the objection into a general denial of human dignity or a theory of "the enemies of mankind." Tabataba'i locates the contempt specifically in the **messengers' humanity**.

### Other mainstream Shia scholarship
***Majma' al-Bayan*** (al-Tabrisi) on the same phrase: «**أبشرٌ يهدوننا … استصغاراً منهم للبشر أن يكون رسلاً من الله**» — *out of their belittling of human beings, that they should be messengers from God*. The two commentaries converge.

### The attribution problem
❌ He introduces the verse and this reading as *"taken from the tafsir of the leader"* [02:31]. **NOT VERIFIED.** Ayatollah Khamenei has no published Qur'an commentary that could be reached or confirmed, and **english.khamenei.ir and leader.ir are 403-blocked at the egress proxy** (re-tested during this audit), with farsi.khamenei.ir robots-blocked. So this is an **unreachable archive, not a completed negative search** — and it should be reported that way. What *can* be said with confidence is that **the content of the reading is Tabataba'i's and al-Tabrisi's, essentially verbatim**, so nothing in the substance depends on the attribution.

⚠ Note also that his framing phrase — *"not the regular garden variety kuffar"* [03:34] — reproduces almost exactly a phrase the shared verification index already records as an **unverified Khamenei attribution** from Part 2 (on Surat al-Nur 47–50). Counting this and the near-death-experience claim at [42:05], this lecture alone carries **two** unverified attributions to the Leader; the running series tally is **two verified against six unconfirmed**, with the caveat that the archive has been unreachable since partway through the project.

### Connection to parenting
⚠ *(my synthesis, not tafsir.)* The verse gives a parent an unexpectedly useful sentence for a ten-year-old: **contempt for human beings is not modern and it is not marginal — it is the oldest form of disbelief in the Qur'an's account.** A child raised in a culture that flatters him constantly while treating people as content, data and disposable labour is being told something about human worth every day. Q 64:6 says the argument is ancient, that Allah's messengers were on one side of it, and that the child is being asked to take a side. That is a bigger and more interesting claim than "be good."

**Confidence:** verse — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. al-Mizan content — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. Attribution to the Leader — **UNRESOLVED (archive unreachable)**.

## 24:30–31 — al-Nur: lowering the gaze

### Verse (Arabic)
> 24:30 — قُل لِّلْمُؤْمِنِينَ يَغُضُّواْ مِنْ أَبْصَٰرِهِمْ وَيَحْفَظُواْ فُرُوجَهُمْ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ أَزْكَىٰ لَهُمْ ۗ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ خَبِيرٌۢ بِمَا يَصْنَعُونَ
> 24:31 — وَقُل لِّلْمُؤْمِنَٰتِ يَغْضُضْنَ مِنْ أَبْصَٰرِهِنَّ وَيَحْفَظْنَ فُرُوجَهُنَّ …

### Translation
"Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard their chastity. That is purer for them. Surely Allah is All-Aware of what they do." / "And tell the believing women to lower their gaze and guard their chastity…"

### Context of Revelation
Madinan. The passage sits immediately after the *ifk* verses and the etiquette-of-entry verses, so the gaze instruction is embedded in a block about **household and communal decency** rather than standing alone as a sexual-ethics rule. ⚠ That placement is itself an argument for treating it as a matter of family culture rather than of individual policing.

*al-Mizan*'s *bahth riwa'i* on the passage records the narration of a young Ansari struck in the face while staring at a woman, after which Jibril brought these verses.

### Tafsir al-Mizan
Retrieved in Arabic. Two lexical findings, both useful:

- **«الغضّ إطباق الجفن على الجفن»** — *ghadd* is the **closing of the eyelid upon the eyelid**. It is a physical act, not an attitude.
- **«ومن هنا يظهر أن {من} في {من أبصارهم} لابتداء الغاية لا مزيدة»** — the particle *min* in *min absarihim* marks the **starting point** of the act; it is neither partitive nor redundant.
- In the *bahth riwa'i*, al-Mizan cites **al-Kafi** (Abu 'Amr al-Zubayri ← Imam al-Sadiq (a)): «**كل شيء في القرآن من حفظ الفرج فهو من الزنا إلا هذه الآية فهو من النظر**» — *every Qur'anic mention of guarding the private parts concerns fornication except this verse, which concerns looking.* ⭐ This is the Imami anchor for reading 24:30–31 as **the gaze verses specifically**, and it is exactly the point the Shaykh is making without citing it.

### Other mainstream Shia scholarship
The al-Kafi gloss above is itself the standard Imami authority for this reading; no separate *Majma' al-Bayan* material was retrieved for this passage.

### Connection to parenting
✅ *(his, and correct)* He observes that **the Qur'an addresses men first** and that the women's verse follows — both accurate, and the order matters pedagogically in a household with a son and a daughter: the instruction is not aimed at girls with boys as an afterthought.

⚠ *(my synthesis)* Two things follow that he does not say. First, *ghadd* as **eyelid closing on eyelid** is a **physical, momentary, repeatable act** — which makes it teachable to a seven-year-old as a movement rather than a mood: *when you see something you shouldn't, the first thing that moves is your eyelid.* Second, *min* as the **starting point** means the verse is describing where the act begins, not how much of it is required — which is why the tradition reads it as habituation rather than as a quota.

**Confidence:** verse and numbering — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. al-Mizan — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. The *tarbiyah* rule he attaches (a child habituated to forbidden looking is harder to reach) is **his own pastoral extension**, not tafsir.

## 59:10 — al-Hashr: the supplication against *ghill*

### Verse (Arabic)
> وَٱلَّذِينَ جَآءُو مِنۢ بَعْدِهِمْ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا ٱغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَٰنِنَا ٱلَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِٱلْإِيمَٰنِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِى قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِّلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ رَبَّنَآ إِنَّكَ رَءُوفٌ رَّحِيمٌ

### Translation
"And those who came after them say, 'Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in faith, and put not in our hearts any rancour toward those who have believed. Our Lord, You are indeed Most Compassionate, Most Merciful.'"

### Context of Revelation
Surat al-Hashr, following the verses on the **Muhajirun** (v.8) and the **Ansar** (v.9). *al-Mizan* identifies "those who came after them" as a **third and later cohort** — those whose belief occurred after the cessation of hijrah following the conquest.

### Tafsir al-Mizan
Retrieved in Arabic, and the operative gloss is short and decisive:

> «**والغلّ العداوة**» — *ghill* is **enmity**.

The supplication asks that such enmity not be placed in their hearts toward **any** of the believers.

**NOT PRESENT in al-Mizan:** any application of the verse to family life, to grudges between relatives, or to child-rearing. Al-Mizan reads it as describing a specific historical cohort.

### Other mainstream Shia scholarship
Not separately retrieved for this verse in this audit. ⚠ Recorded as a gap rather than filled in from general knowledge.

### Connection to parenting
✅ **The verse is verified and his rendering is accurate** — he gives no reference, and the reference is **59:10**.

⚠ **One honest caveat a Shia reader should have.** This verse is a live text in the intra-Muslim argument about the Companions, and al-Mizan reads it as a description of a historical cohort. Using it as a **family supplication against carrying grudges** is a legitimate application of a general moral — it is not a claim about what the verse is *about*. The right phrasing in the home is *"the Qur'an teaches us to ask this of Allah,"* not *"this verse is about our household."*

⚠ *(my synthesis)* With that caveat honoured, it is one of the most practically usable verses in the whole series for a family: it asks Allah **not to let rancour form**, which is a prayer about the heart's default settings rather than about behaviour. A family that says it aloud together — and the Arabic clause *wa la taj'al fi qulubina ghillan* is short enough for a four-year-old to echo — is doing something a behaviour chart cannot do. See §10, Activity E4.

**Confidence:** verse — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. al-Mizan (*ghill* = enmity; the "third cohort" identification) — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. The family application — **homiletic, and labelled as such**.

## 41:34 (with 23:96) — repelling evil with what is better

### Verse (Arabic)
> 41:34 — وَلَا تَسْتَوِى ٱلْحَسَنَةُ وَلَا ٱلسَّيِّئَةُ ۚ ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ فَإِذَا ٱلَّذِى بَيْنَكَ وَبَيْنَهُۥ عَدَٰوَةٌ كَأَنَّهُۥ وَلِىٌّ حَمِيمٌ
> 23:96 — ٱدْفَعْ بِٱلَّتِى هِىَ أَحْسَنُ ٱلسَّيِّئَةَ ۚ نَحْنُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا يَصِفُونَ

### Translation
41:34 — "Good and evil are not equal. Repel evil with what is better, and then the one between whom and you there was enmity will become as though he were a devoted friend."
23:96 — "Repel evil with what is best. We know well what they claim."

### Context of Revelation
41:34 sits in Fussilat, addressed to the Prophet (s) amid Meccan hostility; 23:96 closes a passage of the same kind in al-Mu'minun. ⭐ **Both are singular imperatives addressed to the Prophet (s)** — which is precisely the point on which the Shaykh's claim turns, and he is right.

### Tafsir al-Mizan
Retrieved in Arabic for 41:34–35.

- On the imperative: «**ادفع بالخصلة التي هي أحسن الخصلة السيئة**» — repel the bad *trait* with the better *trait*, whether that is truth against falsehood, forbearance against ignorance, or pardon against wrongdoing. ⚠ Note that al-Mizan reads the verse at the level of **character traits**, not merely of individual acts — which suits a *tarbiyah* lecture better than the Shaykh's own transactional reading.
- On the second clause: «**بيان لأثر الدفع بالأحسن**» — it is a statement of the **effect** of so repelling, not an additional command.
- ⭐ On **41:35** — «وَمَا يُلَقَّاهَا إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ صَبَرُواْ وَمَا يُلَقَّاهَا إِلَّا ذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍ» — he glosses the "great share" as «**نصيب وافر من كمال الإنسانية**», an abundant share of human perfection, and the verse itself attaches the explicit condition of ***sabr***. **The Qur'an presents this capacity as rare.**

### Other mainstream Shia scholarship
Not separately retrieved. ⚠ Recorded as a gap.

### The count, verified
✅ **"Twice the prophet is told to meet a bad deed with a good deed"** [57:13] is **precisely accurate**:

| Verse | Verb | Person | Addressee |
|---|---|---|---|
| **23:96** | ٱدْفَعْ | 2nd person **singular imperative** | the Prophet (s) |
| **41:34** | ٱدْفَعْ | 2nd person **singular imperative** | the Prophet (s) |
| **13:22** | وَيَدْرَءُونَ | 3rd person **plural, descriptive** | *ulu'l-albab*, believers generally |
| **28:54** | وَيَدْرَءُونَ | 3rd person **plural, descriptive** | the People of the Book who believed |

All four checked directly against the Arabic. ⚠ The lexical difference is worth teaching: **دفع** is to *push away*; **درء** is to *ward off / avert*. The same manoeuvre seen from the Prophet's (s) side and from ours.

### Connection to parenting
✅ *(his)* He uses 41:34 as the Qur'anic warrant for the closing *karam* material — the *karim* returns good for harm — and he is right to. ⭐ **This is the anchor he should have led with instead of the misattributed maxim at N21.** It is a verse, it is unambiguous, and it is stronger than any chainless *hikmah*.

⚠ *(my synthesis)* Three things a parent can take from al-Mizan's reading that the lecture does not give:
1. **It is a contest of traits, not of moves.** You are not teaching Abbas a clever tactic for defusing a bully; you are asking him to meet a bad *trait* with a better *trait*.
2. **The promised outcome is real but conditional.** *Ka-annahu waliyyun hamim* is the verse's own promise — and 41:35 immediately says only the patient are granted this. A child told it always works, and then bullied again next week, has been set up. Tell him it is the higher path *and* that it is hard, which is what the next verse says.
3. **Rarity is a motivator, not a discouragement.** «ذُو حَظٍّ عَظِيمٍ» — this is the Qur'an describing a small and distinguished group. For a seven-year-old, that framing is worth more than a promise of results.

**Confidence:** verses — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. al-Mizan on 41:34–35 — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**. His count of two imperatives — **STRONGLY VERIFIED**.

⚠ Not retrieved. Recorded as a gap.

# 6. Hadith and Narration Verification

> **Ratings.** **Strongly verified** = text, source and reference confirmed in a primary collection. **Moderately verified** = located but chainless, weak, or the exact reference unpinned. **Needs further verification** = not located, or the attribution unsettled.
>
> **A standing note on chainless works, which matters more in this lecture than in any other in the series.** *Ghurar al-Hikam*, *'Uyun al-Hikam wa'l-Mawa'iz*, *Tuhaf al-'Uqul*, *Makarim al-Akhlaq*, *Misbah al-Shari'ah* and *Nahj al-Balagha* carry **no isnad at all** — in the case of *Tuhaf al-'Uqul*, by Ibn Shu'ba al-Harrani's own declared editorial method, stated in his preface. Material from these works is **ungradeable in principle**: no assessor has graded it and none can, because there is nothing to grade. That is a statement about the genre, not a criticism of the text. Such material is **corroborative, not probative** — excellent for teaching, insufficient for establishing a claim on its own.
>
> ⭐ **Where this lecture's maxims come from — a structural finding.** Almost every *karam* maxim used across Parts 14, 15 and 16 maps, **entry for entry and in order**, onto a single topical compilation: the *al-Karam* file of the Shia thematic hadith collections (*al-Karam fi aqwal al-Nabi Muhammad wa Ali Baytih*, drawn from Rayshahri's *Mizan al-Hikmah*). Its entries appear here as **#27** (N1), **#28** (N7), **#26** (N20), **#36** (N19), **#35** (N21) and **#4** (N10). This explains the pattern exactly: **he quotes accurately because the compilation is accurate, and he never gives a grade because *Ghurar al-Hikam* has no chains to give.** It is a limitation of his working method, not of his honesty, and it should be said plainly.

### 1. «سُنَّةُ اللِّئَامِ قُبْحُ الْكَلَامِ» — "the way of the base is ugly speech" [06:37]

**Arabic (both halves):** «سُنَّةُ الْكِرَامِ تَرَادُفُ الْإِنْعَامِ · **سُنَّةُ اللِّئَامِ قُبْحُ الْكَلَامِ**»
**Translation:** "The way of the noble is favour upon favour; the way of the base is ugliness of speech."
**Reference:** ⭐ **Located and numbered.** Imam 'Ali (a), ***Ghurar al-Hikam wa Durar al-Kalim*** (al-Amidi), **entries 5550–5551** — a matched pair, *kiram* first and *li'am* second. Reproduced with those numbers in the *al-Karam* topical compilation (entry #27).
**Chain:** **None.** *Ghurar al-Hikam* carries no isnad for any of its ~11,000 entries.
**Authenticity assessment:** **Ungradeable in principle.** No assessor named, because none exists.
**Scholarly comment:** The rhyme *li'am / kalam* is what makes it memorable, and the pairing is the teaching device: nobility shows in what you **give**; baseness shows in what you **say**.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ✅ **Wording exactly right.** ⚠ He introduces it as *"the hadith of the Imam,"* which flattens a real distinction — this is a maxim in a 6th/12th-century thematic collection, not a transmitted report. ⚠ He quotes only the second half, and the first half is the one that gives a child something to **do** rather than something to avoid.
**Practical meaning:** For a lecture whose thesis is that *karamah* produces behaviour automatically, the positive limb is the more useful half. Teach both halves together or neither.
**Confidence: Moderately verified** — text and entry number pinned; provenance unverifiable in principle.

### 2. «لَا تَتَكَلَّمُوا بِالْفُحْشِ» + "this is not worthy of us" [06:37–07:38]

**As delivered:** *"La tatakallamu bi'l-fuhsh"* — don't speak to one another using foul language — **"this is not worthy of us … it is beneath us, it's also beneath our Shia that anybody use foul language."**
**Reference:** ⚠ **NOT LOCATED with this wording.** The *la tatakallamu bi'l-fuhsh* construction was searched in Arabic against Nahj al-Balagha, *Ghurar al-Hikam* and the al-Kafi *fuhsh/badha'* material. Nothing returned that sentence with the "not worthy of us / of our Shia" clause attached.
**Chain / authenticity:** Not applicable — the text was not located.
**What IS attested, and says the same thing:**
- ⭐ **Nahj al-Balagha, Khutbah 206** — spoken at **Siffin**, when he heard some of his men reviling the people of Sham: «**إِنِّي أَكْرَهُ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَكُونُوا سَبَّابِينَ**» — *"I dislike that you should be revilers."* Chainless (Nahj), but famous, occasioned, and checkable in a minute. ⭐ **He used this very text in Part 14**, where the audit verified it word for word.
- ⚠ **And the continuation matters**: the shared index records that Khutbah 206 goes past the prohibition to a **substitution clause** — «لَوْ وَصَفْتُمْ أَعْمَالَهُمْ…» — telling them what to say *instead*. Speakers routinely stop at the prohibition; the constructive half is the better half for a parent.
- The al-Kafi chapter ***bab al-badha'*** (Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr) is the Imami home of this theme.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ⚠ **The content is entirely sound and thoroughly Imami; the specific sentence he puts in Amir al-Mu'minin's (a) mouth could not be found.** Because he delivers it as a direct quotation in Arabic, this matters. **Switch to Khutbah 206.**
**Practical meaning:** "Foul speech is beneath the followers of 'Ali (a)" is exactly what Khutbah 206 says, with the added force that he said it to his **own army, mid-war, about the enemy**. That is a far stronger teaching frame than an unsourced sentence.
**Confidence: Needs further verification** for the quoted wording; **strongly verified** for the substitute.

### 3. ⭐⭐ «أَدِّ الْأَمَانَةَ إِلَى مَنِ ائْتَمَنَكَ، وَلَا تَخُنْ مَنْ خَانَكَ» [09:40] — **the load-bearing problem of this lecture**

**Arabic:** «أَدِّ الْأَمَانَةَ إِلَى مَنِ ائْتَمَنَكَ، وَلَا تَخُنْ مَنْ خَانَكَ»
**Translation:** "Discharge the trust to the one who entrusted it to you, and do not betray the one who betrayed you."
**As delivered:** *"This is from Rasulullah. He says: at-til amana … ila man tamanaka … wa la takhun man khanaka. Don't even betray, don't do khiyanah to the person who has betrayed you. It's that bad."*

**Reference.** ⚠ **This is a Sunni text, and it is one of the most heavily contested hadiths in the entire Sunni corpus.** It appears at **Abu Dawud 3535**, **al-Tirmidhi 1264**, in Musnad Ahmad, al-Daraqutni and al-Bayhaqi, from **Abu Hurayrah**, with variant chains from **Anas** and **Ubayy b. Ka'b**.

**Chain.** Multiple routes; **every one of them criticised.**

**Authenticity — the assessors, named.** From the dorar.net *takhrij* table (17 entries with named graders):

| Assessor | Verdict | Where recorded |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Imam Ahmad b. Hanbal** | «**بَاطِل**» — *false, void* | via Ibn al-Mulaqqin, *Khulasat al-Badr al-Munir* 2/150; *al-Muqarrar* 2/393 |
| **Abu Hatim al-Razi** | «**مُنْكَر**» | *al-Muharrar fi'l-Hadith* 328 |
| **al-Shafi'i** (via al-Bayhaqi) | «**لَيْسَ بِثَابِت**» — not established | *al-Sunan al-Kubra* 10/271 |
| **Ibn al-Jawzi** | «**جَمِيعُ طُرُقِهِ لَا يَصِحّ**» — *none* of its routes is sound, said separately of the Abu Hurayrah, Anas **and** Ubayy versions | *al-'Ilal al-Mutanahiya* 2/592–593 |
| **Ibn al-Mulaqqin** | "six routes, **all of them weak**" | — |
| **Ibn Hazm** | «**لَا يَصِحّ**» | — |
| **Ibn al-Mundhir** | «**غَيْرُ ثَابِت**» | — |
| **Ibn al-Qayyim** and **Ibn Hazm** | treat the al-Hasan al-Basri route as ***mursal*** | — |
| **al-Tirmidhi** — *the sole dissent* | «**حَسَنٌ غَرِيب**» | *Sunan* 1264 |

**Imami status.** ❌ **No al-Kafi, al-Saduq or al-Tusi placement located for this wording.** It is Sunni in provenance and it is being given to a Shia audience as "from Rasulullah" with no source at all.

**Verdict on the speaker's use.** ⭐ ⚠ **This is the most significant single problem in the lecture, and it sits at the top of the argument.** He introduces it as *"This is from Rasulullah"* and makes it the anchor of the entire *khiyanah* section — the whole proposition that betrayal is so grave you may not even return it in kind. The text he is quoting is one **Imam Ahmad called *batil*** and Ibn al-Jawzi judged unsound by every route.

Two honest options, and both are available to him:
1. **Drop the attribution and keep the principle**, which is independently sound; or
2. ⭐ **Use texts that carry it properly** — **Q 4:58** for the *amanah* half (with al-Mizan's «يعم الأمانات المالية وغيرها من المعنويات», which also hands him the material/immaterial taxonomy he builds by hand), and **Q 5:8** for the "do not betray the betrayer" half.

**Practical meaning.** Worth saying plainly to any parent about to repeat this from the pulpit or at the dinner table: **the ethic needs no weak hadith to prop it up.** *You do not become a traitor because someone was a traitor to you* is Qur'anic. The lecture's argument survives the correction entirely intact — which is exactly why the correction costs nothing and should be made.

**Confidence: Strongly verified as a finding** — the *takhrij* is unambiguous and multiply attested. **The hadith itself is NOT ESTABLISHED.**

### 4. «الْكَذِبُ وَالْخِيَانَةُ لَيْسَا مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ» [10:40]

**Arabic:** «الْكَذِبُ وَالْخِيَانَةُ لَيْسَا مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ»
**Translation:** "Lying and treachery are not among the traits of the noble."
**Reference:** Imam 'Ali (a); belongs to the **chainless maxim corpus** — ***Ghurar al-Hikam*** / ***'Uyun al-Hikam wa'l-Mawa'iz*** (al-Laythi al-Wasiti). ⚠ **Exact entry number NOT PINNED** — three Arabic phrase searches, including against the *al-Karam* topical compilation that yielded N1 and N7, returned nothing carrying an entry number. **Chain:** None. **Authenticity:** Ungradeable.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ✅ **Accurate, and correctly attributed** — he says "this is now from Amir al-Mu'minin," and in the maxim collections that is right. ⚠ Presented as a *hadith*; it is a maxim.

### 6. ⭐ «إِفْشَاؤُكَ سِرَّ أَخِيكَ خِيَانَةٌ» [13:46] — **his best-executed narration in the *khiyanah* section**

**Arabic (with the clause he omits):** «**الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ**، وَإِفْشَاؤُكَ سِرَّ أَخِيكَ خِيَانَةٌ، فَاجْتَنِبْ ذَلِكَ، وَاجْتَنِبْ مَجْلِسَ الْعَشِيرَةِ»
**Translation:** "**Gatherings are held in trust**; your divulging your brother's secret is treachery, so avoid that — and avoid the gathering of the clan [that engages in it]."
**Reference:** ⭐ **LOCATED, and the speaker's attribution is right.** It is a clause of the ***wasiyyah* of the Prophet (s) to Abu Dharr al-Ghifari** — the long counsel preserved in ***Makarim al-Akhlaq*** (al-Tabrisi) and the ***Amali* of al-Tusi**, and gathered into ***Bihar al-Anwar* vol. 74** (*bab mawa'iz al-Nabi*). Text confirmed verbatim against a published edition of the *wasiyyah*.
**Chain:** The *wasiyyah* as transmitted through *Makarim al-Akhlaq* is **chainless**; al-Tusi's *Amali* recension carries a chain, but **no rijal verdict for it was retrieved** in this audit.
**Authenticity assessment:** Not gradeable from *Makarim*. The *wasiyyah* as a whole is a famous and much-commented text — Shahid al-Thani treats it seriously in *Munyat al-Murid*, as do modern commentators — but it is **late-attested**.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ✅ ⭐ He says *"this one is from our Prophet"* — **correct**, and this is the item in the section where the attribution holds. He quotes it accurately, including *fa-ijtanib dhalik*. ⚠ Two things he leaves out strengthen his own argument: (a) the opening clause **«الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ»**, which is precisely the principle behind his case study of the boy who broadcasts what happens at home; and (b) the fact that it is **advice to a single named Companion**, which is why it reads as pastoral rather than legislative.
**Practical meaning:** ⭐ This is the narration that does the work his unlocated "four signs of a *kha'in*" (N8) cannot. It establishes an **immaterial amanah — information** — in an unimpeachably Prophetic voice, and it does so with the family-facing clause attached.
**Confidence: Strongly verified** as to text, source-family and attribution to the Prophet (s); **needs further verification** as to grading.

### 7. «سُنَّةُ الْكِرَامِ الْوَفَاءُ بِالْعُهُودِ» [13:46]

### 8. ❌ "The signs of a *kha'in* are four" [14:47]

**As delivered:** *"The prophet is talking and he says that the signs of a kha'in are four. One of them he mentions is harming the neighbour."*
**Reference:** ❌ **NOT LOCATED — do not attribute.** dorar.net returns nothing for a four-item enumeration of *'alamat al-kha'in*; al-Kafi's own trust material was checked; no Imami or Sunni collection produces it.
**Chain / authenticity:** Not applicable.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ❌ ⚠ **The tell is in his own delivery: he announces four signs and supplies one.** A preacher reading from a book gives all four. This is the first of two enumerated narrations in this lecture that are announced and left unenumerated (see N15) — a reliable signature of material that reached the speaker through preaching rather than through a text.

**Practical meaning:** The teaching point is excellent and worth keeping: a child who understands that *nobody signed anything and it is still binding* has understood covenant. Attach it to Jibril and the neighbour, not to four unnamed signs.
**Confidence: NOT LOCATED.**

### 9. «الْعَيْنَانِ زِنَاهُمَا النَّظَرُ» — "the zina of the eyes is the look" [21:49]

**Arabic:** «الْعَيْنَانِ زِنَاهُمَا النَّظَرُ» / «زِنَا الْعَيْنِ النَّظَرُ»
**Reference:** **Sahih al-Bukhari 6612** (Kitab al-Qadar); **Sahih Muslim 2657b**; also Abu Dawud 2152, al-Tirmidhi 2786, *Riyad al-Salihin* 1622. Narrated by **Abu Hurayrah**, inside the longer hadith that assigns each limb its share of *zina* and ends *"and the private part confirms it or denies it."*
**Chain:** Multiple sound chains.
**Authenticity assessment:** ***Sahih*, and *muttafaq 'alayh*** — agreed upon by al-Bukhari and Muslim. At this level named graders are unnecessary.
**Imami status:** The wording is at home in the Shia preaching tradition, but the canonical placement is **Sunni**. The standard Imami text for the same point is «**النَّظْرَةُ سَهْمٌ مِنْ سِهَامِ إِبْلِيسَ مَسْمُومٌ**» — *"the look is a poisoned arrow from the arrows of Iblis"* — in al-Kafi, *Kitab al-Nikah*, *bab al-nazar*; and the al-Kafi gloss on 24:30 already quoted in §5 («كل شيء في القرآن من حفظ الفرج فهو من الزنا إلا هذه الآية فهو من النظر») is the Imami anchor for the verse itself.

Here it costs him nothing, because the *sahm min siham Iblis* narration says the same thing, is more vivid, and is Imami. **This is a recurring habit worth naming, and it is not a doctrinal problem — it is a sourcing habit.**
**Practical meaning:** For a child, the *arrow* image is better than the *zina* image in every respect: it is age-appropriate, it names an attacker rather than an appetite, and it makes the gaze a matter of defence rather than of shame.
**Confidence: Strongly verified** (Sunni, *muttafaq 'alayh*); the Imami parallel located by family, not pinned to a hadith number in this audit.

### 10. ⭐⭐ THE SPINE — «ثَلَاثَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى كَرَمِ الْمَرْءِ» [23:54], reprised [41:05]

**Arabic:** «**ثَلَاثَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى كَرَمِ الْمَرْءِ: حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ، وَكَظْمُ الْغَيْظِ، وَغَضُّ الطَّرْفِ**»
**Translation:** "Three things indicate a man's nobility: good character, swallowing anger, and lowering the gaze."
**Reference:** ⭐ **LOCATED AND PINNED.** Imam **Ja'far al-Sadiq (a)**, ***Tuhaf al-'Uqul* of Ibn Shu'ba al-Harrani, p. 319** — in the section headed «ومن كلامه عليه السلام سماه بعض الشيعة **نثر الدرر**», the collection of the Imam's short maxims. Confirmed independently at almerja.com's *ida'at* index and in the *al-Karam fi aqwal al-Nabi wa Ali Baytih* compilation, which cites it as *Tuhaf al-'Uqul*: 319 (entry #4).
**Chain:** ❌ **None.** *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* **omits every isnad in the book** — Ibn Shu'ba's declared editorial method, stated in his own preface.
**Authenticity assessment:** **Ungradeable in principle.** No assessor has graded it and none can. ⚠ This is the same standing limitation that applies to *Ghurar*, *'Uyun al-Hikam*, *Nahj al-Balagha*, *Makarim al-Akhlaq* and *Misbah al-Shari'ah*.
**Scholarly comment:** ⭐ The corroboration is nonetheless unusually strong, because **each of the three limbs is independently attested in graded material**: *husn al-khuluq* throughout al-Kafi's *bab husn al-khuluq*; *kazm al-ghayz* in Q 3:134 and the graded *kazm* narrations; *ghadd al-tarf* in Q 24:30–31 with the al-Kafi gloss. The maxim assembles three well-founded items; it does not introduce a new claim.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ⭐ **This is the structural spine of the lecture and he uses it well.** Everything after [23:54] hangs on it: *ghadd al-basar* becomes goals two and four, *husn al-khuluq* becomes goal five and the Haydar case study, and *kazm al-ghayz* is the bridge into the forgiveness material at the end. He is right that it is Imam al-Sadiq (a), right that there are three, and right about all three items. Three cautions, in descending order of importance:
1. ⚠ **It carries no chain, and he presents it as "This is now hadith."** For the load-bearing text of a 59-minute argument, a listener deserves to know it comes from a chainless 4th/10th-century maxim collection. Corroborative, not probative.
2. ⚠ **The Arabic is غَضُّ الطَّرْفِ (*ghadd al-tarf*), not *ghadd al-basar*** — which is what he says at [24:54]. Same meaning; but he then pivots straight to Q 24:30–31, where the Qur'anic word **is** *absar*. Say that the maxim and the verse **converge**; do not imply that the maxim quotes the verse.
3. ⚠ **At [41:05] the reprise collapses.** "There are three things which indicate if a person has Karamah. The first one that the Imam mentions he says—" and the sentence is never completed. On the second pass the three items are never restated.
**Practical meaning:** ⭐ The three are a **behavioural definition of dignity**, which is exactly what a parent can work with: how you speak, what you do with anger, where you let your eyes go. And note the internal logic he never draws out — **all three are forms of self-restraint under pressure**, which is why *karamah* can be the upstream cause and compliance the downstream effect. Pair it with the verified **Nahj al-Balagha Hikmah 449** («مَنْ كَرُمَتْ عَلَيْهِ نَفْسُهُ هَانَتْ عَلَيْهِ شَهْوَتُهُ») and the mechanism is stated in two sentences.
**Confidence: Strongly verified** as to text, wording, source, page and attribution to Imam al-Sadiq (a); **ungradeable** as to authenticity.

### 12. «الْحَيَاءُ سَبَبٌ إِلَى كُلِّ جَمِيلٍ» [28:57]

**Translation:** "Modesty is a cause of everything beautiful."
**Reference:** Imam 'Ali (a); chainless maxim corpus (*Ghurar* / *'Uyun al-Hikam*). ⚠ **Entry NOT PINNED** — same failed searches as N11.
**Chain:** None. **Authenticity:** Ungradeable.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ✅ Accurate. ⚠ Chainless, and it is the **third maxim in a row delivered as "hadith."**
⭐ **The graded alternative he could have used instead:** the *haya'* material in **al-Kafi 2, Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr, *bab al-haya'***, including «**الْحَيَاءُ مِنَ الْإِيمَانِ، وَالْإِيمَانُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ**» — chains instead of maxims, for the theme this lecture leans on hardest after *karam*.
**Practical meaning:** The claim is causal and forward-looking — *haya'* is not a restraint on beauty but its **cause** — which is the right register for talking to a daughter about modesty and the opposite of the register modesty usually gets discussed in.
**Confidence: Moderately verified.**

### 13. ⭐ Nahj al-Balagha, Hikmah 124 — a woman's *ghayrah* and a man's [29:59]

**Arabic:** «**غَيْرَةُ الْمَرْأَةِ كُفْرٌ، وَغَيْرَةُ الرَّجُلِ إِيمَانٌ**»
**Translation:** "A woman's jealousy is *kufr*; a man's jealousy is faith."
**Reference:** **Nahj al-Balagha, Hikmah 124** (Subhi Salih numbering). ✅ Confirmed verbatim.
**Chain:** **None** — Nahj al-Balagha carries no isnad; al-Sharif al-Radi selected for eloquence, not for transmission.
**Authenticity assessment:** **Ungradeable** as transmitted.
**Scholarly comment:** The commentators — **Ibn Abi'l-Hadid**, **Muhammad 'Abduh**, and among the moderns **Makarim Shirazi** in *Payam-e Imam* — read the *ghayrah* here as jealousy that **objects to what the Shari'ah has permitted**, which is why it can be called *kufr*: it is objection to a divine ruling, not a verdict on an emotion.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ✅ ⭐ **He handles a genuinely difficult text responsibly, and this deserves saying plainly.** He raises it **unprompted** — *"there's that hadith … that women are not supposed to be having ghayrah, cuz that's in Nahj al-Balagha"* — and immediately restricts it: *"that's a different type of ghayrah … that's ghayrah [when] the husband is trying to marry again. That ghayrah, no. **This kind of ghayrah is for everybody.**"* That restriction is the mainstream Imami reading.
⚠ **What he does not tell the audience** is that the restriction is **an interpretation**, not something the *hikmah* itself says. As al-Radi gives it, the text is unqualified. The honest form is: *"the commentators restrict this, and here is the text they restrict it with"* — which brings us to N14.
**Practical meaning:** A parent whose daughter will one day meet this line — and she will, on the internet, out of context — is far better served by a father who raised it first, restricted it, and named the restricting text.
**Confidence: Strongly verified** as to text and location; **ungradeable** as to chain.

### 14. ⭐ «لَا غَيْرَةَ فِي الْحَلَالِ» — the citation the *ghayrah* section needed and never got [29:59, unquoted]

**Arabic:** «**لَا غَيْرَةَ فِي الْحَلَالِ**»
**Translation:** "There is no *ghayrah* in what is lawful."
**Reference:** ⭐ **al-Kafi vol. 5, *Kitab al-Nikah*, chapter 178** — a **one-hadith chapter whose title is the hadith**, on a chain of the strongest type.
**Chain:** Confirmed of the strongest type in this audit.
**Authenticity assessment:** **Strongly verified.**
**Verdict:** ⭐ **This is the missing citation of the whole section.** The Shaykh makes exactly the right move at [29:59] and then makes it **on his own authority**. Al-Kulayni gave the restriction a **chapter heading** eleven centuries ago. Citing it converts *"that's a different type of ghayrah"* from an assertion into a report — which is the difference between a listener taking your word and a listener being able to check.
⚠ **And the limb he omits.** A lecture recommending *ghayrah* to parents raising teenagers needs the brake as well as the accelerator:
- **al-Kafi 5/3/177 h.9** and **Nahj al-Balagha, Letter 31** both condemn **misplaced** *ghayrah*, warning that it drives a sound woman toward sickness and an innocent one toward suspicion.
- ⚠ Note separately from the shared index: Letter 31 also contains the restrictive passage on consulting women, and its provenance is contested (al-Radi's heading places it at Hadirin, returning from Siffin; the addressee is disputed). Cite it for the *ghayrah* warning, not as a general charter.
**Practical meaning:** Together these two texts state the Imami position precisely: *ghayrah* is protectiveness about what genuinely must be protected, and it is **forbidden territory** where the Shari'ah has permitted, and **corrosive** where it becomes suspicion of the innocent. That is a usable rule for a father of a teenage daughter.
**Confidence: Strongly verified.**

### 15. ⭐ ❌ The captured assassin spared for five traits [30:59]

**As delivered:** *"These terrorists, these Daesh-type terrorists, were captured. Apparently they had planned to kill Rasulullah … All of them were executed except one … Rasulullah said, 'Jibra'il told me from God … you have five traits that Allah and His Messenger love. The first one, al-ghayratu shadidatun 'ala haramik.'"*
**Reference:** ❌ **NOT LOCATED — do not attribute.** Absence-checked against the Sunni corpus via dorar.net and against **al-Kafi 5, chapter 177**, the dedicated *ghayrah* chapter, **all nine hadith read**. A further Arabic phrase search on «الغيرة الشديدة على حرمك» paired with the five-traits frame returned nothing but modern Friday-sermon pages.
**Chain / authenticity:** Not applicable.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ❌ ⚠ **A high-colour narrative, told in circumstantial detail, that no source supports.** Three compounding problems:
1. **The report is unlocated** — neither the frame (prisoners condemned, one released), nor the five-trait list, nor the *ghayrah* clause.
2. **He supplies one of the five** — *"and he mentions the four others"* — exactly as at N8. ⚠ **Twice in one lecture an enumerated narration is announced and left unenumerated.**

⭐ **What to use instead:** if the point is that *ghayrah* is a trait God loves, the graded route is in that same chapter — «**إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَيُورٌ يُحِبُّ كُلَّ غَيُورٍ**», *God is ghayur and loves every ghayur*. Prophetic, in the dedicated chapter, and it needs no captives.

### 16. «أَعْقَلُ النَّاسِ أَحْيَاهُمْ» [32:59]

### 17. ⭐ ❌ "If *husn al-khuluq* were a creation you could see…" [41:05–42:05]

**As delivered:** «مَا كَانَ مِمَّا خَلَقَ اللهُ شَيْءٌ أَحْسَنُ مِنْهُ» / «مَا كَانَ مِمَّا خَلَقَ اللهُ شَيْءٌ أَقْبَحُ مِنْهُ» — *"if husn al-khuluq were a creation of God that we could see, there would not be anything out of all the things Allah has created more beautiful than it; and if bad akhlaq were a creation we could see, there would be nothing more hideous."* Introduced as **"the Prophet says this."**
**Reference:** ⚠ **NOT LOCATED with this wording — do not attribute.** Checked against **dorar.net** (Sunni *takhrij* with named graders); the Shia topical compilation *al-Khuluq fi aqwal al-Nabi Muhammad wa Ali Baytih* (**43 entries, none matching**); and two further Arabic phrase searches. Nothing in either corpus carries the **double comparative** — "most beautiful of what Allah created" / "most hideous."
**The nearest attested text, and it is not the same:** dorar.net returns «**لَوْ كَانَ حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ رَجُلًا يَمْشِي فِي النَّاسِ لَكَانَ رَجُلًا صَالِحًا**» — *"if good character were a man walking among people he would be a righteous man"* — graded ***da'if*** to ***da'if jiddan*** by the Sunni critics indexed there. That is a **much smaller claim** (a righteous man, not the most beautiful thing in creation) and it has **no ugliness clause at all**.
**Chain / authenticity:** Not applicable to the quoted wording; the nearest text is ***da'if***.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ⚠ ⭐ **He says "the Prophet says this" and gives Arabic for both halves.** That is a stronger claim than the evidence supports, and **it matters more here than a stray misattribution normally would**, because this narration is the **hinge into the Behrouz Azimi near-death sequence**: the whole "bad akhlaq is objectively hideous, and someone has *seen* it" movement rests on it.
⚠ **Note also the slide between two different propositions.** "If it were a creation we could **see**" is a **thought experiment**. The near-death account is offered as a **report** that someone **did** see it. The narration, even if it existed, would license only the first.
⚠ For completeness: the *construction* belongs to a known homiletic family — compare the genuine al-Kafi saying on obscenity, «**لَوْ كَانَ الْفُحْشُ مِثَالًا لَكَانَ مِثَالَ سُوءٍ**» ("if obscenity were an image it would be an evil image," *Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr*, *bab al-fuhsh*) — but the specific sentence he quotes was not found.
⭐ **What to use instead:** al-Kafi 2, ***bab su' al-khuluq***: «**إِنَّ سُوءَ الْخُلُقِ لَيُفْسِدُ الْعَمَلَ كَمَا يُفْسِدُ الْخَلُّ الْعَسَلَ**» — *bad character spoils deeds as vinegar spoils honey*. Graded, Imami, and vivid enough to carry the whole section without a monster in it.
**Practical meaning:** The vinegar-and-honey image is also the better one for a child: it says that bad character does not merely coexist with your good deeds, it **ruins** them — which is a claim about the whole jar, and a seven-year-old understands jars.
**Confidence: NOT LOCATED.**

### 18. «سَبَبُ الْفِتَنِ الْحِقْدُ» [50:07]

### 19. «لَيْسَ مِنْ شِيَمِ الْكِرَامِ تَعْجِيلُ الِانْتِقَامِ» [52:08]

### 20. «الْمُبَادَرَةُ إِلَى الْعَفْوِ مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ» [56:11]

**Arabic:** «**الْمُبَادَرَةُ إِلَى الْعَفْوِ مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ**»
**Translation:** "Taking the initiative toward pardon is among the traits of the noble."
**Reference:** ⭐ **Located and numbered.** Imam 'Ali (a), ***Ghurar al-Hikam*, entries 1566–1567** — another matched *kiram / li'am* pair (entry #26 in the *al-Karam* compilation).
**Chain:** None. **Authenticity:** Ungradeable.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ✅ Sense exactly right, and this is the maxim the whole closing section is built on. ⚠ **One wording correction**: the attested verb is **مُبَادَرَة** (*mubadarah*), not *musara'ah*. ### 21. ⭐ «مَنْ لَمْ يُجَازِ الْإِسَاءَةَ بِالْإِحْسَانِ فَلَيْسَ مِنَ الْكِرَامِ» [57:13] — **misattributed, and repeatedly**

**Arabic:** «مَنْ لَمْ يُجَازِ الْإِسَاءَةَ بِالْإِحْسَانِ فَلَيْسَ مِنَ الْكِرَامِ»
**Translation:** "Whoever does not requite mistreatment with kindness is not one of the noble."

**Chain:** None. **Authenticity:** Ungradeable.
**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ⚠ ⭐ **Misattribution, and a repeated one across the series — with a twist that resolves it.** Here at [57:13] he gives it to the Prophet (s). **In Part 16 he cites the same maxim and attributes it correctly to Imam 'Ali (a).** Same speaker, same sentence, two lectures, two attributions. The correction is firm: ***Ghurar al-Hikam* 8958, Amir al-Mu'minin (a)**.

### 22. ⭐ *Makarim al-akhlaq* — three items, or four? [58:14]

**As delivered:** *"Sometimes these hadith that talk about … makarim al-akhlaq — the Prophet advising Amir al-Mu'minin. What's makarim al-akhlaq? The Prophet says: one, you **give the person who denied you**; two, you **reconnect with the person who cut you off**; three, you **forgive the person who oppressed you**."*

**The best-graded Imami text.** ⭐ ***al-Kafi*, vol. 2, Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr, chapter 53, hadith 1.**
- **Grading: al-Majlisi, *Mir'at al-'Uqul*: *hasan ka'l-sahih*.**
- **Frame:** the Prophet (s) **addressing a public gathering** — not 'Ali (a).
- **Items: four, not three** — *silat man qata'ak* (reconnect with the one who cut you off), *i'ta' man haramak* (give the one who denied you), *al-'afw 'amman zalamak* (pardon the one who wronged you), **and** — the fourth, which the speaker omits — **that you speak the truth even against yourself.**

**The three-item version addressed to 'Ali (a).** It exists, but it is **Sunni**: al-Bayhaqi and al-Tabarani carry «يَا عَلِيُّ، أَلَا أَدُلُّكَ عَلَى مَكَارِمِ الْأَخْلَاقِ…» with the three items, and it is graded ***da'if***.

**Verdict on the speaker's use:** ⭐ ⚠ **The narration is real; he has taken the weaker recension.** He has preferred the ***three-item, addressed-to-'Ali, da'if*** version over the ***four-item, public-sermon, hasan ka'l-sahih*** one sitting in al-Kafi. Two costs, and the first is severe:

1. ⭐ **He loses the fourth item — truthfulness even against oneself.** That is a serious loss **in this lecture specifically**, whose opening third is about a boy who lies and steals, and whose thesis is that *karamah* shows up as honesty (N4: *"a person who has karamah won't try to save themselves by lying"*). **The al-Kafi text hands him the connection between his first section and his last, and he drops it.**
2. ⚠ **The "advising Amir al-Mu'minin" frame is not the Imami one.** For a Shia audience this is an odd inversion: the version he prefers is the Sunni weak one; the version he omits is the Imami strong one.

⭐ **Recommendation: cite al-Kafi 2/53/1 and give all four.** This is the single easiest upgrade available in the lecture, and it costs nothing but one extra clause.
**Practical meaning:** All four items are behaviours a child can be shown. The fourth — *truthfulness even when it costs you* — is the one that closes the circle back to Aziz's son, and it is the one that turns *makarim al-akhlaq* from a list of generosities into a definition of integrity.
**Confidence: Strongly verified** (both limbs).

### A note on the two enumerations that were never enumerated

⚠ Twice in this lecture — at **N8** ("the signs of a *kha'in* are four") and **N15** ("you have five traits that Allah and His Messenger love") — the Shaykh announces a numbered list from a narration and supplies **exactly one item** before moving on. Both narrations turned out to be unlocated. That correlation is not a coincidence and it is a useful diagnostic for any listener: **a speaker reading from a text gives the whole list; a speaker recalling a story he heard gives the item that made an impression.** Where you hear an announced enumeration left unfinished, treat the narration as unverified until you have checked it.

## Reading the audit fairly

**Start with the arithmetic, because it is the fairest summary available.** Of the twenty-two narrations in this lecture: **six are strongly verified** (four of them Qur'anic or Prophetic and well placed), **nine are moderately verified** — accurately quoted chainless maxims, **five of them now pinned to *Ghurar al-Hikam* entry numbers** — **three are NOT LOCATED**, **one is not established and was declared *batil* by Imam Ahmad**, and **two are correctly located but misattributed or taken in the weaker recension**. Alongside that, **five Qur'anic verses were verified verse by verse, with the readings matching al-Mizan**, and one empirical claim turned out to be the best-supported in the entire series.

**The pattern, stated plainly: he quotes accurately and sources loosely.** His Arabic is reliable — in twenty-two items there is not a single garbled or invented Arabic text, and where the wording needs correcting (*ghadd al-tarf* not *ghadd al-basar*; *mubadarah* not *musara'ah*) the corrections are one word each. What is unreliable is **"the Prophet says this."** Three of this lecture's five worst items are attribution failures on texts that are otherwise fine or on texts that do not exist, and every one of them would have been avoided by a single habit: naming the collection.

**Three items must be retracted, not softened.**
1. ⭐ **N3**, the *amanah* hadith — **declared *batil* by Imam Ahmad**, unsound by every route according to Ibn al-Jawzi, *munkar* to Abu Hatim, *laysa bi-thabit* to al-Shafi'i, *la yasihh* to Ibn Hazm; Sunni in provenance with no Imami chain. It is the **opening move of the lecture's main argument**, and it must be replaced by **Q 4:58** and **Q 5:8**, which say the same thing better.
2. **N15**, the captured assassin spared for five traits — unlocated in both corpora, with a five-times-repeated "Daesh-type terrorists" overlay that is his own and does real rhetorical work.
3. **N17**, "if *husn al-khuluq* were a creation you could see" — unlocated, and the **hinge into the near-death sequence**, so its failure propagates.

**Two are the right idea with the wrong label.** N21 belongs to **Imam 'Ali (a)**, not the Prophet (s) — and he himself gets it right in Part 16. N22 is the **weak Sunni recension** of a hadith whose strong Imami recension sits in al-Kafi with an extra item that would have tied his own lecture together.

**And two silences cost him real ground.** He restricts Nahj Hikmah 124 correctly and never cites **al-Kafi 5 ch.178**, the chapter whose *title* is the restriction. He builds the material/immaterial *amanah* distinction over five minutes and never cites **al-Mizan on 4:58**, where Tabataba'i states it in one clause. Both are cases of a good scholar doing by hand what his own tradition had already done for him.

**Now the other side of the ledger, which is substantial.**

- ⭐ **The Qur'anic spine holds under inspection, and one claim in it is better than most preachers' claims ever are.** "Twice the Prophet is told to meet a bad deed with a good deed" is the sort of count that usually collapses on checking. It is **exactly right**, down to the grammatical person of the verb in all four relevant verses.
- ⭐ **The spine narration is real, correctly attributed, and pinned to a page** — *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* 319, Imam al-Sadiq (a). For a lecture of this length to rest on a text that survives verification is not the norm in this series.
- ✅ **He handles the hardest text in the lecture honourably.** Hikmah 124 on a woman's *ghayrah* is a line most speakers either avoid or weaponise. He raises it **unprompted** and immediately restricts it in line with the mainstream commentators.
- ⭐ **He flags his fiction before telling it.** *"Can I tell you a story that's not true first?"* In a genre where illustrative fables routinely acquire chains, that sentence is a discipline worth naming.
- ⭐ **He preserves Nasrallah's "this is my opinion, not a fatwa" caveat** rather than upgrading a personal view into a ruling.
- ⭐ **He identifies Behrouz Azimi exactly** — programme, person, and an account that matches the press coverage headline for headline. In a series where several contemporary stories could not be traced at all, this one checks out.
- ⭐ **And his one hard empirical claim is right, and better supported than he knew.** Filters really do buy far less than parents believe (Przybylski & Nash 2018), and the remedy he then spends fifty minutes recommending — talking to the child — is the arm the meta-analytic literature actually supports.

**On the unreachable archives, and this matters for fairness.** Three attributions in this lecture point to Ayatollah Khamenei: the tafsir of 64:6, "the leader says near-death experiences are real," and "he was our student; he became our teacher" about Mahmoud Kaveh. **None could be confirmed. None can be reported as refuted.** `english.khamenei.ir` and `leader.ir` are **403-blocked at the egress proxy**, `farsi.khamenei.ir` is robots-blocked, and the Iranian outlets that carry the Kaveh material (Farhikhtegan, DefaPress, IRIB, Tasnim) were robots- or DNS-blocked. A negative here means **the archive could not be reached**. Any of the three might be settled in two minutes from an ordinary browser, and a reader who cares about them should do exactly that rather than treat this document's silence as a verdict.

**In fairness, the deepest point is about method, not about honesty.** The single largest category in this audit — nine of twenty-two items — is *accurately quoted, correctly attributed, and ungradeable*. That is not a failure of scholarship. It is what happens when a preacher works from a **topical anthology** (the *al-Karam* compilation, ultimately *Ghurar al-Hikam*) that carries no chains **because the source it draws on has none to carry**. The audit confirmed that nine of his *karam* maxims across Parts 14–16 map onto that compilation entry for entry, in order. He is quoting his source correctly. The right correction is not "stop using these"; it is **"say what they are"** — that they are maxims from the *hikam* literature, corroborative and beautiful, and that where a claim has to bear weight, a graded narration or a verse should carry it.

**The series pattern holds here exactly as elsewhere: what he learned from books is good; what reached him through preaching is where the failures cluster.** The three unlocated items in this lecture are a story about captives, a story about a *kha'in*'s four signs, and a homiletic thought-experiment. The verified items are a verse count, a page number in *Tuhaf al-'Uqul*, a chapter of Nahj al-Balagha, and two *sahih* Sunni hadith. That is not carelessness about the deen. It is the ordinary condition of preaching from memory — and it is precisely why the checking step earns its keep.

# 8. Parenting Action Guide

> Written for a father of **Abbas, 7** — who is at the very start of the second seven years, the stage this lecture is about — and **Eliyya, 4**, who is still in the first. Everything below is either the Shaykh's, labelled ✅, or mine, labelled ⚠.

## For Parents

### Daily practices

- ✅ **Say one true thing to Abbas each day about who he is, not about what he did.** The whole lecture turns on the child's felt worth [23:54]. "That was clever" is about output. "You are somebody who tells the truth even when it costs you" is about identity, and identity is what the *karim* consults under pressure.
- ✅ **Run the home test on yourself, silently, once a day** [40:05]. *Was the version of me my family saw today the same version my community saw?* No score, no journal entry — just the question. This is the only diagnostic in the lecture that requires nothing from the child.
- ⚠ **Answer question three of the four every day: use the name he likes.** It is the cheapest of the Shaykh's four diagnostics [55:11] and the one most easily lost in a rushed morning. ⚠ Note that "call people by their favourite name" is **not** a located hadith; do it because it is obviously right, not because a narration commands it.
- ✅ **Correct in private, always** [55:11]. If the correction cannot wait, take him out of the room first. The Imami warrant is **Nahj al-Balagha Hikmah 222** on *taghaful* — overlooking what you know — plus the Prophet's (s) practice of addressing a fault without naming the person.
- ⚠ **Guard your own eyes where he can see you doing it.** The lecture argues that *ma'siyah* degrades receptivity [22:53]. Turn the phone face-down when he enters. Look away, visibly, when something on a screen turns. He is learning *ghadd al-tarf* as a **movement**, and movements are learned by copying.
- ⚠ **One deliberate act of *mubadarah ila'l-'afw* per day** [56:11] — one thing you were entitled to be annoyed about that you let go of **first**, before anyone apologised, and out loud enough that the children notice the sequence.

### Weekly practices

- ✅ **The trust audit.** Once a week at the table, name the three kinds of *amanah* [11:45–14:47] and ask each child for one example from their own week: something borrowed, something told in confidence, something nobody wrote down. Keep it descriptive, not accusatory.
- ⚠ **Read Q 59:10 aloud on a fixed night**, and have everyone say the clause *wa la taj'al fi qulubina ghillan* together. ⚠ Frame it as the Qur'an teaching us what to ask for, **not** as a verse about our household — see §5.
- ⚠ **A weekly "who moved first?" review with your wife.** Over the past seven days, in the three or four small conflicts the family had, who took the initiative toward repair? If it was never a parent, the children have not seen the behaviour modelled.
- ✅ **One conversation about something he actually saw online this week** [24:54]. Not an inspection — a conversation. This is the *active mediation* arm, the only one with meta-analytic support, and it needs to be routine before it can be useful in a crisis.
- ⚠ **Ask Abbas his opinion about something that affects him, and then do what he says.** Once a week, on something small and real. Question one of the four [55:11] is not answerable by good intentions; it is answerable by instances.

### Habits to develop

- ⚠ **Name the source when you quote.** This is the habit the whole of §7 is about, and it transfers to parents. "Imam al-Sadiq (a) said, and it is in a book called *Tuhaf al-'Uqul*…" teaches a child that claims have provenance — which is, in the long run, worth more than any single narration.
- ⚠ **Convert rules into identity where you honestly can, and leave them as rules where you cannot.** "We don't speak like that — it's beneath us" is the *karamah* register [06:37]. But do not pretend everything is identity; some things are simply forbidden, and a child needs to know that too.
- ✅ **Stop escalating when repetition is making it worse** [51:08]. If you are on the fourth repetition of an instruction and the resistance is hardening, the transaction is no longer about the instruction. Change register, or leave the room.
- ⚠ **Build your citation shelf.** Four texts carry most of this lecture and all four are checkable: *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* p.319 (three signs of *karam*), al-Kafi 5 *Kitab al-Nikah* ch.178 (*la ghayrata fi'l-halal*), al-Kafi 2 ch.53 h.1 (the four *makarim*), and the Prophet's (s) *wasiyyah* to Abu Dharr (*al-majalis bi'l-amanah*). Learn those four and you can teach this entire lecture without any of its unverified material.
- ⚠ **Practise *taghaful* deliberately.** Choose, in advance, two categories of small fault you will visibly not notice this month. Nahj Hikmah 222 calls overlooking what you know one of the noblest acts of the *karim* — and it is also, practically, how you stop a coercive cycle before it starts.

## Mistakes to avoid

- ❌ **Do not repeat the *amanah* hadith as Prophetic** [09:40]. **Imam Ahmad called it *batil*.** Teach the ethic from **Q 4:58** and **Q 5:8**. If you have already taught it to Abbas as a hadith, correct it in front of him — that correction is itself the best possible lesson in *amanah*.
- ❌ **Do not tell the captured-assassin story** [30:59]. Unlocated, and the "Daesh-type terrorists" framing is a modern overlay on a report about the Prophet (s). If you want to teach that *ghayrah* is beloved to Allah, use «إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَيُورٌ يُحِبُّ كُلَّ غَيُورٍ» from al-Kafi 5 ch.177.
- ❌ **Do not tell a seven-year-old about the hooved creature** [44:05]. The moral — that bad character is genuinely ugly — is sound and available from al-Kafi's vinegar-and-honey narration. The monster is not needed, the hadith that frames it is unlocated, and the *akhirah* should not enter a child's imagination as a horror film.
- ⚠ **Do not let *ghayrah* become surveillance.** The lecture recommends protective jealousy and omits the brake. **al-Kafi 5/3/177 h.9** and **Nahj Letter 31** warn that misplaced *ghayrah* drives a sound woman toward sickness and an innocent one toward suspicion. If your protectiveness is producing suspicion, it has stopped being *ghayrah*.
- ⚠ **Do not conclude that your child's defiance is entirely your fault.** The lecture's two hardest case studies both resolve onto the parents' conduct [47:06, 56:11]. Parenting is one well-evidenced contributor to oppositional behaviour; it is not the only one, and temperament and heritable factors are substantial. Take the audit seriously; do not take it as a verdict.
- ⚠ **Do not treat filters as a plan** [24:54] — and do not throw them away either. They are worth a small, real *r+ = −.06*. What they are not worth is the confidence parents place in them.
- ❌ **Do not use "co-viewing" as your strategy.** Collier et al. 2016 puts simply watching alongside your child at **r+ = +.09** — associated with *worse* outcomes. Watching together and **saying nothing** is not mediation. Talking is.
- ⚠ **Do not dress the doctrine in science.** "Sin weakens willpower" is Qur'anic (83:14) and does not need ego depletion, which failed a 23-lab replication. Say *the Qur'an says the heart rusts*; do not say *studies show willpower is depleted*.
- ⚠ **Do not repeat "the leader said…" without a date and an occasion.** Three such attributions in this one lecture could not be confirmed. That is not proof they are false — the archives are blocked — but it is a reason to say "I have heard that…" rather than "the Leader says."

# 10. Child Application Activities

> ⚠ **All activities below are mine**, built from the lecture's verified content. Nothing here is attributed to the Shaykh except where a timestamp appears. Everything is designed to be cheap, short, and doable in an ordinary week. **Nothing here uses a narration that failed verification.**

## For Abbas (7)

Abbas is at the threshold of the second seven years — the exact stage this lecture addresses. At seven he can hold a category, follow a rule with a reason, and notice hypocrisy at fifty paces. Use all three.

### Activity A1 — The Three Boxes *(discussion prompt)*

**Objective.** Give him the category of *amanah* in all three of its forms, so that "don't tell people what happens in our house" and "give back the pencil" become one idea instead of two nags.
**Materials.** Three empty boxes, bowls or drawn circles on paper. Three slips of paper.
**Steps.**
1. Label the boxes **THINGS**, **WORDS**, **PROMISES NOBODY WROTE DOWN**.
2. Explain: an *amanah* is anything somebody puts in your hands. Sometimes it is an object. Sometimes it is something they told you. Sometimes it is just an understanding — like the fact that we don't wake the neighbours, even though nobody signed anything.
3. Ask him to think of one real example from his own week for each box, and put a slip in.
4. Then ask the hard question: *which one would be worst to break, and why?*
**Islamic lesson.** ***Al-majalis bi'l-amanah*** — "gatherings are held in trust" — from the Prophet's (s) *wasiyyah* to Abu Dharr. ✅ Verified source-family. And **Q 4:58**: Allah commands you to render trusts to their owners.
**Success indicators.** He generates his own examples rather than repeating yours; a week later, he uses the word *amanah* unprompted about something you did not raise.

### Activity A2 — The Aziz Story, stopped three times *(story)*

**Objective.** Let him reach the lecture's diagnosis himself instead of receiving it.
**Materials.** None.
**Steps.**
1. Tell the case study from [15:47–17:48] as a story about "a boy in another city." Stop after the **wallet**: *what should the dad do?*
2. Stop after the **school phone call**: *why is the dad more upset now than about the money?*
3. Stop after the **household secrets**: *why do you think the parents said they don't feel safe around him?*
4. Let him answer each time before you go on. Do not correct a wrong answer; ask another question.
**Islamic lesson.** *Khiyanah* escalates through the three forms of *amanah*, and the third is the one that breaks a household.
**Success indicators.** At step 3 he says something about trust rather than about punishment. ⚠ If he identifies with the boy, stop the activity and follow that instead — it is worth more than the lesson.

### Activity A3 — The Eyelid Drill *(character-building exercise)*

**Objective.** Turn *ghadd al-tarf* from an abstract prohibition into a physical reflex, at the age when reflexes are cheap to build.
**Materials.** None.
**Steps.**
1. Teach him al-Mizan's definition, in his words: *ghadd* means the eyelid closing on the eyelid. It is a **movement**, not a mood.
2. Practise it as a game, deliberately silly: you point at something across the room, he looks away first. Three seconds, then reset. Ten reps, laughing.
3. Then explain the real use in one sentence, with no lecture attached: *when your eyes land somewhere they shouldn't, that's the movement.*
4. ⚠ Use the Imami image, not the Sunni one: **the look is a poisoned arrow from Iblis's arrows** — al-Kafi, *Kitab al-Nikah*, *bab al-nazar*. It names an attacker rather than an appetite, which is far better for a seven-year-old.
**Islamic lesson.** Q 24:30 addresses believing **men first**; the response is a movement Allah described precisely.
**Success indicators.** He can explain what *ghadd* physically means. Months later, you see him do it once without being told — that is the whole return on this activity.

### Activity A4 — Repel It With Better *(real-life challenge)*

**Objective.** Give him one experiment in **41:34** with an honest brief, including the fact that it is hard.
**Materials.** A small notebook.
**Steps.**
1. Read him **Q 41:34** in translation: *good and evil are not equal; repel evil with what is better, and then the one between whom and you there was enmity becomes as though a devoted friend.*
2. Tell him the truth about it: **the very next verse says only patient people manage this, and that they have "a great share."** Do not sell it as a trick that always works.
3. The challenge: once this week, when somebody is unkind to him, do something *better* rather than something equal. Then write down in the notebook what happened — including if it did not work.
4. Read the notebook together at the end of the week. **Treat a failure as a valid result**, because 41:35 says it would be.
**Islamic lesson.** The Prophet (s) himself was given this instruction twice — **23:96 and 41:34** — and it is described as rare.
**Success indicators.** He tries it once. Not that it works.

### Activity A5 — Who Moved First? *(character-building, family-wide)*

**Objective.** Make *mubadarah ila'l-'afw* visible, countable and slightly competitive.
**Materials.** A sheet on the fridge with everyone's name, including both parents.
**Steps.**
1. Explain the maxim: *taking the initiative toward pardon is among the traits of the noble* — ⚠ Imam 'Ali (a), *Ghurar al-Hikam* 1566–1567, and say that it is a **saying collected in a book of wise words**, not a chain-verified report. He is old enough to be told that distinction, and being told it is part of the lesson.
2. Whenever there is a small falling-out, whoever moves first to repair it gets a tick — **and parents are on the sheet.**
3. At the end of the week, do not give a prize to the winner. Read out the total, and ask what it was like.
**Islamic lesson.** Nobility is measured by who moves first, not by who was right.
**Success indicators.** A parent's name has ticks on it. If it does not, the activity has still told you something important.

### Activity A6 — The Home Test, run on the family *(discussion, use with care)*

**Objective.** Apply [40:05] to the whole household, starting with the adults.
**Materials.** None.
**Steps.**
1. Ask at dinner: *is a person's real character the one strangers see, or the one their family sees?*
2. Then take the hit first: name one thing about **yourself** that is better in public than at home. Be specific and brief. Do not perform humility.
3. Invite — do not require — others to do the same. ⚠ **If nobody follows you, the activity is still complete.** Do not press a child on this.
**Islamic lesson.** *"Test how I am with my family when it doesn't matter"* [40:05].
**Success indicators.** Nobody was shamed. You went first.

### Reward ideas *(all experiences, none purchased)*

- ⚠ Deliberately calibrated to the lecture's own logic: rewards that **confer standing** rather than goods, because the whole thesis is that standing is what forms character.
- **He chooses the family's Friday night** — the meal, the outing, the film — and everyone abides by it. This is question one of the four, turned into a reward.
- **He leads one part of a family gathering**: gives the salaam at the door, or serves the guests first.
- **A late-night walk with you alone**, no siblings, no phone, and he sets the topic.
- **He teaches Eliyya something** he learned this week, formally, with everyone watching. Being trusted to teach is the highest-status reward available in a house.
- **His name on the family calendar** for a job that matters — locking up, checking the door, waking everyone for Fajr on a weekend.

## For Eliyya (4)

Eliyya is in the **first** seven years, where the lecture's own framework says the currency is play, affection and freedom — not instruction. Nothing below teaches her *khiyanah* or *ghayrah*. What she can absorb now is the **feeling** of being honoured and the **habit** of small honesty, which is what the second seven years will later be built on.

### Activity E1 — The Amanah Game *(play-based learning)*

**Objective.** Build the felt experience of being trusted with something, at four.
**Materials.** One object that genuinely matters a little — a small dish, a tasbih, a key on a ribbon.
**Steps.**
1. Hand it to her with visible ceremony: *"This is an amanah. I am giving it to you to look after until after dinner."*
2. Do not check on it. This is the whole activity.
3. When she returns it, thank her the way you would thank an adult — same tone, same length.
4. Increase the duration over weeks. A day. A weekend.
**Islamic lesson.** An *amanah* is given back to its owner. ✅ **Q 4:58.**
**Age-appropriate adaptation.** Keep the object small and the time short at first; a four-year-old's sense of time is the limiting factor, not her integrity. If she loses it, that is a chance to model *mubadarah ila'l-'afw* — you move first.

### Activity E2 — Look Away, Look Back *(play-based, emotional development)*

**Objective.** Make "looking away" a happy, neutral, physical game long before it is ever a moral instruction.
**Materials.** None.
**Steps.**
1. "When I say *ghadd*, we both cover our eyes. When I say *nazar*, we look." Swap who calls it.
2. Add a giggle rule: whoever looks when they shouldn't has to make the other one laugh.
3. Play for two minutes. Never longer. Never attach a moral.
**Islamic lesson.** None stated to her. ⚠ You are building the word and the movement, and nothing else. The meaning arrives in three years.
**Age-appropriate adaptation.** ⚠ **Do not explain why.** A four-year-old given a reason for gaze control will supply her own, and it will be worse than no reason.

### Activity E3 — "Don't Put It In Our Hearts" *(Islamic manners, bonding)*

**Objective.** Give her one Qur'anic clause she can say, and one feeling attached to it.
**Materials.** None.
**Steps.**
1. Teach only these four words: **رَبَّنَا … وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا** — *"Our Lord … do not put rancour in our hearts."*
2. Say it together after any squabble with her brother — not as a punishment, as a closing ritual, in the same voice you would use for *alhamdulillah*.
3. Explain *ghill* in one sentence a four-year-old can hold: *the yucky feeling that stays after you've stopped being angry.*
**Islamic lesson.** **Q 59:10.** ✅ Verified. al-Mizan: *ghill* is enmity. ⚠ Frame it as something the Qur'an teaches us to **ask for**, not as a verse about our family.
**Age-appropriate adaptation.** Four words is the whole target. Do not add the rest of the verse this year.

### Activity E4 — The Sorry Race *(emotional development)*

**Objective.** Attach a good feeling to moving first, before she is old enough to weigh who was right.
**Materials.** None.
**Steps.**
1. After a small conflict, say cheerfully: *"Who's going to be first?"*
2. Whoever gets there first — including you — gets a hug from everyone else.
3. ⚠ **Never use it to force an apology out of a genuinely wronged child.** If she was wronged, you go first, every time. That is the version of this activity that actually teaches something.
**Islamic lesson.** The noble move first.
**Age-appropriate adaptation.** At four the point is the **race**, not the remorse. Sincerity is a seven-year-old's problem.

### Activity E5 — Guest of Honour *(parent–child bonding, Islamic manners)*

**Objective.** Let her *feel* *karamah* rather than hear about it — the lecture's central claim applied at the age where feeling is all there is.
**Materials.** Whatever is already on the table.
**Steps.**
1. One meal a week, she is served **first**, greeted with a proper salaam at the door of the room, and asked her opinion on one thing before anyone else is asked.
2. Everyone plays it straight. No joking about it.
3. Rotate the role weekly through the whole family, including the parents.
**Islamic lesson.** Allah has honoured the children of Adam — **Q 17:70**, with al-Mizan's reading that the honouring is **intrinsic**, not comparative. Being served first is a picture of something already true.
**Age-appropriate adaptation.** ⚠ Rotate it. A four-year-old who is *always* the guest of honour learns entitlement, which is the counterfeit of *karamah*, not the thing itself.

### Activity E6 — Softly, With Your Own Name *(Islamic manners, bonding)*

**Objective.** Question three of the Shaykh's four diagnostics, run in reverse — let her hear her own name said well.
**Materials.** None.
**Steps.**
1. Ask her which version of her name she likes best. Ask seriously; wait for the answer.
2. Use it for a week, especially when correcting her.
3. Ask her, at the end of the week, whether she noticed.
**Islamic lesson.** ⚠ There is **no located hadith** commanding this — the Prophet's (s) practice of using *kunyas* is real, but the maxim is not. Do it because the lecture is right about it, not because a narration requires it. Being honest with yourself about that is the same discipline §7 is asking of the Shaykh.
**Age-appropriate adaptation.** She will probably choose something absurd. Use it anyway. That is the activity.

# 11. Character Development Map

> ⚠ **My extraction.** The traits are the lecture's; the compounding logic and the age projections are mine, built on what the lecture claims and what the verified sources support. Nothing here is a prediction about a real child.

## The traits this lecture builds

### 1. *Karamah* — felt worth before Allah *(the root)*
Everything else is downstream. It is **received, not achieved** — al-Mizan on 17:70: *takrim* is «معنى نفسي», intrinsic. A parent's job is disclosure, not conferral.
**Observable at 7:** he does not need to win to be all right. **At 14:** he does not need to be seen to be all right. **At 21:** he does not need to be liked to be all right.

### 2. *Amanah* — fidelity to what is placed in your hands
Three species: objects, information, unwritten expectations [11:45–14:47].
**Compounds into:** reliability, and eventually into the only reputation that survives adolescence — the one his friends have of him when he is not there.

### 3. Truthfulness against oneself
⭐ The item the Shaykh **omitted** from *makarim al-akhlaq* (al-Kafi 2/53/1, *hasan ka'l-sahih*) and the one his own lecture needed. It is the bridge from N4 ("the *karim* won't lie to save himself") to the closing section.
**Compounds into:** the capacity to be corrected — which is the single most valuable trait a teenager can carry into a bad decision.

### 4. *Ghadd al-tarf* — restraint of the eyes
A **movement**, per al-Mizan («إطباق الجفن على الجفن»), not a mood.
**Compounds into:** the general capacity to not do a thing you want to do, which is what the other five traits are made of.

### 5. *Haya'* — modesty as disposition, not compliance
«الْحَيَاءُ تَمَامُ الْكَرَمِ وَأَحْسَنُ الشِّيَمِ» — a *shimah*, an **ingrained disposition**. ⚠ Chainless, but the lexical point stands.
**Compounds into:** *'iffah*, and into the resistance to being embarrassed out of a position.

### 6. *Kazm al-ghayz* — swallowing anger
One of the three signs [23:54]; Qur'anic in its own right (3:134).
**Compounds into:** the ability to stay in a difficult conversation, which is the whole of marriage and most of work.

### 7. *Husn al-khuluq* — character that is the same at home
The lecture's sharpest test [40:05].
**Compounds into:** integrity in the literal sense — being one thing rather than several.

### 8. *Mubadarah ila'l-'afw* — moving first toward pardon
⚠ *Ghurar* 1566–1567; chainless. The Qur'anic version is stronger: **41:34**.
**Compounds into:** a person who does not accumulate enemies, and a household that does not accumulate history.

## How they compound

```
   kazm al-ghayz  ->  patience (41:35)           keeping confidence -> being trusted

```

The load-bearing claim of the diagram, and of the lecture, is the **top arrow**: restraint and honesty are not separately installed virtues but two expressions of one settled self-estimate. **Nahj al-Balagha Hikmah 449** states the mechanism in eight words — *whoever's soul is precious to him, his appetite is cheap to him* — and it is verified.

## What the child looks like

> ⚠ **These are illustrative sketches, not developmental claims.** The shared index records that the popular assertion "personality is largely formed by age seven" is **overstated on both grounds**: the hadith divides *upbringing* into stages rather than closing formation, and Roberts & DelVecchio's meta-analysis of 152 longitudinal studies puts rank-order stability at roughly **.31 in childhood**, rising to ~.54 by college and plateauing near .74 only at 50–70. **Formation is long.** Nothing below is fixed at any of these ages.

### At 7 — the beginning of the second seven years *(Abbas, now)*
- Can name what an *amanah* is and give one example of each of the three kinds.
- Returns things without being asked more often than not.
- Owns a mistake **once in a while**, and is not punished on the occasions he does.
- Can look away on request, and treats it as a physical thing rather than a shameful one.
- Has heard his father say "I was wrong" at least once this month.
- ⚠ Still lies sometimes. Lying is a developmental milestone, not a verdict — the index records Talwar & Lee (2002) on exactly this. A seven-year-old who never lies is not necessarily further along; he may simply be more frightened.

### At 14 — the end of the stage this lecture governs
- Has a category for "this is beneath me," and uses it about himself rather than about others.
- Keeps confidences, including his parents'. ⚠ This is the single best observable indicator that the *amanah* teaching landed.
- Can be told he is wrong without the conversation becoming about his standing.
- Has an internal answer to *"is it really that bad?"* — the *la'im*'s question at [31:59] — that is not fear of punishment.
- Moves first, sometimes, after a fight. Not always.
- ⚠ Is probably arguing with you about something. That is not a failure of *tarbiyah*; the second seven years end at *balagh* and the third stage is counsel, not command.

### At 21 — the outcome the lecture is aiming at
- Is the same person in his house as in the world [40:05].
- Discharges trusts nobody is checking.
- Tells the truth when it costs him — the fourth *makarim* item, al-Kafi 2/53/1.
- Meets an injury with something better, sometimes, and knows from **41:35** that this is rare and requires *sabr* rather than assuming it is easy.
- Does not need to be quick to punish, because he does not need the demonstration [52:08].
- ⚠ Knows the difference between a verse, a graded hadith, and a beautiful maxim — which, if this document has any lasting use, is the thing it most wants to hand on.

## Core principles

1. **Dignity is upstream of every behaviour you are enforcing.** Work upstream.
2. **The window is temporary and you know its length.** Build what survives your absence.
3. **All three signs of *karam* are restraint under pressure.** Watch for restraint, not for piety displays.
4. **An *amanah* can be an object, a word, or an understanding.** Teach all three or the child learns one.
5. **Sin degrades receptivity, so it degrades your leverage** — including your own sin, on your own leverage.
6. **Filters buy little; conversation buys most; watching together in silence buys less than nothing.**
7. **Character is measured at home, when nothing is at stake.**
8. **Repetition of a command is fuel in a coercive cycle**, not pressure.
9. **The noble move first.** Not fastest — first.
10. ⚠ **And: say what your sources are.** A maxim is not a hadith; a *sahih* Sunni hadith is not an Imami chain; an unreachable archive is not a refutation.

## Parent responsibilities

- Disclose the child's worth; do not pretend to confer it.
- Answer the four diagnostic questions honestly, in writing, with your spouse.
- Correct in private, without exception.
- Guard your own gaze visibly, and your own tongue at home.
- Move first toward pardon where the children can see the sequence.
- Cite your sources when you teach, and say when you are unsure.
- Keep the brake on *ghayrah*: not in the lawful, never as suspicion of the innocent.
- Decide the device policy before it is contested.

## Child responsibilities *(stage-dependent)*

- **0–7 (Eliyya):** be loved, play, be honoured at the table, learn four words of a du'a. No moral instruction on *khiyanah* or *ghayrah*.
- **7–14 (Abbas):** learn the lawful and the unlawful; learn the three species of *amanah*; practise the eyelid; own a mistake out loud; try 41:34 once and report honestly.
- **14–21:** counsel rather than command; the truth against oneself; initiative toward pardon; the beginnings of *'iffah* as a chosen stance rather than an inherited rule.

## Family culture practices

- One meal a week with a rotating guest of honour, served first and asked first.
- Q 59:10's clause said aloud together after conflicts, in the same register as *alhamdulillah*.
- A visible "who moved first?" tally that includes the parents.
- Household information treated explicitly as *amanah*, with the two named exceptions.
- Corrections taken out of the room.
- Screens discussed weekly with no consequence attached to disclosure.

# 13. Common Mistakes and Misunderstandings

## Mistakes the lecture addresses directly

**1. Believing that parental controls solve the device problem.** ✅ [24:54] He is right, and the evidence is stronger than he presents. ⚠ The precise version: filters are worth a small real effect (**r+ = −.06**), conversation is worth much more, and **co-viewing in silence is worth less than nothing** (**r+ = +.09**). The mistake is not installing a filter; it is *counting on it*.

**2. Confusing enforced behaviour with formed character.** ✅ [39:05] You can compel a smile. You cannot compel *husn al-khuluq*, and the difference shows up at home.

**3. Escalating a command against a child who is using disobedience as revenge.** ✅ [51:08] The best practical insight in the lecture. Each repetition is another opportunity for the child to punish you.

**4. Correcting a child in public.** ✅ [55:11] With real Imami warrant — Nahj Hikmah 222 on *taghaful*.

**5. Thinking a school is Islamic because of its food and its demographics.** ✅ [07:38]

**6. Mistaking a near-death account for information about the *akhirah*.** ✅ **He makes this point himself, twice** [43:05] — "this is not the akhirah… not even a hundredth." ⚠ And then partly undoes it by using the account as evidence for a doctrinal claim.

## Mistakes the lecture makes, or risks

**7. ⚠ Attributing a household's problems entirely to how the parents speak.** Both of the lecture's hard case studies — Haydar [47:06] and the honest mother [56:11] — resolve completely onto parental conduct. The behaviour cluster described is recognisably the DSM-5 picture of **oppositional defiant disorder**, including the vindictiveness criterion, and ODD is **multiply determined**: temperament and heritable factors are substantial contributors alongside parenting. In a pastoral setting, pointing at the variable the parent controls is often the right move. On paper it needs a warning: *this framework is a hypothesis worth testing, not a diagnosis, and a parent whose child does not improve has not thereby been proved guilty.*

**8. ⚠ Recommending *ghayrah* without its brake.** See §6, N14. The tradition regulates *ghayrah* in both directions and the lecture gives only one.

**9. ⚠ Treating chainless maxims as hadith.** Nine items in this lecture. Not an error of quotation — an error of labelling. See §7.

**10. ⚠ Announcing an enumeration and supplying one item.** Twice [14:47, 30:59], and both times the underlying narration turned out to be unlocated.

**11. ⚠ Reaching for psychology to bolster doctrine.** "Sin weakens willpower" [22:53] is sound Qur'anic teaching (83:14) and does **not** need ego depletion, which failed a 23-lab preregistered replication.

**12. ⚠ Circumstantial detail in an unverified story.** Eighteen toilets, a week, a colonel's wife, "45 steps this way" [35:02]. The detail is what makes a listener hear devotional memoir as documentary record. One clause fixes it: *"as his comrades relate in his biography."*

## Cultural practices commonly confused with Islam

**13. ❌ "Ghayrah means keeping tabs on the women of the household."** Not the Imami position. **al-Kafi 5, *Kitab al-Nikah*, ch.178**: «لا غيرة في الحلال» — no *ghayrah* in what is lawful. **al-Kafi 5/3/177 h.9** and **Nahj Letter 31**: misplaced *ghayrah* drives a sound woman toward sickness and an innocent one toward suspicion. Protectiveness that produces suspicion has changed category.

**14. ❌ "A woman's jealousy is disbelief" as a general put-down.** Nahj Hikmah 124 is real and it is restricted — by al-Kafi 5 ch.178, by Ibn Abi'l-Hadid, by 'Abduh, by Makarim Shirazi — to objection against what the Shari'ah has permitted. ✅ **The Shaykh restricts it correctly.** The cultural misuse of the line, stripped of that restriction, is not his and should not be laid at his door — but it is common, and a daughter will meet it.

**15. ❌ "Modesty is a women's subject."** ✅ **The Qur'an addresses men first** (24:30 before 24:31) and the Shaykh says so [21:49]. A household in which *hijab* is discussed and *ghadd al-basar* is not has inverted the Qur'an's own order.

**16. ⚠ "Islamic upbringing means strictness."** The lecture's entire argument is the opposite: strictness is what you are left with when *karamah* was not installed. ⚠ Note the shared index's finding, from the wider series, that the Imami disciplinary material is markedly restrained — «لا تضربه، واهجره ولا تطل» ("do not strike him; withdraw briefly, and do not prolong it"), itself from a **late, anonymous-narrator source**, is about as far as it goes, and al-Khoei's position on physical discipline is correspondingly narrow.

**17. ❌ "That's her job" — housework and childcare as the wife's obligation.** Not Islamic law. **Sistani, *Islamic Laws*, ruling 2432: "A man has no right to compel his wife to do housework."** The obligations set out at 2430 exclude domestic labour and childcare. This is in the shared index from an earlier lecture and it belongs in any household discussion arising from this one.

**18. ⚠ "Foul language is just how boys talk."** The lecture answers this well [06:37], and the honest Imami text for it is **Nahj Khutbah 206** — said by Amir al-Mu'minin (a) to his **own soldiers, at war, about the enemy**.

**19. ⚠ "If my child lies, my tarbiyah has failed."** Half true at best. Lying is a normal developmental milestone (Talwar & Lee 2002, in the shared index). ⭐ **And the relevant research runs the other way from most parents' instinct:** Talwar & Lee (2011), a natural experiment across two matched West African schools, found that children in the **punitive** school **lied more, and lied better**. Punishment for lying selects for skill at lying.

**20. ⚠ "Devotional memoir is history."** *Salam bar Ibrahim*, *Khak-ha-ye Narm-e Kushk* and *Nime-ye Penhan-e Mah* are real books about real people, and the men in them are real martyrs whose dates and units check out. The anecdotes inside them are **comrades' recollections gathered decades later**. Both facts are true at once, and a child can be told both.

## Key concepts

***Karamah*** = God-given dignity, intrinsic (17:70) · ***Amanah*** = anything placed in your hands: object, word, or unwritten expectation · ***Wadi'ah*** = a material deposit · ***Khiyanah*** = betrayal of any of the three · ***Ghadd al-tarf*** = the eyelid closing on the eyelid · ***Haya'*** = modesty as a *shimah*, an ingrained disposition · ***'Iffah*** = chastity as a stance · ***Ghayrah*** = protectiveness — **none in the lawful, never as suspicion** · ***Kazm al-ghayz*** = swallowing anger · ***Husn al-khuluq*** = character that is the same at home · ***Mubadarah ila'l-'afw*** = **moving first** toward pardon · ***Taghaful*** = deliberately overlooking a fault you know about · ***Karim / la'im*** = the noble / the base · ***Ghill*** = enmity, rancour that stays

## Key parenting principles

1. Dignity is upstream; behaviour is downstream.
2. The window is temporary; build what survives your absence.
3. All three signs of *karam* are restraint under pressure.
4. Trust has three forms; teach all three.
5. Sin degrades receptivity — including yours.
6. Filters buy little; conversation buys most.
7. Real character is what happens at home.
8. Repetition is fuel in a coercive cycle.
9. The noble move first, not fastest.
10. Say what your sources are.

## Most important action items

- **Ask his opinion — and follow it.** Once a week, on something real.
- **Correct in private. Every time.**
- **Use the name he likes**, especially when you are annoyed.
- **Move first toward pardon**, visibly, where the children can see the order of events.
- **Talk about screens weekly** with no consequence attached to disclosure.

# 16. Golden Nuggets

> **How to use this section.** These are the lines from this lecture worth carrying away. Each is quoted with its timestamp; ASR mangling has been cleaned silently for readability, and nothing has been changed in meaning. Where a nugget is **the Shaykh's own formulation** rather than a narration, it says so — that distinction is the point of §7 and it applies here too. Where a nugget rides on a citation that did not survive verification, the note says which, and gives what to say instead.

---

### 1. The question the whole lecture is built to answer

> **"How long are we going to be able to be next to our children? … Are we going to hover over them? Right now we have this window where we're trying to install *karamah*, but we know that this is a temporary window."** [23:54]

⭐ **His own formulation, and the best sentence in the lecture.** It reframes every argument a parent has ever had about rules: the question is not *what will he obey today*, it is *what will hold when I am not in the room*. Everything in §8 and §15 exists to answer it.

---

### 2. Dignity, defined by behaviour

> **"A person who has *karamah* won't try to save themselves by lying to get out of it. They'll take it on the chin and they will own up to their mistakes."** [10:40]

⭐ **His gloss on N4** — the maxim itself («الْكَذِبُ وَالْخِيَانَةُ لَيْسَا مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ», Imam 'Ali (a), chainless) is one sentence; this is what makes it usable. It converts *karamah* from a feeling into an observable act: **owning the mistake is what dignity looks like from outside.** ⚠ Note that this is exactly the fourth *makarim al-akhlaq* item — truthfulness even against oneself — that he loses at [58:14] by taking the weaker recension (§6, N22).

---

### 3. The three signs — the spine

> **«ثَلَاثَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى كَرَمِ الْمَرْءِ: حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ، وَكَظْمُ الْغَيْظِ، وَغَضُّ الطَّرْفِ»** — "Three things indicate a man's nobility: good character, swallowing anger, and lowering the gaze." [23:54]

✅ **A narration, not his own** — Imam Ja'far al-Sadiq (a), ***Tuhaf al-'Uqul* p. 319**, correctly attributed and correctly quoted. ⚠ Chainless and ungradeable in principle (§6, N10). ⭐ The insight he never states: **all three are forms of self-restraint under pressure**, which is precisely why dignity can be the cause and compliance the effect.

---

### 4. What an Islamic school actually is

> **"An Islamic school is not a school where they serve halal pizza."** [07:38] — **"The bare minimum is that public sin is not tolerated or institutionalised."** [08:38]

⭐ **His own formulation**, and the most quotable line in the first ten minutes. ⚠ Not a citation and he does not offer it as one. The positive half of the same thought has an excellent available text he does not use: **Nahj al-Balagha, Hikam 73** on the company that forms a person.

---

### 5. Filters are not a plan

> **"What I'll do is I'll give my children a device, but then I'm so clever, I'm going to put controls on it … Children are a lot smarter than we think. That solution — that's a failed experiment."** [24:54]

⭐ **His own claim, and it is the best-evidenced sentence in the entire series.** Przybylski & Nash (2018) found parental-control software essentially unrelated to whether adolescents encounter online harm (n = 13,176, plus a preregistered n = 1,004 replication). ⚠ The honest counterweight from §7: Collier et al. (2016) puts restrictive mediation at a **small but real *r+* = −.06**, and *co-viewing* — watching alongside and saying nothing — at **+.09**, i.e. associated with worse outcomes. So: filters buy a little, silence buys nothing, and **talking is the arm the literature supports** — which is the fifty minutes he then spends.

---

### 6. The unwritten contract

> **"It's not only a written and signed document. Sometimes there are unwritten agreements between individuals, and those are also expected to be honoured."** [14:47]

✅ **His extension of N7** («سُنَّةُ الْكِرَامِ الْوَفَاءُ بِالْعُهُودِ», *Ghurar al-Hikam* 5556–5557), and textually defensible — *'uhud* has never meant only signed instruments. ⭐ This is the most useful of the three species of *amanah* for a family, because almost everything inside a house runs on it: turns, bedtimes, what gets repeated outside, how a sibling's things are treated. ❌ Do **not** carry away the "four signs of a *kha'in*" that follows it in the same breath — unlocated (§6, N8). For the neighbour, use **Bukhari 6014 / Muslim 2624**: *Jibril kept counselling me about the neighbour until I thought he would make him an heir.*

---

### 7. Gatherings are held in trust

> **«إِفْشَاؤُكَ سِرَّ أَخِيكَ خِيَانَةٌ، فَاجْتَنِبْ ذَلِكَ»** — "Your divulging your brother's secret is treachery, so avoid that." [13:46]

✅ **A narration, correctly attributed to the Prophet (s)** — a clause of the *wasiyyah* to Abu Dharr (§6, N6). ⭐ And the clause he drops is the one a parent most needs: **«الْمَجَالِسُ بِالْأَمَانَةِ»** — *gatherings are held in trust*. That is the whole answer to Aziz's son, who trades what happens at home for popularity at school [17:48]. Teach the two clauses together.

---

### 8. Where the real character lives

> **"When you see the real me is when I'm home and I'm with my family. That's when the real me comes out."** [40:05] — **"If you really want to know how my *akhlaq* is, then test how I am with my family when it doesn't matter. Do the gloves come off?"** [40:05]

⭐ **His own formulation, and the most uncomfortable diagnostic in the lecture** — because it is aimed at the adult in the room, not the child. It needs no citation and it survives every audit in §7. Pair it with the preceding line: **"Sometimes I can enforce *akhlaqi* behaviour … but that doesn't mean that you've installed good *akhlaq*."** [39:05]

---

### 9. The seconds-long battle

> Hamza is added to a Telegram channel at bedtime. **"One voice is telling him, 'Just get away, leave.' The other voice is telling him, 'Well, let's see what it is.' And then he remembers Allah and who he is: *you're a soldier of the Imam*. … He leaves the channel and he goes to bed, and he feels a sense of victory."** [25:55]

⭐ **His own case study**, and the best piece of teaching design in the lecture: he does not describe the outcome, he describes the **thirty seconds inside the child's head**. ⚠ It is illustrative fiction, and unlike the assassin story [30:59] it is not attributed to anyone — which is why it costs nothing and can be used freely.

---

### 10. Why repeating yourself makes it worse

> **"For the person who's getting revenge, every time you tell me 'do this,' that's another time of me punching back and punishing you by being disobedient."** [51:08]

⭐ **His own clinical description, and it is the most practically valuable paragraph in the second half.** It explains why the fourth repetition of an instruction hardens rather than resolves: in a coercive cycle, **your repetition is the child's opportunity**. ⚠ §7 records the fair caveat — the picture matches the DSM-5 oppositional profile including its vindictiveness criterion, and that profile is multiply determined; do not read it as a verdict on your parenting alone.

---

### 11. Quick to punish

> **"Those people who are low, those people who don't feel good about themselves — one of the signs is that I want to punish, and I'm quick to punish if somebody does something wrong."** [52:08]

✅ Paraphrase of a real maxim he does not name: **«لَيْسَ مِنْ شِيَمِ الْكِرَامِ تَعْجِيلُ الِانْتِقَامِ»**, *Ghurar al-Hikam* **7490** (chainless). ⭐ The reading he does not offer, and the one worth carrying: **read it against the father.** A parent who is quick to punish is displaying, on this maxim's own terms, the trait he is trying to remove from his son.

### 12. First, not fastest

> **«الْمُبَادَرَةُ إِلَى الْعَفْوِ مِنْ أَخْلَاقِ الْكِرَامِ»** — "**Taking the initiative** toward pardon is among the traits of the noble." [56:11]

✅ A real maxim, ***Ghurar al-Hikam* 1566–1567**, Imam 'Ali (a); chainless. ⚠ **One word correction**: the attested verb is *mubadarah*, not *musara'ah* as delivered — and the attested word is the better one for a parent. **"Quick to forgive" describes how fast you get there once asked. "First to move" describes who crosses the room before anyone apologises** — and that is the thing a child actually learns by watching.

---

### 13. The four questions

> **"When was the last time you asked your son for his opinion on something — and then you went with his opinion?"** [55:11]

⭐ **His own diagnostic, and the finest pastoral move in the lecture.** A mother describes a son who slashes tyres and smiles about it; he asks nothing about consequences and four things about how she speaks to him. ✅ Three of the four have Imami warrant (consultation — 3:159, 42:38; private correction — Nahj Hikmah 222 on *taghaful*; tone of command — his own). ⚠ "Use the name he likes" is **not** a located narration, though to his credit he never quotes it as one.

### 14. The verdict, in one sentence

> **"This child has never felt that sense of honour inside their house."** [56:11]

⭐ **His own summation**, and the thesis of Parts 14 and 15 compressed to eleven words. ⚠ It is also where the lecture's one pastoral danger sits (§13): a true diagnosis, delivered as a total explanation. Take it as the first question to ask, not as the answer.

### 15. Honesty about his own devices

> **"Can I tell you a story that's not true first? I'm going to tell you a story that's not true."** [04:35]

⭐ In a genre where illustrative fables routinely acquire chains and become hadith within a generation, that sentence is a real discipline and it deserves to be said out loud. ⚠ It also makes the contrast sharper: the same speaker introduces the *amanah* hadith at [09:40] with *"This is from Rasulullah"* — a text Imam Ahmad declared *batil*. **He knows how to flag fiction. The failures are of sourcing, not of candour.**

### 16. The closing warning, and it is aimed at us

> **"Sometimes what happens is either people despair, or they start to reinvent Islam so that I can feel good about myself — instead of keeping these Islamic ideals and saying that we have to work towards the Islamic ideals."** [58:14]

⭐ **His own, and the right note to end on.** The standards in this lecture are high enough to make a parent flinch: repay harm with good, be first to pardon, be the same person at home. His answer is neither to lower the standard nor to pretend you have met it. ⚠ It is worth noticing that this sentence is the best possible argument for §7: **an ideal you cannot check is not an ideal, it is a rumour.** Keep the standard; fix the citation.

## ⚠ My inference — not the speaker's, and not any source's

*(Everything below is my synthesis. It is labelled in the body of the document too. None of it should be repeated as though it came from the lecture or from a text.)*

- ⚠ That *ghadd* as **"eyelid closing on eyelid"** makes the gaze **teachable to a young child as a movement rather than a mood** — *when you see something you shouldn't, the first thing that moves is your eyelid*. Al-Mizan supplies the lexical fact; the pedagogy is mine.

- ⚠ That the internal logic of the three signs is that **all three are self-restraint under pressure**, and that this is *why* dignity can be upstream of compliance. The Shaykh never states it.
- ⚠ That a father who is **quick to punish** is displaying the *la'im*'s trait from *Ghurar* 7490 while trying to remove it from his son.

| **Ibn Hazm** | «**لَا يَصِحّ**» |
| **Ibn al-Mundhir** | «**غَيْرُ ثَابِت**» |
| **Ibn al-Mulaqqin** | six routes, **all weak** |
| **Ibn al-Qayyim / Ibn Hazm** | the al-Hasan al-Basri route is ***mursal*** |
| **al-Tirmidhi** — *the sole dissent* | «**حَسَنٌ غَرِيب**», *Sunan* **1264** |

❌ **No Imami chain located** — no al-Kafi, al-Saduq or al-Tusi placement for this wording. It is Sunni in provenance and was given to a Shia audience as "from Rasulullah" with no source named.

⭐ **Substitutes, and they are stronger than the original:** **Q 4:58** for the *amanah* half — with al-Mizan's «يعم الأمانات المالية وغيرها من المعنويات», which also supplies the taxonomy he builds by hand — and **Q 5:8** for the "do not betray the betrayer" half: *do not let the hatred of a people lead you into injustice.* ⭐ **The lecture's argument survives the correction entirely intact**, which is exactly why the correction costs nothing and should be made.

### ⭐ The spine: correctly quoted, but not what it was called

**N10 — «ثَلَاثَةٌ تَدُلُّ عَلَى كَرَمِ الْمَرْءِ» [23:54], presented as *"This is now hadith."***

✅ **Located and pinned**: Imam al-Sadiq (a), ***Tuhaf al-'Uqul* p. 319**. ❌ **Chainless, and ungradeable in principle** — Ibn Shu'ba al-Harrani omits every isnad in the book by his own declared method. For the **load-bearing text of a 59-minute argument**, a listener deserves to be told that it comes from a chainless 4th/10th-century maxim collection: **corroborative, not probative**. ⭐ Its corroboration is nonetheless unusually strong, because each of the three limbs is independently attested in graded material (*husn al-khuluq* in al-Kafi's *bab husn al-khuluq*; *kazm al-ghayz* in Q 3:134 and the graded *kazm* narrations; *ghadd al-tarf* in Q 24:30–31 with the al-Kafi gloss). ⚠ Two precisions: the Arabic is **غَضُّ الطَّرْفِ (*ghadd al-tarf*)**, not *ghadd al-basar* as delivered at [24:54] — the maxim and Q 24:30 **converge**, the maxim does not quote the verse; and at [41:05] **the reprise collapses** mid-sentence and the three items are never restated.

### ⭐ Misattributed — right text, wrong speaker

**N21 — «مَنْ لَمْ يُجَازِ الْإِسَاءَةَ بِالْإِحْسَانِ فَلَيْسَ مِنَ الْكِرَامِ» [57:13], given twice to the Prophet (s).**

❌ It is ***Ghurar al-Hikam* entry 8958 — Imam 'Ali (a)**, not the Prophet (s); chainless and ungradeable. ⭐ **In Part 16 the Shaykh cites the same maxim and attributes it correctly to Amir al-Mu'minin (a)** — same speaker, same sentence, two lectures, two attributions. ⭐ And the Qur'anic anchor for the identical principle is far stronger ground, and he had used it **thirty seconds earlier**: **Q 41:34**, *idfa' bi'llati hiya ahsan*, verified above with al-Mizan. **Lead with the verse; keep the maxim as illustration.** ⚠ "Litmus test" is also a strong reading to place on a chainless maxim, though he immediately and creditably acknowledges how hard the standard is.

### ⭐ The weaker recension, at real cost

**N22 — *makarim al-akhlaq* as **three** items addressed to Amir al-Mu'minin (a) [58:14].**

⚠ **Wrong recension.** The three-item, addressed-to-'Ali version exists but is **Sunni** (al-Bayhaqi, al-Tabarani) and graded ***da'if***. The best-graded Imami text is **al-Kafi 2, *Kitab al-Iman wa'l-Kufr*, ch. 53, h. 1** — **al-Majlisi, *Mir'at al-'Uqul*: *hasan ka'l-sahih*** — where the Prophet (s) addresses a **public gathering** and gives **four** items: reconnect with the one who cut you off, give the one who denied you, pardon the one who wronged you, **and speak the truth even against yourself**. ⭐ **The omitted fourth item is the one his own argument needed.** This lecture opens on a boy who lies and steals, and its thesis is that *karamah* shows up as honesty — *"a person who has karamah won't try to save themselves by lying"* [10:40]. **The al-Kafi text ties his first section to his last, and he drops it.** For a Shia audience the inversion is doubly odd: the version preferred is the Sunni weak one, the version omitted is the Imami strong one. ⭐ **Cite al-Kafi 2/53/1 and give all four** — the single easiest upgrade available in the lecture.

### ❌ Not located — do not attribute

- **N8 — "the signs of a *kha'in* are four" [14:47].** ❌ Nothing in dorar.net for a four-item *'alamat al-kha'in* enumeration; al-Kafi's trust material checked. **He supplies one of the four.** ⭐ Substitute the Jibril-and-the-neighbour hadith (**Bukhari 6014 / Muslim 2624, *sahih***) and al-Kafi 2, *bab haqq al-jiwar*.
- **N15 — the captured assassin spared for five traits [30:59].** ❌ Absence-checked against dorar.net and against **al-Kafi 5 ch. 177**, the dedicated *ghayrah* chapter, **all nine hadith read**; a further Arabic search on «الغيرة الشديدة على حرمك» with the five-traits frame returned only modern sermon pages. **He supplies one of the five.** ⚠ **"Daesh-type terrorists" is his own overlay, repeated five times** — no version of any comparable report frames the Prophet's (s) captives that way, and the anachronism pre-loads the audience's verdict on people whose execution is being described. It must not be heard as part of the narration.
- **N17 — "if *husn al-khuluq* were a creation you could see…" [41:05–42:05], introduced as *"the Prophet says this"* with Arabic given for both halves.** ❌ Not located in either corpus, including the Shia topical compilation *al-Khuluq fi aqwal al-Nabi wa Ali Baytih* (43 entries, none matching). The nearest attested text — «لَوْ كَانَ حُسْنُ الْخُلُقِ رَجُلًا يَمْشِي فِي النَّاسِ لَكَانَ رَجُلًا صَالِحًا» — is graded ***da'if* to *da'if jiddan*** by the Sunni critics indexed at dorar.net, makes a **much smaller claim**, and **has no ugliness clause at all**. ⭐ This matters more than a stray misattribution would, because it is the **hinge into the near-death sequence**: the whole "bad *akhlaq* is objectively hideous and someone has *seen* it" movement rests on it. ⚠ Note also the slide between two propositions — *"if it were a creation we could **see**"* is a **thought experiment**; the Azimi account is offered as a **report that someone did see it**. Even if the narration existed it would license only the first. ⭐ Substitute **al-Kafi 2, *bab su' al-khuluq***: bad character spoils deeds as vinegar spoils honey.
- **N2 — «لَا تَتَكَلَّمُوا بِالْفُحْشِ» with the "not worthy of us / of our Shia" clause [06:37–07:38].** ❌ Not located with this wording. ✅ Content entirely sound and thoroughly Imami. ⭐ Substitute **Nahj al-Balagha Khutbah 206** — «إِنِّي أَكْرَهُ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَكُونُوا سَبَّابِينَ», said at Siffin to his own army about the enemy — which is a **stronger** frame, and which he himself used correctly in Part 14.

- **Both Borunsi stories [35:02–38:05].** ✅ The man is real and precisely identifiable. ❌ Neither the landmine account nor the military-service account could be located in any published source.
- **"What we learned from Qasim: if you want to become a shahid you have to live like a shahid" [35:02].** ❌ Not located. ⚠ Mitigated by the fact that he does not deliver it as a direct quotation.
- **"Call people by the name they like" [55:11].** ❌ Not located as a saying. ✅ **Mitigated entirely** — he does not quote it as one; it is his own diagnostic question.

## Where he is right, and it should not be lost in the corrections

- ⭐ **"Twice the Prophet is told to meet a bad deed with a good deed" [57:13] is exactly right** — down to the grammatical person of the verb in all four relevant verses. **23:96** and **41:34** carry the second-person **singular imperative** ٱدْفَعْ addressed to the Prophet (s); **13:22** and **28:54** carry the third-person plural descriptive وَيَدْرَءُونَ, of *ulu'l-albab* and of the People of the Book who believed. All four checked directly against the Arabic. **This is the sort of count that usually collapses on checking, and it did not.**
- ⭐ **The spine narration is real, correctly attributed, and pinned to a page** — *Tuhaf al-'Uqul* 319, Imam al-Sadiq (a). For a lecture of this length to rest on a text that survives verification is not the norm in this series.
- ✅ **He handles the hardest text in the lecture responsibly.** Nahj al-Balagha **Hikmah 124** on a woman's *ghayrah* is a line most speakers either avoid or weaponise. He raises it **unprompted** and immediately restricts it to the co-marriage case, which is the mainstream Imami reading of Ibn Abi'l-Hadid, 'Abduh and Makarim Shirazi. ⚠ The one thing he does not say is that the restriction is **an interpretation** — and **al-Kafi 5 ch. 178**, «لَا غَيْرَةَ فِي الْحَلَالِ», is the chapter whose very title is that restriction. He makes the right move on his own authority when al-Kulayni had made it for him eleven centuries ago.
- ⭐ **He flags his fiction before telling it** — *"Can I tell you a story that's not true first?"* [04:35]. In a genre where illustrative fables routinely acquire chains within a generation, that is a discipline worth naming.
- ⭐ **He preserves Nasrallah's own "not a fatwa" caveat** rather than upgrading a personal opinion into a ruling [34:01].
- ⭐ **He identifies Behrouz Azimi exactly** — programme, season, episode and an account matching the press coverage headline for headline [43:05].
- ⭐ **His one hard empirical claim is right, and better supported than he knew.** "Parental controls are a failed experiment" [24:54] is **Przybylski & Nash 2018** — and the remedy he then spends fifty minutes recommending, *talking to the child*, is the arm the meta-analytic literature actually supports. ⚠ With the honest counterweight kept in view: **Collier et al. 2016**, restrictive mediation ***r+* = −.06** — small, but real, and not nothing.
- ⭐ **He qualifies the near-death material himself**: *"this is not the akhirah … not even a hundredth of what the akhirah is"* [43:05].
- ✅ **In twenty-two narrations there is not one garbled or invented Arabic text.** Where the wording needs correcting — *ghadd al-tarf* not *ghadd al-basar*; *mubadarah* not *musara'ah* — the corrections are **one word each**. **He quotes accurately and sources loosely**, and those are two different faults with two different remedies.
- ⭐ **The largest single category in this audit is *accurately quoted, correctly attributed, and ungradeable* — nine of twenty-two.** That is not a failure of scholarship. It is what happens when a preacher works from a **topical anthology that carries no chains because its source has none to carry**. The right correction is not "stop using these." It is **"say what they are."**

## The honest limitations of this document

1. ⚠ **The transcript is YouTube ASR.** It has no reliable punctuation, it garbles Arabic terms and proper names badly ("Boronsi" for Borunsi, "qawamah" for *karamah*, "a clock" for *akhlaq* throughout the second half), and at [39:05–40:05] it is corrupt enough that some clauses are readable only by context. Every Arabic text in this document was re-derived from the sources, **not** from the ASR. Where the ASR is too corrupt to be sure of his meaning, the body of the document quotes it and flags it. **Timestamps are approximate to about a minute**, because the captions are chunked at roughly one-minute intervals.
2. ⚠ **Several archives were unreachable at the egress proxy, and this is the single biggest limitation on §17.** **english.khamenei.ir** and **leader.ir** return 403; **farsi.khamenei.ir** is robots-blocked; **defapress.ir**, **farhikhtegandaily.com** and **tasnimnews.com** were robots- or DNS-blocked; **alarab.co.uk** is 403-blocked. Consequently the two Khamenei attributions in this lecture — *"near-death experiences are real"* [42:05] and *"Kaveh was our student, he became our teacher"* [48:06] — together with the Q 64:6 tafsir attribution and the confirmation of the **Nasrallah** statement on sisters' photographs [34:01], are recorded as **unreachable archive, not as completed negative searches**. **A reader with an ordinary browser can very likely settle all four in a few minutes, and should.**
3. ⚠ **The English (WOFIS) *al-Mizan* covers only to roughly Surat al-Nisa'/al-Ma'ida.** **Every verse in this lecture lies beyond that**, so all *al-Mizan* material here was read in **Arabic**. Two verses (**13:22**, **28:54**) were **not** looked up in *al-Mizan* at all and are recorded as a gap; ***Majma' al-Bayan*** was retrieved for **64:6 only**. Nothing has been supplied to fill those gaps from general knowledge.
4. ⚠ **Five *Ghurar al-Hikam* entry numbers are pinned; four are not.** N4, N5, N11, N12 and N18 are recorded as "wording stable, entry unpinned" rather than being given a number I could not verify.
5. ⚠ **Gradings were retrieved where they exist and named where retrieved** — al-Majlisi in *Mir'at al-'Uqul* for al-Kafi 2/53/1; Ahmad, Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Hatim, al-Shafi'i, Ibn Hazm, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn al-Mulaqqin and al-Tirmidhi for N3; the dorar.net apparatus for the Sunni material. **No grade has been invented.** For the *Amali* al-Tusi chain of the Abu Dharr *wasiyyah*, **no rijal verdict was retrieved**, and that is stated rather than guessed.
6. ⚠ **Sections 8 through 16 are applications, not findings.** Every activity, table, prompt and reflection question in them is mine unless a timestamp appears, and they are built only on material that survived §§5–7. **Nothing in this document that failed verification is used anywhere downstream of §7** — the memorisation sheet at §14 in particular contains only verified items, and the three retracted narrations appear nowhere in it.
7. ⚠ **The psychological literature cited is summarised, not reviewed.** Przybylski & Nash 2018, Collier et al. 2016, Rupp & Wallen 2008 and Hagger et al. 2016 are reported with their designs and effect sizes so a reader can weigh them; none was read as a full methodological appraisal, and none of them is about Muslim families in particular.
8. ⚠ **Finally, and it should be said plainly: this document is a check on citations, not a judgement of a man.** The pattern it found is the same one the whole series shows — **what he learned from books is good; what reached him through preaching is where the failures cluster.** The three unlocated items here are a story about captives, a story about a *kha'in*'s four signs, and a homiletic thought-experiment. The verified items are a verse count, a page number in *Tuhaf al-'Uqul*, a chapter of Nahj al-Balagha, a chapter of al-Kafi and two *sahih* hadith. **That is the ordinary condition of preaching from memory — and it is precisely why the checking step earns its keep.**
